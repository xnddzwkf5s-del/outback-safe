# Queensland Town Water Sources

> **SHTF Reference** — Nearest water source (river, creek, lake, bore) for every major Queensland town. Includes coordinates for navigation. Distances are approximate straight-line from town centre. "Nearest" means nearest reliable surface water or known permanent water source.

---

## South East Queensland

### Brisbane
- **Nearest water**: Brisbane River — runs through the city centre. Multiple access points at South Bank, Kangaroo Point, New Farm, West End, and riverfront parks.
- **Coordinates**: -27.4698, 153.0251 (city centre, river adjacent north)
- **Alternative**: Enoggera Reservoir (-27.48, 152.93), Gold Creek Dam (-27.46, 152.88), Lake Manchester (-27.49, 152.75) — all within 15-20 km west. Mount Crosby Weir (-27.54, 152.80) is the primary drinking water abstraction point ~20 km south-west.
- **Note**: Tidal river. Salinity varies. Dozens of creeks feed into the river: Enoggera Creek, Ithaca Creek, Moggill Creek, Oxley Creek, Norman Creek, Bulimba Creek. All flow year-round but urban runoff quality is poor. Treat thoroughly.

### Gold Coast
- **Nearest water**: Pacific Ocean (eastern beaches). Nerang River runs through the city.
- **Coordinates**: -28.0167, 153.4000 (Surfers Paradise — ocean 200 m east)
- **Alternative**: Hinze Dam / Advancetown Lake (-28.05, 153.28) — primary water supply ~15 km inland. Coomera River, Tallebudgera Creek, Currumbin Creek — all perennial, flowing east off the hinterland.
- **Note**: Nerang River is tidal in lower reaches. Hinze Dam stores ~310,000 ML. Gold Coast's water supply is robust. Numerous freshwater creeks in the hinterland (Springbrook, Lamington).

### Sunshine Coast (Maroochydore)
- **Nearest water**: Maroochy River — mouth at Cotton Tree, runs through the town centre.
- **Coordinates**: -26.6550, 153.0930 (Maroochydore CBD — river ~200 m)
- **Alternative**: Pacific Ocean beaches. Mooloolah River (-26.68, 153.12), Ewen Maddock Dam (-26.74, 152.98), Baroon Pocket Dam (-26.69, 152.88). Pumicestone Passage to south.
- **Note**: Maroochy River is tidal. Several freshwater tributaries: Paynter Creek, Eudlo Creek, Petrie Creek. Hinterland streams (Mary River tributaries) are perennial.

### Toowoomba
- **Nearest water**: Condamine River headwaters are east of Toowoomba near Killarney, but no major river in town. Toowoomba sits on the Great Dividing Range.
- **Coordinates**: -27.5667, 151.9500
- **Alternative**: Cooby Dam (-27.40, 151.94) ~20 km north, Perseverance Dam (-27.32, 152.09) ~25 km north-east, Cressbrook Dam (-27.27, 152.20) ~30 km north-east — the city's three water storage dams. Gowrie Creek (runs through town, ephemeral). Numerous farm dams on the Darling Downs. Bores accessing Great Artesian Basin available on properties north and west of town.
- **Note**: Toowoomba is a critical water location — it's on top of the range with limited surface water. Primary water comes from three dams in the upper Brisbane River catchment via pipeline. In SHTF, these dams are the go-to water points.

### Ipswich
- **Nearest water**: Bremer River — runs through the city centre, joins Brisbane River at Moggill. Brisbane River flows along northern boundary.
- **Coordinates**: -27.6167, 152.7667 (city centre — Bremer River ~100 m)
- **Alternative**: Brisbane River ~5 km north. Colleges Crossing (-27.57, 152.81) on Brisbane River. Bundamba Creek, Woogaroo Creek. Lake Marburg (-27.60, 152.58) ~20 km west. Mount Crosby Weir ~10 km north-east.
- **Note**: Bremer River is a significant Brisbane River tributary. Perennial but can stagnate in dry periods. Treat before drinking.

### Logan City
- **Nearest water**: Logan River — runs through the city from west to east, entering Moreton Bay at Redland Bay area.
- **Coordinates**: -27.6500, 153.1000 (Logan Central — Logan River ~2 km east)
- **Alternative**: Albert River (parallel to Logan, to east). Slacks Creek, Scrubby Creek, Dam across various suburbs. Leslie Harrison Dam (-27.55, 153.19) to north-east ~15 km.
- **Note**: Both Logan and Albert Rivers are perennial. Tidal in lower reaches. Affected by urban runoff. Treat thoroughly.

## Darling Downs & Granite Belt

### Warwick
- **Nearest water**: Condamine River — runs through the town.
- **Coordinates**: -28.2167, 152.0333
- **Alternative**: Leslie Dam (-28.22, 151.92) ~10 km west — major water storage. In 2019/2020 drought, the Condamine dried to pools. Leslie Dam is the primary reliable water source. Rosenthal Creek, Canal Creek, and various farm dams.
- **Note**: Headwaters of the Condamine River (which becomes the Balonne, then the Darling). Location matters: even small creeks here are part of the Murray-Darling system headwaters.

### Dalby
- **Nearest water**: Myall Creek — runs through town, tributary of the Condamine.
- **Coordinates**: -27.1833, 151.2667
- **Alternative**: Condamine River ~10 km west. Bores accessing Great Artesian Basin throughout. Lake Broadwater (-27.34, 151.11) ~30 km south — a natural freshwater lake (ephemeral in drought). Numerous bores and stock dams in the area.
- **Note**: Myall Creek can dry in drought. Bores are the reliable fallback.

### Goondiwindi
- **Nearest water**: Macintyre River — runs along the southern edge of town, forms the Qld/NSW border.
- **Coordinates**: -28.5500, 150.3167
- **Alternative**: Bores throughout the district. Callandoon weir pool on the Macintyre provides permanent water. Dumaresq River to east, Weir River to west.
- **Note**: Macintyre is a perennial river in this section (regulated flow from upstream dams in NSW — Pindari, Glenlyon). Becomes the Barwon River downstream when it meets the Weir River. Critical water source for cotton and grain irrigation.

### Stanthorpe
- **Nearest water**: Quart Pot Creek — runs through the town centre.
- **Coordinates**: -28.6500, 151.9333
- **Alternative**: Storm King Dam (-28.73, 151.99) ~10 km south-east — town water supply. Severn River to the east and west (two branches). Farm dams on granite country (good catchment). Multiple creeks in the Granite Belt.
- **Note**: Stanthorpe ran out of water in 2019/2020 drought — water had to be trucked in. Storm King Dam is small and vulnerable. This area can be a problem zone in SHTF.

### Kingaroy
- **Nearest water**: Stuart River headwaters near town; Barker Creek through town.
- **Coordinates**: -26.5333, 151.8333
- **Alternative**: Boondooma Dam (-26.13, 151.43) ~50 km west on the Boyne River. Bjelke-Petersen Dam (-26.30, 151.69) ~30 km north-west. Gordonbrook Dam (-26.28, 151.84) ~20 km north. Bores accessing Great Artesian Basin available.
- **Note**: Kingaroy is in the Burnett catchment. Surface water is limited in the South Burnett — dams are the primary water supply. Peanut and cropping country with good artesian bore access.

## Central Queensland

### Rockhampton
- **Nearest water**: Fitzroy River — runs through the city, the Fitzroy Barrage is in the city centre.
- **Coordinates**: -23.3783, 150.5136
- **Alternative**: The city sits at the tidal limit. Fitzroy Barrage creates a permanent freshwater pool upstream. Back-up bores available. Gracemere Lakes (-23.46, 150.45) ~10 km south-west. Numerous waterholes in dry riverbeds.
- **Note**: One of the best-watered major towns in Queensland — the Fitzroy catchment is enormous. The barrage provides a reliable freshwater pool even in drought. However, quality can be poor after floods due to upstream mining and agricultural sediment.

### Gladstone
- **Nearest water**: Calliope River — ~10 km south of city centre. Boyne River ~25 km south.
- **Coordinates**: -23.8422, 151.2586
- **Alternative**: Awoonga Dam (-24.07, 151.31) on the Boyne River ~25 km south-west — the city's primary water supply, stores ~777,000 ML. Targinie Creek. Lake Callemondah is tidal/industrial (Port of Gladstone).
- **Note**: Awoonga Dam is a huge, reliable water source. Gladstone is well-supplied. Multiple industrial water sources associated with the port and alumina/power facilities but these may be contaminated.

### Bundaberg
- **Nearest water**: Burnett River — runs through the city centre.
- **Coordinates**: -24.8600, 152.3500
- **Alternative**: Kolan River ~20 km north. Pacific Ocean (Bargara/Moore Park Beaches). Bores accessing coastal aquifers. Multiple agricultural dams. Fred Haigh Dam (Lake Monduran) ~60 km north-west (-24.83, 151.87) on the Kolan. Paradise Dam on Burnett ~60 km upstream (-25.35, 151.92) — town water supply, but partially drained.
- **Note**: The Burnett River is perennial through Bundaberg. Tidal influence extends approximately 40 km upstream from the mouth at Burnett Heads. Good water year-round but treat for agricultural runoff (sugar cane, macadamias).

### Maryborough
- **Nearest water**: Mary River — runs through the town.
- **Coordinates**: -25.5375, 152.7019
- **Alternative**: Tinana Creek (parallels the Mary). Lenthall's Dam / Lake Lenthall (-25.40, 152.53). Tidal reach of the Mary at the Great Sandy Strait (River Heads, ~15 km downstream). Bores available.
- **Note**: The Mary River at Maryborough is the uppermost limit of tidal influence. Fresh water available above the weir in town. Home to the endangered Mary River cod and Mary River turtle — a pristine water system by SEQ standards.

### Hervey Bay
- **Nearest water**: No major river in town. Pacific Ocean beaches. Mary River ~20 km south-west at River Heads.
- **Coordinates**: -25.2900, 152.8300
- **Alternative**: Lake Lenthall (-25.40, 152.53) ~20 km south — town water supply. Burrum River ~15 km north. Mary River at River Heads. Bores in coastal sand aquifer. Artificial lakes/canals in residential areas (treated water, likely brackish).
- **Note**: Hervey Bay has no natural freshwater river. Primary water is piped from Lake Lenthall. Bores in the coastal sand dune aquifer provide groundwater but may be saline. Important to know that you must travel at least 15 km for freshwater.

### Emerald
- **Nearest water**: Nogoa River — runs along the southern edge of town.
- **Coordinates**: -23.5167, 148.1667
- **Alternative**: Fairbairn Dam / Lake Maraboon (-23.66, 148.09) ~20 km south — massive water storage (1,300,000 ML). Theresa Creek Dam (-22.91, 148.13) ~70 km north-east on Theresa Creek. Selma Weir on the Nogoa (south of Emerald).
- **Note**: Fairbairn Dam is one of Queensland's largest water storages and provides permanent water security for Emerald and the gemfields. Lake Maraboon is also a significant recreational fishery (redclaw, barramundi stocked).

### Moranbah
- **Nearest water**: Isaac River — ~15 km east of town. No permanent river through town.
- **Coordinates**: -22.0000, 148.0500
- **Alternative**: Eungella Dam (-21.14, 148.39) ~130 km north-east (on the Broken River, part of the Burdekin system). Grosvenor Downs Water Supply Scheme on the Isaac River. Kinchant Dam (-21.23, 148.96) ~100 km east. Bores and mine water (coal seam gas water — treat thoroughly) throughout the Bowen Basin.
- **Note**: Moranbah is in coal mining country. Mine-affected water is available but requires significant treatment for heavy metals, salts, and hydrocarbons. The area is semi-arid with limited natural surface water. Bores are the most reliable non-mine water source.

### Yeppoon
- **Nearest water**: Pacific Ocean. Water Park Creek ~15 km north.
- **Coordinates**: -23.1333, 150.7333
- **Alternative**: Water Park Creek supplies Capricorn Coast water (via Woodbury treatment). Iwasaki (Capricorn) Lakes ~5 km inland. Byfield creeks and national park streams ~30-40 km north. Bores in coastal aquifers.
- **Note**: Yeppoon faces water shortages during drought — relies heavily on Water Park Creek, which can run low. Desalination or water carting has been needed in recent droughts. Byfield's perennial rainforest creeks are the best SHTF source (Sandy Creek, Waterpark Creek upper reaches).

## North Queensland

### Mackay
- **Nearest water**: Pioneer River — runs through the city centre, mouth at Mackay Harbour.
- **Coordinates**: -21.1400, 149.1900
- **Alternative**: Teemburra Dam (-21.20, 148.68) ~50 km west on Teemburra Creek. Kinchant Dam (-21.23, 148.96) ~30 km south-west. Eungella Dam (-21.14, 148.39) ~80 km west on the Broken River. Bakers Creek, Sandy Creek. Numerous freshwater lagoons in the area.
- **Note**: Mackay is well-watered — the Pioneer Valley has high rainfall and the Pioneer River flows year-round. Multiple dams provide security. Sugar cane country — treat agricultural runoff.

### Bowen
- **Nearest water**: Don River — runs through the west side of town. Pacific Ocean / Edgecumbe Bay to the east.
- **Coordinates**: -20.0139, 148.2458
- **Alternative**: Bowen River (tributary of the Burdekin) ~40 km south-west. Euri Creek. Peter Faust Dam (-20.37, 148.38) ~50 km south-west on the Proserpine River. Bores and coastal aquifers.
- **Note**: The Don River is intermittent. The Burdekin Delta aquifer is a major groundwater system. Bowen is famous for mangoes — irrigation bores are common.

### Ayr / Home Hill
- **Nearest water**: Burdekin River — flows between Ayr and Home Hill (Ayr on east bank, Home Hill on west bank).
- **Coordinates**: -19.5833, 147.4000 (Burdekin River bridge between towns)
- **Alternative**: Burdekin Delta aquifer — one of Australia's richest groundwater systems, extensive bore network. Plantation Creek, Kalamia Creek, Barratta Creek, and extensive irrigation channel network. The aquifer is recharged by the Burdekin River and provides water across the delta.
- **Note**: Among the best-watered agricultural areas in Queensland. The Burdekin River at this point is tidal near the mouth but fresh above the delta irrigation weirs. Groundwater bores everywhere — the aquifer is an enormous underground water resource.

### Townsville
- **Nearest water**: Ross River — runs through the city from Ross River Dam to Cleveland Bay.
- **Coordinates**: -19.2590, 146.8169
- **Alternative**: Ross River Dam (-19.42, 146.75) ~25 km south-west — city's primary water storage (~215,000 ML). Burdekin Falls Dam pipeline (~100 km south) supplies water in drought. Paluma Dam (-18.96, 146.16) on Mount Spec ~65 km north-west. Alligator Creek, Bohle River.
- **Note**: The Ross River is perennial through Townsville but quality is poor (urban runoff). Ross River Dam is the primary potable source. The Burdekin pipeline (2007) provides drought security from Burdekin Falls Dam. Townsville is a major garrison city — water security is well-planned.

### Charters Towers
- **Nearest water**: Burdekin River — ~8 km west of town (Macrossan Bridge). No major river through the town centre.
- **Coordinates**: -20.0833, 146.2667
- **Alternative**: Burdekin River at Macrossan is the main water source. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin — Dalrymple bore fields supply town water. Millchester Creek, Gladstone Creek through town (ephemeral). Multiple mining dams and bores in the district.
- **Note**: Charters Towers relies primarily on the Burdekin River and artesian bores. The river is highly seasonal — can be dry at Macrossan in winter. Bores are the reliable year-round source. Alluvial aquifers along the Burdekin also provide groundwater.

### Ingham
- **Nearest water**: Herbert River — runs through the town.
- **Coordinates**: -18.6500, 146.1667
- **Alternative**: Palm Creek, Cattle Creek — both tributaries of the Herbert. Hinchinbrook Channel ~20 km east. Numerous freshwater lagoons and wetlands in the lower Herbert floodplain.
- **Note**: The Herbert River flows year-round with high rainfall in the headwaters. Excellent freshwater source but treat for agricultural runoff (sugar cane). Hinchinbrook Channel is tidal/estuarine (brackish to saline). Crocodile territory — extreme caution near water.

### Innisfail
- **Nearest water**: Johnstone River — North and South Johnstone branches converge at Innisfail.
- **Coordinates**: -17.5225, 146.0308
- **Alternative**: Mourilyan Harbour (estuarine) ~10 km south. Ninds Creek, Jubilee Creek. Extensive tropical streams and rainforest creeks in the Johnstone catchment (all within 10 km of town).
- **Note**: Innisfail is in one of the wettest parts of Australia. Water is abundant year-round from the Johnstone River. Extreme flooding risk — major floods in 1913, 1918, 1986, and 2014. Crocodiles present.

## Far North Queensland

### Cairns
- **Nearest water**: Pacific Ocean (Trinity Inlet / Cairns Esplanade). Barron River ~5 km north of city centre.
- **Coordinates**: -16.9186, 145.7781
- **Alternative**: Barron River and Barron Gorge. Freshwater Creek ~10 km north. Mulgrave River ~30 km south. Lake Morris / Copperlode Dam (-16.98, 145.69) ~10 km west — Cairns' water supply. Crystal Cascades, Stoney Creek — popular freshwater swimming spots. Mossman River ~80 km north.
- **Note**: Cairns has abundant water from the Barron River catchment. Copperlode Dam supplies town water. Trinity Inlet is estuarine (mangroves, crocs). Freshwater is found in Barron River and hinterland creeks. Crocodiles in all waterways — treat all inland water bodies as croc territory.

### Cooktown
- **Nearest water**: Endeavour River — runs through town, mouth at Cooktown harbour.
- **Coordinates**: -15.4667, 145.2500
- **Alternative**: Annan River ~10 km south. Bloomfield River ~50 km south. Numerous tropical rainforest creeks (Trevethan Creek, Little Annan). Pacific Ocean (Endeavour Reef).
- **Note**: Endeavour River is perennial and freshwater above tidal reach. Named by Captain Cook after his ship Endeavour was beached here for repairs in 1770. McIvor River to the north. Croc-heavy — extreme caution. Good fishing (barramundi, mangrove jack).

### Weipa
- **Nearest water**: Gulf of Carpentaria. Embley River and Mission River run around the town.
- **Coordinates**: -12.6333, 141.8833
- **Alternative**: Embley River (western side), Mission River (eastern side), Hey River (further east).
- **Note**: Weipa is a mining town on the west coast of Cape York. The Embley and Mission Rivers are tidal and estuarine — freshwater is limited to upstream reaches and seasonal creeks. Crocodiles and box jellyfish/stingers in all marine waters. Town water supplied by Lake Patricia (artificial lake, rainfall-fed). Bores also available.

### Thursday Island
- **Nearest water**: No natural rivers or lakes — Thursday Island is a small granitic island in the Torres Strait. Pacific Ocean surrounds the island.
- **Coordinates**: -10.5833, 142.2167
- **Alternative**: Seawater only. Town water supplied by desalination plant and rainwater tanks. No natural surface freshwater on the island. Prince of Wales Island (larger island to the south) has small seasonal creeks but no permanent rivers.
- **Note**: Thursday Island is one of the most water-vulnerable locations you can be in SHTF. The desalination plant is the only reliable water source. Rainwater collection is essential. Do not rely on finding surface freshwater on TI.

### Bamaga
- **Nearest water**: Jardine River ~40 km south. Pacific Ocean (Torres Strait) to the north.
- **Coordinates**: -10.8833, 142.3833
- **Alternative**: Jardine River — the largest perennial river in Queensland (by discharge), perennial year-round despite monsoonal pattern. Loyalty Beach and Punsand Bay to the north. Fruit Bat Falls, Eliot Falls, Twin Falls on the Jardine — excellent freshwater swimming (crocodile-free in upper reaches due to waterfalls as barriers).
- **Note**: Bamaga is at the tip of Cape York. The Jardine River is the primary freshwater source for the Northern Peninsula Area. Fruit Bat Falls and Eliot Falls are rare croc-free swimming spots in FNQ due to natural barriers. Seisia and Umagico nearby rely on the same sources.

## Gulf of Carpentaria

### Normanton
- **Nearest water**: Norman River — runs through town on the eastern side.
- **Coordinates**: -17.6667, 141.0833
- **Alternative**: Bynoe River, Flinders River mouth ~50 km east. Gilbert River ~150 km east.
- **Note**: The Norman River is the town's water source — flows into the Gulf of Carpentaria. Seasonal — floods during the monsoon. The Norman River is famous for barramundi fishing. Crocodiles abundant. In the dry season, the river level drops dramatically but maintains pools for months. Bores available.

### Burketown
- **Nearest water**: Albert River — runs along the north side of town, entering the Gulf.
- **Coordinates**: -17.7333, 139.5500
- **Alternative**: Leichhardt River ~60 km west. Nicholson River ~30 km south. Gregory River ~100 km south-west.
- **Note**: The Albert River flows past Burketown into the Gulf. Seasonal, but permanent waterholes persist. The Gulf rivers are croc territory without exception. Barramundi capital — enormous tidal fishing. Burketown was nearly destroyed by a cyclone/storm surge in 1899 (a "morning glory" cloud phenomenon is famous here).

### Karumba
- **Nearest water**: Norman River — mouth at Karumba, entering Gulf of Carpentaria. Gulf of Carpentaria itself.
- **Coordinates**: -17.4833, 140.8333
- **Alternative**: Karumba is at the mouth of the Norman River. The river mouth and estuary are tidal/saltwater. Freshwater requires travel upstream (toward Normanton, 50+ km). Bores available.
- **Note**: Karumba is a fishing port. The Norman River at Karumba is estuarine — saline. Freshwater must be sourced upstream or from bores. Crocodiles and marine stingers in all water. Excellent barramundi and prawn fishing.

## Western / Outback Queensland

### Mount Isa
- **Nearest water**: Leichhardt River — runs through town (north-south). Lake Moondarra — ~15 km north of town on the Leichhardt River.
- **Coordinates**: -20.7333, 139.5000
- **Alternative**: Lake Moondarra (-20.58, 139.55) — the city's primary water supply, stores ~107,000 ML. Rifle Creek Dam (-20.68, 139.48). Lake Julius (-20.21, 139.87) ~70 km north-east — back-up supply. Corella Dam (-20.62, 139.92) ~50 km east.
- **Note**: Mount Isa is heavily reliant on Lake Moondarra and the Leichhardt River system. Lake Moondarra is a recreational fishing destination (barramundi stocked). Mining contamination is a concern — Leichhardt River carries elevated metal levels from Mount Isa Mines. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin are also available but mineralised.

### Cloncurry
- **Nearest water**: Cloncurry River — runs through town, tributary of the Flinders River.
- **Coordinates**: -20.7000, 140.5000
- **Alternative**: Corella Dam (-20.62, 139.92) ~20 km west, Chinaman Creek Dam (-20.62, 140.33) ~15 km south — town water supply. Flinders River ~50 km north-east.
- **Note**: Chinaman Creek Dam is Cloncurry's primary water storage. The Cloncurry River is the major tributary of the Flinders system. Recorded Queensland's hottest temperature: 53.1°C (in shade) in 1889. Waterholes in the Cloncurry River persist through dry season. Bores available.

### Longreach
- **Nearest water**: Thomson River — ~3 km west of town. Longreach Waterhole is a permanent waterhole on the Thomson.
- **Coordinates**: -23.4500, 144.2500
- **Alternative**: Longreach Waterhole (permanent). Landsborough Creek (tributary of Thomson). Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin — artesian bore water supplies the town. The Stockman's Hall of Fame overlooks the Thomson River.
- **Note**: The Thomson River is ephemeral but the Longreach Waterhole is permanent — artesian-fed and holds water year-round. Bores are the reliable primary water source. The Thomson River can flood dramatically in wet years (2010 floods covered huge areas).

### Barcaldine
- **Nearest water**: Alice River (a different Alice from the one in the Cooper system) — ~2 km west of town.
- **Coordinates**: -23.5500, 145.2833
- **Alternative**: The Alice River at Barcaldine is a Thomson River tributary. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin — the town is named after the artesian bore that made settlement possible. The Tree of Knowledge (famous ghost gum marking birthplace of the ALP) stood near the bore.
- **Note**: Barcaldine exists because of artesian water. The bore is the primary water source — the Alice River is ephemeral. The Great Artesian Basin provides reliable water across the entire region.

### Blackall
- **Nearest water**: Barcoo River — ~5 km south of town.
- **Coordinates**: -24.4167, 145.4667
- **Alternative**: Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin — town water supply. The Barcoo River can flow strongly in wet years but dries to waterholes in the dry. Permanent waterholes exist.
- **Note**: Blackall sits near the Barcoo River headwaters. Famous for its artesian bore baths (Blackall Aquatic Centre uses geothermal bore water at ~47°C). The "Black Stump" — a survey marker that became shorthand for the outback — originates here.

### Winton
- **Nearest water**: Western River — runs through town, tributary of the Diamantina River.
- **Coordinates**: -22.3833, 143.0333
- **Alternative**: Diamantina River ~30 km south. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin. Several permanent waterholes on local watercourses.
- **Note**: Winton sits on the Western River. The Diamantina is the major watercourse to the south. Artesian bore water is the primary supply. Winton is dinosaur fossil country (Australian Age of Dinosaurs museum nearby). The Waltzing Matilda Centre commemorates Banjo Paterson's famous song — the "billabong" referenced is thought to be Combo Waterhole on the Diamantina.

### Roma
- **Nearest water**: Bungil Creek — runs through town, tributary of the Condamine/Balonne.
- **Coordinates**: -26.5833, 148.7833
- **Alternative**: The Great Artesian Basin bores throughout the district. Roma was the site of Australia's first natural gas discovery (1900) and first town to have natural gas street lights. Multiple bores and agricultural dams.
- **Note**: Roma sits on Bungil Creek (part of the Murray-Darling system via the Condamine-Balonne). The GAB provides reliable bore water. Roma is the heart of the Maranoa — good water with bores and seasonal creek flows.

### Charleville
- **Nearest water**: Warrego River — runs along the southern edge of town.
- **Coordinates**: -26.4000, 146.2500
- **Alternative**: Bores (town water supplied by local bores). Langlo River (tributary of the Warrego) ~50 km north-west. The Warrego River is part of the Murray-Darling Basin, joining the Darling River at Bourke. Several waterholes on the Warrego near town.
- **Note**: Charleville depends on artesian bores and Warrego River waterholes. The Warrego flows seasonally but permanent waterholes persist. Charleville was the site of an early rain-making experiment (Stiger vortex cannons fired into clouds in 1902 — no success). The "Bilby Experience" is here.

### Cunnamulla
- **Nearest water**: Warrego River — runs through town.
- **Coordinates**: -28.0667, 145.6833
- **Alternative**: Paroo River ~60 km west. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin. The Cunnamulla weir on the Warrego provides a permanent pool.
- **Note**: The Warrego River at Cunnamulla is the town's lifeline. The weir pool is permanent. Paroo River to the west is the last unregulated river in the Murray-Darling system. Artesian bores are the reliable water source.

### Thargomindah
- **Nearest water**: Bulloo River — runs through town.
- **Coordinates**: -27.9833, 143.8167
- **Alternative**: Bores — Thargomindah was the third town in the world with hydroelectric street lighting (after Paris and London), powered by artesian bore water pressure in 1893. The bore still operates. The Bulloo is ephemeral but permanent waterholes exist. Lake Bindegolly ~40 km east is a national park with wetlands.
- **Note**: The Bulloo River is the primary surface water. Artesian bores are the reliable source. The Bulloo system is endorheic — terminal lakes. The Bulloo Lakes are important waterbird breeding habitat when filled.

### Birdsville
- **Nearest water**: Diamantina River — ~1 km west of town.
- **Coordinates**: -25.9000, 139.3500
- **Alternative**: The Diamantina at Birdsville is usually just a series of waterholes (known as the Birdsville Billabong, or specific named waterholes like Pelican Waterhole). Bores — the Birdsville Hotel and town rely on artesian bore water. The Birdsville geothermal bore provides hot water (the Birdsville bore baths).
- **Note**: Birdsville is isolated. The Diamantina rarely flows past here — it's typically a chain of waterholes. The bore is essential. If the bore fails, water must be trucked from hundreds of km away. In flood, the Diamantina can isolate the town for weeks or months (the famous "Birdsville Track" becomes impassable).

## Notable Border Locations

### Innamincka (SA, but on Cooper Creek north-east of the Strzelecki)
- **Nearest water**: Cooper Creek — the town sits directly on Cooper Creek.
- **Coordinates**: -27.7000, 140.7333
- **Alternative**: Permanent waterholes on Cooper Creek upstream and downstream. The Cullyamurra Waterhole on Cooper Creek (upstream of town) is reputed to be the deepest waterhole in South Australia. Bores available.
- **Note**: Innamincka is on Cooper Creek. It's included here because it's a critical water point for anyone crossing the Qld/SA border — the Cooper Creek system is one of the few reliable water sources in the Strzelecki/Sturt Stony Deserts. Burke and Wills died at the Dig Tree on Cooper Creek ~50 km downstream.

### Bourke (NSW, included for cross-border reference)
- **Nearest water**: Darling River — runs through town.
- **Coordinates**: -30.0833, 145.9333
- **Alternative**: The Darling River at Bourke is regulated (Menindee Lakes system upstream). Permanent weir pool. Bores accessing the Great Artesian Basin throughout. The Darling is the primary water source.

## Key SHTF Water Strategy

### If you have a vehicle and fuel:
1. **SEQ**: Brisbane River, Wivenhoe Dam, Hinze Dam, Baroon Pocket — perennial sources within 30 min drive of any SEQ town.
2. **Darling Downs**: Leslie Dam (Warwick), Cooby/Cressbrook/Perseverance Dams (Toowoomba), Myall Creek/bores (Dalby). Bores are the most reliable in this region.
3. **Central Qld**: Fairbairn Dam (Emerald), Fitzroy Barrage (Rockhampton), Awoonga Dam (Gladstone), Burnett River (Bundaberg) — all reliable perennial or massive storage.
4. **North Qld**: Pioneer River (Mackay), Ross River Dam (Townsville), Burdekin Falls Dam (Townsville via pipeline — dam is at -20.64, 147.14), Herbert River (Ingham), Johnstone River (Innisfail) — all perennial.
5. **FNQ**: Barron River/Lake Morris (Cairns), Daintree River, Endeavour River (Cooktown), Jardine River (Bamaga) — abundant water year-round except TI.
6. **Gulf**: Rely on permanent waterholes and bores. Crocs in ALL Gulf water. The Norman (Normanton/Karumba), Albert (Burketown), Leichhardt (Mt Isa) — all have permanent pools or dams.
7. **Channel Country**: Bores or permanent waterholes are essential. Do NOT rely on river flow. Know waterhole locations before traveling (e.g., Longreach Waterhole on Thomson, Cullyamurra on Cooper, Birdsville Billabong on Diamantina).

### If you're on foot:
- Town proximity matters most. Every town in this guide has a mapped water source within walking distance OR reliable bores.
- In the Channel Country and western Qld, waterholes are widely spaced — walking may not be feasible between sources. Plan accordingly.
- **Always assume crocodiles** in any water body north of Gladstone, including inland rivers.
- **Always treat** water from any source — boiling is most reliable when chemicals and filters are unavailable.

### Water Procurement Priorities (best to worst):
1. Flowing water from perennial rivers (upper reaches — less contamination)
2. Dam/lake water (stored water — treat for algae, bacteria)
3. Artesian bore water (may be mineralised but potable; may need cooling)
4. Permanent waterholes in ephemeral rivers (treat — may be stagnant)
5. Rainwater tanks (if accessible and maintained)
6. Desalinated seawater (energy-intensive, equipment dependent)
7. Agricultural/irrigation water (high risk — pesticides, fertilisers, bacteria)
