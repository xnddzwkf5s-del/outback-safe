# Western Australia — Major River Systems

> **Important:** Most WA rivers are **ephemeral** — they flow only after significant rainfall and are dry for much of the year. Only rivers in the far south-west and parts of the Kimberley are reliably perennial. In an SHTF scenario, never assume a WA river is flowing without local confirmation.

---

## Perth Region

### Swan River (Derbarl Yerrigan)
- **Length:** ~72 km
- **Source:** Confluence of the Avon River and Wooroloo Brook, near Walyunga National Park
- **Mouth:** Fremantle Harbour — Indian Ocean
- **Key towns:** Perth, Fremantle, Guildford, Maylands, Bayswater
- **Perennial?** Yes — tidal estuary from Fremantle to Maylands; fresh upstream
- **Features:** The defining waterway of Perth. Broad tidal estuary through the city, narrowing to a freshwater river upstream. Canning River joins at Melville Water. Prone to algal blooms in summer. Significant Noongar cultural site — the Dreamtime Waugal created the river. Swan Valley wine region in the upper reaches. Saline wedge extends inland ~50 km during summer low-flow.

### Canning River (Djarlgarro)
- **Length:** ~110 km
- **Source:** Darling Scarp near Wungong
- **Mouth:** Swan River at Melville Water (Applecross/Canning Bridge)
- **Key towns:** Armadale, Gosnells, Cannington
- **Perennial?** Mostly — dam-regulated upstream
- **Features:** Major tributary of the Swan. Canning Dam and multiple reservoirs regulate flow. Southern River and Wungong Brook are key tributaries. Canning River Regional Park protects riparian bushland. Saline in dry season below Kent Street Weir; fresh above.

### Helena River
- **Length:** ~70 km
- **Source:** Darling Scarp near Mount Helena
- **Mouth:** Swan River at Guildford
- **Key towns:** Mundaring
- **Perennial?** Regulated — Mundaring Weir
- **Features:** Dammed by Mundaring Weir (1903), which supplies the Goldfields Water Supply Scheme (the pipeline to Kalgoorlie — one of the world's longest freshwater pipelines). Below the weir, flow is seasonal. Pipeline extends ~560 km to Kalgoorlie.

### Murray River (WA)
- **Length:** ~134 km
- **Source:** Darling Scarp near Dwellingup
- **Mouth:** Peel Inlet / Indian Ocean at Mandurah
- **Key towns:** Dwellingup, Pinjarra, Mandurah
- **Perennial?** Mostly — regulated
- **Features:** Not to be confused with the Murray in eastern Australia. Dammed by multiple reservoirs in its upper catchment. Serpentine River joins near the mouth at Peel-Harvey Estuary. Important for irrigation in the Peel region. Pinjarra Massacre (1834) site. Banksia and jarrah forests in upper catchment.

### Avon River
- **Length:** ~240 km
- **Source:** Near Yealering, Wheatbelt
- **Mouth:** Becomes the Swan River at confluence near Walyunga NP
- **Key towns:** Northam, York, Toodyay, Beverley
- **Perennial?** Seasonal/ephemeral — flows strongly in winter, reduces to pools in summer
- **Features:** Technically the upper section of the Swan-Avon system. Historic towns along the banks — Northam, York, Toodyay. Ballardong Noongar country. Prone to flooding in winter. Avon Valley National Park is a key conservation area. Whitewater in winter months.

## South-West Region

### Blackwood River
- **Length:** ~300 km
- **Source:** Junction of Arthur River and Balgarup River near Quelarup
- **Mouth:** Hardy Inlet at Augusta — Southern Ocean
- **Key towns:** Bridgetown, Nannup, Augusta
- **Perennial?** Yes — one of WA's largest permanently flowing rivers
- **Features:** Longest river in the South-West. Flows through jarrah, marri, and karri forests. Blackwood River Valley is prime farming and viticulture country. Tidal influence extends inland ~40 km. Hardy Inlet is a large estuarine system. Canoeing and camping along much of its length.

### Warren River
- **Length:** ~140 km
- **Source:** Tone-Perup area, north of Manjimup
- **Mouth:** Southern Ocean near Northcliffe (D'Entrecasteaux National Park)
- **Key towns:** Pemberton (nearby), Northcliffe
- **Perennial?** Yes — high rainfall catchment
- **Features:** Flows through some of Australia's tallest forests (karri, tingle). Warren National Park. Excellent water quality. Important for marron and trout fishing. Remote mouth accessible by 4WD only.

### Donnelly River
- **Length:** ~150 km
- **Source:** Near Manjimup
- **Mouth:** Southern Ocean at Donnelly River mouth (D'Entrecasteaux National Park)
- **Key towns:** None directly; Manjimup ~20 km east
- **Perennial?** Yes — high rainfall zone
- **Features:** Flows through karri forests and coastal heath. The Donnelly River Mill (now tourist village) is a historic timber settlement. Remote beaches at the river mouth. One of the few WA rivers that maintains strong summer flow.

### Shannon River
- **Length:** ~65 km
- **Source:** Shannon National Park
- **Mouth:** Broke Inlet / Southern Ocean
- **Key towns:** None (remote)
- **Perennial?** Mostly — moderate rainfall catchment
- **Features:** Entirely within Shannon National Park and surrounding state forest. Broke Inlet is a large, intermittently-open coastal lagoon. Remote and largely inaccessible.

### Frankland River
- **Length:** ~150 km
- **Source:** West of Broomehill
- **Mouth:** Nornalup Inlet / Walpole Inlet — Southern Ocean
- **Key towns:** Frankland River (town), Walpole
- **Perennial?** Mostly — moderate rainfall
- **Features:** Joins the Deep River to form Nornalup Inlet at Walpole. Wine region named after the river. Walpole-Nornalup National Park. Rich biodiversity in the inlet system.

### Gordon River (WA)
- **Length:** ~120 km
- **Source:** North-west of Broomehill
- **Mouth:** Frankland River near Walpole
- **Key towns:** None (rural)
- **Perennial?** Seasonal/mostly
- **Features:** Not to be confused with Tasmania's Gordon River. Tributary of the Frankland River. Flows through wheatbelt-scarp transition zone. Low flow in summer.

## Kimberley Region

### Ord River (Kununurra)
- **Length:** ~650 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau, near Mount Wells
- **Mouth:** Cambridge Gulf / Timor Sea at Wyndham
- **Key towns:** Kununurra, Wyndham, Lake Argyle
- **Perennial?** Regulated — dam-controlled year-round flow
- **Features:** One of WA's most significant rivers. Lake Argyle (Ord River Dam) is Australia's largest freshwater reservoir by volume (10,760 GL — ~21x Sydney Harbour). Ord River Irrigation Area near Kununurra supports tropical agriculture (mangoes, melons, sandalwood). Upper reaches in Purnululu (Bungle Bungle) region. Massive seasonal flows during wet season (November-April). Hydroelectric power generation.

### Fitzroy River
- **Length:** ~733 km
- **Source:** King Leopold Ranges (Wunaamin Miliwundi Ranges)
- **Mouth:** King Sound at Derby — Indian Ocean
- **Key towns:** Fitzroy Crossing, Derby, Noonkanbah
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — dry season pools only; massive wet season flows
- **Features:** Largest WA river by catchment area (~93,000 km²). World's largest tidal range at King Sound (up to 11.8 m). Devastating floods in 2023 breached bridges at Fitzroy Crossing. Geikie Gorge (Danggu) National Park. Margaret River (Kimberley, not the SW one) is a major tributary. Critical to Bunuba, Gooniyandi, and Nyikina peoples.

### Lennard River
- **Length:** ~180 km
- **Source:** King Leopold Ranges
- **Mouth:** King Sound (north of Derby)
- **Key towns:** None (remote)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Windjana Gorge in Windjana Gorge National Park — impressive 100 m limestone walls. Tunnel Creek runs nearby. Important Bunuba country.

### Isdell River
- **Length:** ~200 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Walcott Inlet / Collier Bay — Indian Ocean
- **Key towns:** None (extremely remote)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Remote north Kimberley river. Walcott Inlet is a significant estuary. Supports barramundi. Accessible only by 4WD (Gibb River Road rough track) or boat.

### Charnley River
- **Length:** ~150 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau, near Charnley River Station
- **Mouth:** Walcott Inlet / Isdell River system
- **Key towns:** None (remote station country)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Charnley River-Artesian Range Wildlife Sanctuary (Australian Wildlife Conservancy). Remote. Pristine catchment with high conservation value.

### Durack River
- **Length:** ~300 km
- **Source:** Durack Range / Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Cambridge Gulf / Ord River system
- **Key towns:** None (stations only)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral with significant wet season flow
- **Features:** Named after the pioneering Durack family. Largely within pastoral station country. Gibb River Road crosses near El Questro.

### Pentecost River
- **Length:** ~150 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Cambridge Gulf (south arm near Wyndham)
- **Key towns:** None (El Questro Station nearby)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — dry season pools; wet season torrents
- **Features:** Famous river crossing on the Gibb River Road (iconic photo spot, crocodiles present). El Questro Wilderness Park. Spectacular gorge country. Saltwater crocodiles.

### King George River
- **Length:** ~80 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean (north Kimberley coast)
- **Key towns:** None (cruise access only)
- **Perennial?** Mostly — spring-fed base flow
- **Features:** Twin King George Falls — among Australia's highest waterfalls (~80 m drop into the river). Only accessible by boat (cruises from Kununurra or Broome). Remote, pristine. Important tourism site.

### Berkeley River
- **Length:** ~120 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Joseph Bonaparte Gulf — Timor Sea
- **Key towns:** None (remote)
- **Perennial?** Mostly — moderate base flow
- **Features:** Remote north Kimberley. Berkeley River Lodge (fly-in/fly-out). Spectacular gorges and waterfalls. Estuarine crocodile habitat.

### Forrest River
- **Length:** ~130 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau
- **Mouth:** Cambridge Gulf (west arm near Wyndham)
- **Key towns:** None (Former Forrest River Mission)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Site of the 1926 Forrest River Massacre (Oombulgurri). Aboriginal community history. Remote pastoral country.

### Drysdale River
- **Length:** ~430 km
- **Source:** Kimberley Plateau, near Doongan Station
- **Mouth:** Napier Broome Bay — Timor Sea
- **Key towns:** None (Drysdale River Station ~70 km from river)
- **Perennial?** Mostly — significant dry season pools
- **Features:** Entirely within Drysdale River National Park — one of WA's most remote and pristine national parks. No roads, no facilities. Access by air or foot only. High biodiversity. One of the last truly wild rivers in Australia.

## Pilbara Region

### Ashburton River
- **Length:** ~680 km
- **Source:** Ophthalmia Range (south of Newman)
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean near Onslow
- **Key towns:** Onslow, Nanutarra Roadhouse
- **Perennial?** Strongly ephemeral — flows only after cyclonic rain
- **Features:** Crosses the North West Coastal Highway at Nanutarra. Major floods during cyclone events. Important to Thalanyji and Baiyungu peoples. Winding desert course before coastal plain.

### Robe River
- **Length:** ~250 km
- **Source:** Hamersley Range
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean (south of Pannawonica)
- **Key towns:** Pannawonica
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Iron ore mining country. Rio Tinto operations at Pannawonica. Coarse sandy bed. Can flow strongly after cyclonic rain but typically dry.

### Fortescue River
- **Length:** ~760 km
- **Source:** Ophthalmia Range near Newman
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean at Mardie Station (east of Dampier)
- **Key towns:** Newman (upper catchment), Nullagine (tributary)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — long dry stretches; OPBG (Ophthalmia) Dam provides some permanent water
- **Features:** Major Pilbara river draining the Hamersley Range. Upper reaches include the Fortescue Marsh — a vast inland salt lake. Millstream-Chichester National Park (Fortescue Falls, permanent pools at Millstream — critically important permanent water in the Pilbara). Iron ore mining water demand has significantly impacted groundwater.

### Maitland River
- **Length:** ~170 km
- **Source:** Chichester Range
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean near Port Hedland (south-east of town)
- **Key towns:** None (mining infrastructure)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Drains the north Pilbara. Iron ore operations nearby. Seasonal flow only.

### Harding River
- **Length:** ~100 km
- **Source:** Chichester Range
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean near Roebourne / Port Walcott
- **Key towns:** Roebourne, Wickham
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Harding Dam (Lake Poongkaliyarra) supplies Karratha, Dampier, and the West Pilbara. Important water supply. Dam holds moderate permanent storage.

### Yule River
- **Length:** ~180 km
- **Source:** Chichester Range
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean south of Port Hedland
- **Key towns:** None (crosses Great Northern Highway)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Crosses the highway between Port Hedland and Karratha. Yule River bridge is an important infrastructure point. Dry for most of the year. Significant Aboriginal cultural sites (petroglyphs, grinding patches).

### De Grey River
- **Length:** ~280 km
- **Source:** Junction of Oakover and Strelley Rivers
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean north-east of Port Hedland
- **Key towns:** None (De Grey Station)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — can flow massively after cyclones
- **Features:** One of the major Pilbara drainage systems. The De Grey-Great Northern Highway crossing is a known flood hazard. De Grey Station is a historic pastoral property. Important to the Nyangumarta people.

### Oakover River
- **Length:** ~360 km
- **Source:** Ophthalmia Range / Rudall River area
- **Mouth:** Confluence with Strelley River to form De Grey River
- **Key towns:** None (remote)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Flows through the Great Sandy Desert fringe. Rudall River (Karlamilyi) National Park in upper catchment (WA's largest national park). Permanent pools exist in some sections. Extremely remote.

### Strelley River
- **Length:** ~220 km
- **Source:** Chichester Range
- **Mouth:** Confluence with Oakover River to form De Grey
- **Key towns:** None
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Joins the Oakover to form the De Grey. Strelley (tributary) passes through mining tenements. Aboriginal cultural significance.

### Shaw River
- **Length:** ~200 km
- **Source:** Ophthalmia Range / Hamersley Range (western branch)
- **Mouth:** De Grey River (tributary)
- **Key towns:** None
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Tributary of the De Grey River system. Remote Pilbara. Gold and mineral exploration in catchment.

### Sherlock River
- **Length:** ~120 km
- **Source:** Chichester Range hinterland
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean (south of Port Walcott)
- **Key towns:** None (Sherlock Station)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Smaller Pilbara coastal river. Sherlock Station is a pastoral lease. Coastal wetlands at the river mouth.

## Gascoyne Region

### Gascoyne River
- **Length:** ~865 km
- **Source:** Robinson Ranges near Wiluna (upper Gascoyne) / Collier Range (middle)
- **Mouth:** Shark Bay / Indian Ocean at Carnarvon
- **Key towns:** Carnarvon, Gascoyne Junction
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — flows only after significant rainfall in upper catchments; flows underground through aquifer for much of year
- **Features:** Longest river in WA. The Gascoyne is famous for being a "river of sand" — usually dry on the surface but with substantial underground aquifer flow beneath the riverbed. Carnarvon's horticultural region (bananas, vegetables, tropical fruit) depends on groundwater from the Gascoyne alluvial aquifer. Kennedy Range National Park is in the catchment. Major floods recorded (2010-11 devastated Carnarvon plantations). Lyons River is a major tributary.

### Wooramel River
- **Length:** ~220 km
- **Source:** Near Mount Augustus
- **Mouth:** Shark Bay (south-east arm)
- **Key towns:** None
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Flows into Shark Bay south of Carnarvon. Wooramel Station. Typically dry except after rare heavy rain. Hot springs along the river (Wooramel Artesian Bore).

### Murchison River
- **Length:** ~780 km
- **Source:** Robinson Ranges (near Meekatharra)
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean at Kalbarri
- **Key towns:** Kalbarri, Murchison Settlement
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral — flows in winter after inland rain; Kalbarri section may have dry season pools
- **Features:** Famous for Kalbarri National Park — Murchison Gorge, Nature's Window, and Z-Bend. Dramatic sandstone gorges. Lower reaches maintain permanent pools. Popular canoeing in the gorge in season. Mouth at Kalbarri is tidal. Sandford River and Roderick River are major tributaries. Aboriginal heritage (Nanda and Malgana peoples).

### Hutt River
- **Length:** ~60 km
- **Source:** East of Northampton
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean (north of Port Gregory)
- **Key towns:** None (Hutt River Province/Principality until 2020)
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Famous for the now-dissolved Hutt River Province (micronation 1970-2020). Hutt Lagoon (pink lake) adjacent to the mouth. Small coastal river. Dry most of the year.

### Chapman River
- **Length:** ~105 km
- **Source:** Near Yuna
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean at Geraldton (North Beach/Bluff Point)
- **Key towns:** Geraldton, Nabawa
- **Perennial?** Mostly ephemeral — winter flow
- **Features:** Geraldton's primary river system. Chapman River Regional Park protects remnant bushland. Seasonal flow. Important to Yamatji people. Chapman River weir provides some local water.

### Greenough River
- **Length:** ~340 km
- **Source:** East of Morawa
- **Mouth:** Indian Ocean south of Geraldton (Cape Burney)
- **Key towns:** Geraldton (near mouth), Walkaway
- **Perennial?** Ephemeral
- **Features:** Greenough River mouth is a popular beach/fishing spot near Geraldton. Ellendale Pool — a significant permanent waterhole fed by springs — is a key water source about 45 km upstream. Historic Greenough settlement (flats) nearby. Flows only after inland rainfall.

## Goldfields & Wheatbelt

> **Note:** Most rivers in the Wheatbelt/Goldfields are **strongly ephemeral**, flowing only after rare heavy rain. Permanent surface water is almost non-existent — reliance on groundwater bores and the Goldfields Water Supply Scheme.

### Murchison River — also listed under Gascoyne
See above. Upper reaches near Meekatharra/Cue are goldfields country.

### Salt River Systems (Wheatbelt)
The Wheatbelt is characterised by extensive salt lake chains, not flowing rivers. The Avon River (listed under Perth region) is the main waterway. Other named watercourses (Lockhart, Mortlock, Yenyening Lakes system) are almost universally saline and dry. Many were salinised by land clearing. Not listed individually as they are not reliable water sources.

## SHTF Water Notes

1. **South-West (Augusta to Walpole):** Most reliable natural surface water in WA. High rainfall, perennial rivers. Can be filtered/treated. Watch for agricultural runoff.

2. **Perth Region:** Swan/Canning are tidal and saline in lower reaches. Fresh water only upstream of Guildford (Swan) / Kent St Weir (Canning). Hills reservoirs (Mundaring, Canning, Serpentine, Wungong dams) are the actual water supply.

3. **Kimberley:** Abundant wet season water, but dry season reduces most rivers to disconnected pools. Ord system (Lake Argyle) is the only reliable year-round major water source. Saltwater crocodiles in all coastal/ocean-accessible Kimberley rivers — do NOT enter water.

4. **Pilbara:** Surface water is rare. Fortescue Marsh area, Millstream pools, and Harding Dam are the only reliable surface water. Groundwater is saline in many areas. Mining operations control much of the accessible groundwater.

5. **Gascoyne/Murchison:** The Gascoyne is an underground river — you need to dig into the riverbed sand to access water. Murchison Gorge has permanent pools in lower reaches. Water is scarce inland.

6. **Goldfields/Wheatbelt:** Almost no natural surface water. Kalgoorlie relies entirely on the Goldfields pipeline from Mundaring. Vast salt lake systems are undrinkable. Groundwater is often hypersaline. This is the hardest region for water in any SHTF scenario.
