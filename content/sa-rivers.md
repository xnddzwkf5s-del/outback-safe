# South Australia — Major River Systems & Water Bodies

> **Last updated:** 2026-07-28
> **Purpose:** SHTF water awareness — navigable routes, potable sources, flood risks, salinity hazards.
> **Key:** 🟢 potable (treatable) | 🟡 brackish — treat with care | 🔴 saline — do not drink | ⚠️ ephemeral — seasonal/unreliable

---

## 1. Murray River (Lower Reaches)

**Classification:** River — perennial, regulated
**Water quality:** 🟢 Fresh (variable; can be 🟡 during low-flow/drought)
**Length in SA:** ~650 km (SA border to mouth)
**Catchment:** Murray–Darling Basin (~1,060,000 km² total)

South Australia receives the tail end of the Murray–Darling system. Flow is highly regulated by upstream dams and weirs (Hume, Dartmouth, Menindee Lakes). The lower Murray supports irrigation, town water supply, and navigation.

**Course through SA:**
- Enters SA near Renmark (border with Victoria/NSW)
- Flows west through Renmark, Berri, Loxton, Waikerie, Morgan, Blanchetown
- Turns south at Morgan, passing Swan Reach, Mannum, Murray Bridge
- Terminates at Lake Alexandrina and the Murray Mouth (Goolwa)

**Key Towns (riverbank):** Renmark, Berri, Loxton, Waikerie, Morgan, Blanchetown, Swan Reach, Mannum, Murray Bridge, Tailem Bend, Goolwa

**Features:**
- **Lock & Weir System (Locks 1–9):** Maintains navigable pools; Lock 1 at Blanchetown is the first upstream from the mouth
- **Chowilla Floodplain:** Ramsar-listed wetland near the border, critical bird habitat
- **Murray Mouth dredging:** Constant dredging keeps the mouth open; if it closes, the Lower Lakes stagnate
- **Barrages (Goolwa Barrages):** Five barrages between Goolwa and the Coorong prevent saltwater intrusion into Lake Alexandrina

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Generally fresh and treatable, but salinity rises during drought (can exceed 800 EC at Morgan in dry years)
- Agricultural return flows introduce nutrients — algal blooms possible in summer
- Blue-green algae warnings common in weir pools during hot, low-flow periods
- Treat before drinking: filter (1 micron), boil or chemical disinfect

**SHTF Notes:**
- The Murray is SA's primary freshwater artery — critical in any emergency
- Pump-irrigation infrastructure exists along the entire SA reach
- River levels can drop 2–3 m if upstream storages fail
- Houseboats and ferries provide river-crossing capability if bridges are out

## 2. Torrens River (Karrawirra Parri)

**Classification:** River — perennial (upper), intermittent/regulated (lower)
**Water quality:** 🟡 Variable (urban runoff)
**Length:** ~85 km

Rises in the Mount Lofty Ranges near Mount Pleasant, flows through the Adelaide Hills, then across the Adelaide Plains through the Adelaide CBD to the sea at West Beach/Henley Beach.

**Course:**
- Headwaters: near Mount Pleasant (Mount Lofty Ranges)
- Kangaroo Creek Reservoir, Millbrook Reservoir, Hope Valley Reservoir (Adelaide water supply)
- Linear Park (Torrens Linear Park) — 30 km of shared-use trail from hills to city
- Adelaide CBD: Torrens Lake (weir at city — ornamental, not potable)
- Breakout Creek: artificial channel to the sea at Henley Beach South

**Key Towns:** Mount Pleasant, Birdwood, Gumeracha, Campbelltown, Adelaide CBD, Henley Beach

**Features:**
- **Torrens Lake:** Artificial lake in the city centre, held by a weir — recreational only, blue-green algae common in summer
- **Linear Park:** Major east-west green corridor; bike/pedestrian evacuation route
- **Breakout Creek:** Engineered flood-relief channel; during heavy rain prevents CBD flooding

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Upper reaches: good quality near reservoirs (Metro water supply)
- Lower reaches: urban stormwater, industrial legacy — do not drink untreated
- Torrens Lake: stagnant in summer, algae-prone — avoid
- Treat upper-reaches water with filtration and disinfection

**SHTF Notes:**
- Upper Torrens / reservoirs are part of Adelaide's metropolitan water supply
- Linear Park = non-vehicular corridor through the city
- Lower Torrens not a reliable drinking source after urban stretch

## 3. Onkaparinga River

**Classification:** River — perennial (upper), seasonal (lower)
**Water quality:** 🟢 Upper — good / 🟡 Lower — variable
**Length:** ~95 km

Rises near Mount Torrens in the Adelaide Hills, flows south through the Onkaparinga Valley, cuts the Onkaparinga Gorge (Onkaparinga River National Park), and enters the sea at Port Noarlunga (Onkaparinga Estuary).

**Course:**
- Headwaters: Mount Torrens / Charleston area
- Mount Bold Reservoir (SA's largest reservoir — 46,000 ML capacity)
- Onkaparinga Gorge (rugged, deep — National Park)
- McLaren Vale wine region — vineyard irrigation
- Estuary at Port Noarlunga (McLaren Vale/Gulf St Vincent)

**Key Towns:** Charleston, Woodside (near), Hahndorf (near), Clarendon (gorge overlook), Old Noarlunga, Port Noarlunga

**Features:**
- **Mount Bold Reservoir:** Critical drinking water storage for Adelaide
- **Onkaparinga Gorge:** Steep, hard to cross — natural barrier, rich biodiversity
- **National Park:** Walking trails, rock climbing, deep pools in the gorge
- **Estuary:** Tidal lagoon at Port Noarlunga; popular recreational area

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Upper reaches / reservoir: excellent quality, Metro water source
- Gorge pools: generally clean but giardia risk from wildlife — treat
- Lower reaches / estuary: tidal, saline at mouth, agricultural runoff from vineyards
- Seasonal flow in lower reaches; can dry to pools in summer below the gorge

**SHTF Notes:**
- Mount Bold Reservoir = major strategic water asset
- Gorge pools = emergency water for southern Adelaide Hills residents
- Estuary = not potable (saline tidal)

## 4. Gawler River

**Classification:** River — intermittent
**Water quality:** 🟡 Variable
**Length:** ~40 km (confluence to sea)

Formed by the confluence of the North Para and South Para Rivers near Gawler. Drains the northern Mount Lofty Ranges and the Barossa Valley. Flows west across the northern Adelaide Plains to Gulf St Vincent (near Port Gawler).

**Course:**
- Confluence: North Para + South Para meet at Gawler
- Flows west past Angle Vale, Virginia (market gardens)
- Mouth: Port Gawler / Buckland Park (Gulf St Vincent) — often closed sandbar

**Key Towns:** Gawler, Angle Vale, Virginia

**Features:**
- **North Para River:** Drains Barossa Valley — Nuriootpa, Tanunda
- **South Para River:** South Para Reservoir (Adelaide water supply), Williamstown
- **Virginia Pipeline Scheme:** Treated wastewater used for market garden irrigation on the plains (not the Gawler River itself)
- **Sandbar:** Mouth usually closed by a sandbar unless heavy flows open it

**Water Quality Notes:**
- North/South Para Rivers (upper): good quality in hills — treatable
- Gawler River (lower plains): agricultural return flows, nutrient loading — treat before drinking
- Ephemeral in lower reaches; dry in summer except for irrigation returns
- South Para Reservoir: drinking-quality water

**SHTF Notes:**
- South Para Reservoir = water supply asset
- Gawler River not reliable in summer; sandbar-closed mouth means no tidal mixing
- Market gardens along the plains = significant food production zone (but depends on piped water, not river flow)

## 5. Light River

**Classification:** River — intermittent
**Water quality:** 🟡 Variable
**Length:** ~160 km

Rises near Waterloo in the Mount Lofty Ranges, flows north-west through the mid-north through Kapunda, then west to Gulf St Vincent near Port Parham.

**Course:**
- Headwaters: Waterloo / Manoora area
- Kapunda (historic copper mining town)
- Hamley Bridge, Mallala
- Mouth: Port Parham (Gulf St Vincent) — typically sandbar-closed

**Key Towns:** Kapunda, Hamley Bridge, Mallala

**Features:**
- **Kapunda:** Copper mining history — mine water can be acidic/metallic; avoid old mine drainage
- **River Red Gums:** Mature gum-lined sections in the mid-reaches (good shade, firewood, landmark navigation)
- **Ephemeral flow:** Often dry in summer; flows after autumn/winter rains

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Upper reaches: reasonably fresh after rain
- Mid-reaches: variable — agricultural runoff (sheep, wheat)
- Kapunda area: possible mine-water contamination — test before use
- Lower reaches: brackish near mouth, sandbar-closed

**SHTF Notes:**
- Seasonal reliability is poor; find permanent waterholes in deeper sections
- River red gum corridors = shade, navigation, and (historically) indicates groundwater within ~15 m depth
- Not a primary water artery; backup only

## 6. Wakefield River

**Classification:** River — intermittent
**Water quality:** 🟡 Variable
**Length:** ~120 km

Rises near Manoora in the mid-north, flows south-west through Auburn and Balaklava to Gulf St Vincent at Port Wakefield.

**Course:**
- Headwaters: Manoora area
- Auburn (Clare Valley wine region)
- Balaklava
- Mouth: Port Wakefield

**Key Towns:** Auburn, Balaklava, Port Wakefield

**Features:**
- **Clare Valley:** Upper catchment — vineyard and agricultural country
- **Port Wakefield:** Historic port town at the mouth
- **Rail trail:** Old railway corridor follows the river — potential overland route

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Upper reaches (Auburn area): reasonable after rain — treat
- Mid-lower reaches: agricultural runoff, variable salinity
- Mostly dry in summer; waterholes may persist in deeper sections
- Mouth: tidal, saline

**SHTF Notes:**
- Seasonal reliability poor
- Rail-trail corridor = non-vehicular north-south route alternative to Highway 1

## 7. Broughton River

**Classification:** River — intermittent
**Water quality:** 🟡 Variable
**Length:** ~120 km

Rises near Spalding in the southern Flinders Ranges, flows south-west through the mid-north to Spencer Gulf near Port Davis (north of Port Pirie).

**Course:**
- Headwaters: Spalding area
- Flows through mixed farming country
- Passes near Crystal Brook (but not through — tributary connection)
- Mouth: Port Davis (mangrove-lined tidal flats, Spencer Gulf)

**Key Towns:** Spalding, Port Davis (nearby: Crystal Brook, Port Pirie)

**Features:**
- **Spalding:** Town water from Broughton River (offtake)
- **Crystal Brook:** Tributary to Broughton; Crystal Brook township draws from the brook
- **Mangrove estuary:** Port Davis — saltmarsh, bird habitat, not potable

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Upper reaches: fresh, used for town water supply (Spalding) — treatable
- Lower reaches: increasingly saline as it approaches Spencer Gulf
- Intermittent — dry in summer

**SHTF Notes:**
- Spalding/Crystal Brook area = possible fresh water source in the southern Flinders
- Not reliable below the mid-reaches

## 8. Flinders Ranges Creeks

**Classification:** Creeks — ephemeral ⚠️
**Water quality:** 🟢 Natural (remote headwaters) → 🟡 after long dry spells
**Catchment:** Internal drainage — most do not reach the sea

The Flinders Ranges feature numerous creeks that flow only after heavy rain. Permanent waterholes exist in deeper gorges and are critical for wildlife (and historically, for human survival). The three most notable:

### 8a. Brachina Creek (Brachina Gorge)

- **Location:** Brachina Gorge Geological Trail (Flinders Ranges National Park, ~20 km north of Wilpena)
- **Water:** Seasonal pools in the gorge; permanent waterholes in deeper sections
- **Quality:** Generally good (remote catchment) — filter and treat
- **Notes:** Popular tourist drive; deep, shaded pools hold water for months after rain
- **SHTF:** Reliable refuge water — known to locals; dense vegetation indicates water proximity

### 8b. Parachilna Creek (Parachilna Gorge)

- **Location:** Parachilna Gorge (~15 km east of Parachilna township)
- **Water:** Permanent waterholes; spring-fed in sections
- **Quality:** Good — remote arid catchment, minimal agricultural impact
- **Notes:** Gorge cuts through Heysen Range; camping area at gorge mouth
- **SHTF:** One of the more reliable Flinders water sources; marks route to Blinman

### 8c. Wilpena Creek (Wilpena Pound)

- **Location:** Wilpena Pound Resort area
- **Water:** Flows out of Wilpena Pound; semi-permanent pools
- **Quality:** Good — runs through National Park
- **Notes:** The Pound's internal drainage exits through Wilpena Gap; creek runs past the resort
- **SHTF:** Highest-traffic water source in Flinders Ranges; resort bore water also available

**General Flinders Ranges Water Notes:**
- Waterholes persist longest in south-facing gorges with red gum overstorey
- **Aroona, Bunyeroo, and Brachina Gorges** are most reliable
- After rain, surface water disappears within days to weeks; permanent holes in deep gorges
- Groundwater in the Flinders is deep (>100 m in most places) — bores exist at stations and towns
- Wilpena Pound Resort, Rawnsley Park Station, and Arkaroola have established bores

## 9. Cooper Creek (Channel Country)

**Classification:** Creek — ephemeral ⚠️ / annual flood pulse
**Water quality:** 🟡 Fresh when flowing; saline waterholes in drought
**Length:** ~1,300 km (Queensland headwaters to Lake Eyre)
**Catchment:** ~300,000 km² (Lake Eyre Basin)

Cooper Creek is one of the world's last unregulated wild rivers. It rises in western Queensland (near Longreach as the Barcoo and Thomson Rivers), enters SA near Innamincka, and disperses into a vast network of channels, floodplains, and waterholes before terminating at Lake Eyre (Kati Thanda). It reaches Lake Eyre only in exceptional flood years (approx 1 in 5–8 years).

**Course in SA:**
- Crosses the SA border northeast of Innamincka
- Innamincka township sits on Cooper Creek
- The **Strzecki Track** crosses the Cooper floodplain (impassable when flowing)
- Terminal: Lake Eyre (north) via the Cooper's western distributaries

**Key Locations:** Innamincka, Moomba (gas fields, ~100 km south), Birdsville Track crossing point

**Features:**
- **Coongie Lakes:** Ramsar-listed wetland system, fed by Cooper floods — massive bird breeding events after flood years
- **Cullyamurra Waterhole:** Largest permanent waterhole on Cooper Creek (~20 km long when full)
- **Channel Country floods:** Slow-moving flood pulse (takes months from Qld to Lake Eyre) — water spreads across hundreds of km of floodplain
- **Burke and Wills:** Dig Tree and grave sites near Cooper Creek (innamincka area)

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Very fresh when in flood — excellent water
- Permanent waterholes (Cullyamurra, etc.) become increasingly saline during prolonged drought (evaporation concentrates salts)
- Treat all surface water — wildlife/bird contamination in waterholes
- Remote — no agricultural runoff, so chemical contamination is low

**SHTF Notes:**
- Cooper Creek waterholes = lifeline in the far north-east of SA
- Cullyamurra Waterhole = most reliable permanent water in the Channel Country
- Flood waters are drinkable after treatment
- No infrastructure between Innamincka and Birdsville/Lyndhurst — be self-sufficient

## 10. Diamantina River

**Classification:** River — ephemeral ⚠️ / annual flood pulse
**Water quality:** 🟡 Fresh when flowing
**Length:** ~900 km
**Catchment:** ~157,000 km² (Lake Eyre Basin)

Rises in central-west Queensland, flows south-west, crosses into SA, and terminates in the Goyder Lagoon / Warburton River system which ultimately feeds Lake Eyre. Like the Cooper, it is unregulated and flood-driven.

**Course in SA:**
- Crosses SA border north of Birdsville
- Passes through Goyder Lagoon (vast floodplain wetland on the Birdsville Track)
- Joins the Warburton Creek system which channels floodwaters toward Lake Eyre

**Key Locations:** Birdsville (Qld, just over border), Goyder Lagoon, Warburton Crossing (Birdsville Track)

**Features:**
- **Goyder Lagoon:** Enormous ephemeral wetland; fills during Diamantina floods
- **Channel Country:** Multi-channel anabranching — water spreads across the landscape
- **Birdsville Track crossing:** Warburton Creek crossing point; famously impassable when flowing

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Clean when in flood (remote catchment)
- Waterholes shrink and salinise during drought
- Goyder Lagoon water is fresh when recently flooded, brackish as it dries
- Wildlife/mammal contamination risk — always treat

**SHTF Notes:**
- Diamantina/Goyder Lagoon floods = massive but infrequent water resource
- Birdsville Track impassable during floods (weeks to months)
- Warburton waterholes = emergency water on the track

## 11. Frome River

**Classification:** River — ephemeral ⚠️
**Water quality:** 🔴 Generally saline / 🟡 after heavy rain
**Length:** ~240 km

Rises in the northern Flinders Ranges (near Mount Serle), flows west and south-west, then turns north to flow into the eastern side of Lake Eyre (North). The Frome is a major drainage line for the northern Flinders but flows only after significant rain. It carries naturally high sediment and salt loads from the surrounding arid country.

**Course:**
- Northern Flinders Ranges (near Nepabunna / Mount Serle)
- Flows west then north-east
- Terminates at Lake Eyre (east/north-east side)

**Key Locations:** Near Nepabunna; crosses no major towns (remote)

**Features:**
- **Lake Frome:** Note — Lake Frome is a separate large salt lake east of the Flinders Ranges; the Frome River does NOT flow into Lake Frome (counter-intuitive). Lake Frome is an endorheic salt pan.
- **The Blanket:** Shallow floodout areas where Frome spreads across wide plains before reaching Lake Eyre
- **Mineralised soils:** Catchment includes mineralised Flinders geology; water may have elevated minerals/metals

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Seldom flowing; when it does, it carries heavy sediment and dissolved salts
- Bottom-end waterholes may be saline even when fresh upstream
- Treat all surface water; test salinity if possible
- Not a reliable water source

**SHTF Notes:**
- Avoid reliance on the Frome; there are better Flinders Range water sources (see Section 8)
- Remote country — no services

## 12. Lake Eyre / Kati Thanda

**Classification:** Endorheic salt lake — ephemeral
**Water quality:** 🔴 Saline (hypersaline when evaporating); 🟡 fresh only during rare filling events
**Surface area:** ~9,500 km² (combined North + South lakes)
**Lowest point:** ~15 m below sea level (Australia's lowest point)

Australia's largest lake — actually two connected lake beds: Lake Eyre North (8,430 km²) and Lake Eyre South (1,260 km²), joined by the Goyder Channel. Fills completely only 3–4 times per century; partial filling occurs more often (every few years from major Channel Country floods).

**Sources of water:**
- **Cooper Creek** (via the Channel Country) — eastern inflow to Lake Eyre North
- **Warburton River / Diamantina system** — northern inflow to Lake Eyre North
- **Macumba River** (local SA catchment) — into Lake Eyre North
- **Frome River** — eastern inflow to Lake Eyre North
- **Local rainfall** — direct precipitation over the lake

**Features:**
- **Kati Thanda:** Aboriginal name; sacred site for Arabana people
- **Lake Eyre Yacht Club:** Monitors fill events; recreational sailing during rare fillings
- **Oodnadatta Track:** Passes near Lake Eyre South (viewing points)
- **Lake Eyre North:** Remote; accessed via Warburton Track or air
- **Salt crust:** Thick salt crust (up to 50 cm in places) forms as water evaporates; can support vehicle weight in some areas (high risk)

**Water Quality Notes:**
- **Filling phase:** Water is fresh to brackish — theoretically treatable (but remote, turbid, ephemeral)
- **Evaporating phase:** Becomes hyper-saline (>200 g/L TDS — ~6× seawater) — undrinkable
- **Dry phase:** Salt pan — no liquid water
- When the lake fills, a "sea" appears in the desert — but it's temporary, lasting months to ~2 years maximum
- The rapid ecosystem response (fish, birds, wildflowers) indicates the water is viable when fresh

**SHTF Notes:**
- Not a reliable water source — fills too rarely
- Filling events are nationally publicised — you'll know about it
- Dry salt pan = vehicle crossing risk (salt crust can collapse)
- No permanent settlements on the lake itself; nearest town = Marree (~100 km from Lake Eyre South)
- Halligan Bay and ABC Bay on Lake Eyre North = vehicle access points

## 13. Lake Torrens

**Classification:** Endorheic salt lake — ephemeral
**Water quality:** 🔴 Saline
**Surface area:** ~5,700 km²

A large, normally dry salt lake in central SA, west of the Flinders Ranges. Lake Torrens is a national park and is typically a dry salt pan. It fills only after rare heavy rains in its immediate catchment (Flinders Ranges western slopes).

**Features:**
- **Lake Torrens National Park:** Access restricted; no permanent water
- **Western Flinders boundary:** Forms the western edge of the Flinders Ranges
- **Shape:** Elongated north-south, ~240 km long, ~65 km wide
- **Salt crust:** Thick, hazardous — do not attempt to cross
- **Islands:** Andamooka Island, Windy Island — permanent features within the lake bed

**Key Towns (nearby):** Leigh Creek (~40 km east), Andamooka (~30 km south-west)

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Normally dry; when filled, water is saline (salt crust dissolves)
- Not a drinking water source — salt pan
- Groundwater around lake margins is highly saline

**SHTF Notes:**
- Do NOT rely on this lake for water
- Salt pan = navigation hazard for vehicles
- Marks the western boundary of the accessible Flinders Ranges

## 14. Lake Gairdner

**Classification:** Endorheic salt lake — ephemeral
**Water quality:** 🔴 Saline
**Surface area:** ~4,300 km² (SA's third-largest salt lake)

Located on the Gawler Ranges, north of the Eyre Peninsula. A vast, exceptionally flat salt pan. Famous for land-speed record attempts and the "Lake Gairdner Speed Week."

**Features:**
- **Lake Gairdner National Park:** Access via dirt roads from Iron Knob / Woomera / Kingoonya
- **Gawler Ranges:** Bounding the lake; rugged granite hills
- **Extreme flatness:** Surface deviates <1 m over the entire lake bed — hence the speed records
- **Islands:** Several granite islands rise from the salt pan (Mount Ive, etc.)

**Key Towns (nearby):** Kimba (~150 km south), Iron Knob (~120 km east), Woomera (~180 km east)

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Permanently dry salt pan; water in the Gawler Ranges creeks after rain is the real drinking source
- Do not attempt to drink lake water
- Nearby homesteads and stations use rainwater tanks and bores (groundwater variable — mostly saline)

**SHTF Notes:**
- No water at the lake itself
- Gawler Ranges granite rockholes = traditional Aboriginal water sources after rain
- Nearest fresh water: tank water at Mount Ive Station or rainfall in Gawler Ranges rockholes

## 15. Lake Alexandrina

**Classification:** Freshwater coastal lake
**Water quality:** 🟢 Fresh (barrage-managed) → 🟡 during drought (saline intrusion risk)
**Surface area:** ~570 km²

One of the two Lower Lakes at the terminus of the Murray River. Connected to Lake Albert via a narrow channel near Narrung. Separated from the sea (Encounter Bay/Coorong) by five barrages at Goolwa, Mundoo, Boundary Creek, Ewe Island, and Tauwitchere.

**Key Towns:** Milang, Clayton Bay, Goolwa, Narrung (Peninsula)

**Features:**
- **Goolwa Barrages:** Critical infrastructure — prevent seawater intrusion into the Lower Lakes
- **Murray Mouth:** The river's exit point to the Southern Ocean, at the western end of the Coorong
- **Milang:** Historic river port on the lake shore; railway museum
- **Narrung Ferry:** Cable ferry across the narrows between Alexandrina and Albert
- **Ramsar Wetland:** One of Australia's most important wetlands for migratory birds

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Freshwater under normal conditions (barrages closed)
- During Millennium Drought (1997–2009): lake levels dropped >1 m, salinity rose, acid sulfate soils exposed
- Barrages are the single point of failure — if they are breached, the Lower Lakes become tidal/saline
- Algal blooms possible in summer (nutrient-loaded inflow from Murray)
- Town water for Goolwa/Port Elliot/Victor Harbor is drawn from the River Murray upstream of the lakes (pipeline)

**SHTF Notes:**
- If barrages fail or are not maintained, Lake Alexandrina becomes saline within months
- Vast body of fairly shallow water (avg depth ~2.8 m) — evaporation loss is high
- Fishing resource: European carp, callop (golden perch), congolli, yabbies
- Boat access to Murray River system; potential for water transport
- Entire Lower Lakes system depends on Murray River inflows and barrage integrity

## 16. Lake Albert

**Classification:** Freshwater coastal lake
**Water quality:** 🟢 Fresh (managed) → 🟡 during drought
**Surface area:** ~170 km²

The smaller of the two Lower Lakes, connected to Lake Alexandrina at the Narrung Narrows. More susceptible to salinity issues than Alexandrina due to its smaller volume and restricted mixing.

**Key Towns:** Meningie (eastern shore)

**Features:**
- **Narrung Narrows:** ~200 m wide channel connecting to Lake Alexandrina — the only mixing point; natural bottleneck
- **Meningie:** Main town on the lake; fishing and tourism economy
- **Shallow:** Average depth ~1.7 m — warms quickly, high evaporation
- **Coorong connection:** Meningie is the gateway town between the Lower Lakes and the Coorong

**Water Quality Notes:**
- Poorer flushing than Alexandrina — more prone to salinity accumulation and algal blooms
- During drought, Lake Albert suffered worse than Alexandrina
- Fish kills possible during heatwaves (low oxygen in shallow, warm water)
- Same dependence on barrage integrity as Alexandrina

**SHTF Notes:**
- Meningie = key lakeside settlement with boat ramp, services
- Narrow channel at Narrung = chokepoint for boat movement between the two lakes
- Water quality degrades faster than Alexandrina in crisis scenarios

## 17. The Coorong

**Classification:** Coastal lagoon system — saline to hypersaline
**Water quality:** 🔴 Saline (south) → 🟡 Brackish (north, near Murray Mouth)
**Length:** ~140 km

A narrow, elongated lagoon separated from the Southern Ocean by the Younghusband Peninsula (sand dune barrier). Runs south-east from the Murray Mouth (Goolwa) to near Kingston SE. The Coorong is a Ramsar wetland of international significance and a sacred site for the Ngarrindjeri people.

**Features:**
- **Younghusband Peninsula:** Long, narrow sand dune barrier — ocean beach on seaward side, Coorong lagoon on landward side
- **Murray Mouth:** At the northern end; the only connection to the sea (maintained by dredging)
- **South Lagoon:** Closed, hypersaline — >100 g/L TDS (~3× seawater) — no inflow
- **North Lagoon:** Less saline, receives some freshwater seepage from the Murray Mouth/barrages
- **Goolwa Channel:** Area between Goolwa and Murray Mouth, fresher than main Coorong
- **Birdlife:** Massive populations of migratory waders (red-necked stint, sharp-tailed sandpiper, banded stilt)
- **Pelican breeding colony:** Australia's largest at Pelican Point

**Key Towns:** Goolwa (north end), Meningie (mid-point, on Lake Albert, gateway to Coorong), Salt Creek (South Lagoon rest stop)

**Water Quality Notes:**
- **North Lagoon:** Brackish near Murray Mouth — becoming increasingly saline with distance south
- **South Lagoon:** Hypersaline — >100 g/L TDS (saltier than the sea) — undrinkable
- **Goolwa Channel:** Least saline section, but still brackish
- **Murray Mouth area:** Variable — fresh when river is flowing strongly, salty during low-flow
- The Coorong is NOT a drinking water source — treat it as saltwater

**SHTF Notes:**
- NOT a freshwater source — saline lagoon
- Younghusband Peninsula = barrier to overland travel heading south-east from Goolwa (must go inland via Meningie / Princes Highway unless you're on the beach)
- The Coorong's fish (mulloway, mullet, flounder) are a food source even in SHTF — netting from the shore is possible
- 42 Mile Crossing and Tea Tree Crossing = vehicle access across Younghusband Peninsula from the Princes Highway to the ocean beach
- The lagoon is too salty to drink but provides fisheries and birdlife (protein)
