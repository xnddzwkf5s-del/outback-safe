# NSW Rivers — Overview & Geography

> Source: Wikipedia, Geoscience Australia
> Keywords: New South Wales rivers, NSW drainage, Great Dividing Range, coastal rivers, inland rivers, Murray-Darling Basin, water sources NSW, river systems NSW, NSW geography

## Overview

New South Wales has two main categories of rivers: those that rise in the Great Dividing Range and flow eastwards to the Coral and Tasman Seas (Coastal NSW Rivers), and those that rise on the western side of the range and flow inland (Inland NSW Rivers). Most inland rivers eventually combine into the Murray-Darling network.

The Great Dividing Range runs north-south roughly parallel to the coast, ranging from 50-150 km inland. Coastal rivers are generally short, steep, and subject to flash flooding. Inland rivers are long, slow-flowing, and carry low volumes by global standards.

## Major River Systems by Length

- **Murray River**: 2,508 km — longest in Australia, forms NSW/Victoria border
- **Murrumbidgee River**: 1,485 km — flows through Canberra, Wagga Wagga to the Murray
- **Darling River**: 1,472 km — flows from the northern tablelands through Bourke, Menindee
- **Lachlan River**: 1,339 km — flows through Forbes, Condobolin to the Murrumbidgee
- **Warrego River**: 1,380 km — flows from Queensland through western NSW
- **Macquarie River**: 960 km — flows through Bathurst, Dubbo to the Macquarie Marshes
- **Gwydir River**: 490 km — flows from the New England region westwards
- **Namoi River**: 540 km — flows through Gunnedah, Narrabri to the Darling
- **Castlereagh River**: 480 km — flows through Coonamble
- **Hunter River**: 470 km — major river of the Hunter Valley

## Coastal Rivers (East-Flowing)

From north to south, the major coastal rivers are:

**Northern NSW**: Tweed River, Brunswick River, Richmond River, Clarence River (NSW's largest coastal river), Bellinger River, Nambucca River, Macleay River, Hastings River, Manning River

**Central NSW**: Hunter River, Karuah River, Wyong River, Hawkesbury River, Nepean River, Parramatta River, Georges River, Woronora River, Hacking River

**Southern NSW**: Shoalhaven River, Clyde River, Moruya River, Tuross River, Bega River, Towamba River, Snowy River

## Inland Rivers (West-Flowing)

The inland rivers of the Murray-Darling Basin in NSW include:

**Murray Group**: Murray River (border), Murrumbidgee River, Lachlan River
**Darling Group**: Darling River, Macquarie River, Castlereagh River, Namoi River, Gwydir River, Barwon River, Culgoa River, Birrie River, Bokhara River, Narran River
**Western**: Paroo River, Warrego River, Darling River

## Rainfall Patterns

- **Northern NSW**: Summer-dominant rainfall (monsoonal influence), floods December-March
- **Eastern NSW**: Year-round rainfall, winter maximum on the tablelands
- **Southern NSW**: Winter-dominant rainfall, summer dry
- **Western NSW**: Highly variable, drought-flood cycles, average 200-500 mm/year
- **Coastal strip**: 800-1,500 mm/year
- **Great Dividing Range**: 800-1,600 mm/year

## Key Water Safety Notes

- **Flooding**: Coastal rivers flood rapidly after heavy rain. Never attempt to cross a flooded causeway.
- **Water quality**: Inland rivers may have high salinity, blue-green algae (toxic to humans and animals)
- **Crocodiles**: None in NSW waters. No crocodiles south of the Mary River in Queensland.
- **Bull sharks**: Present in Hawkesbury River, Parramatta River, Lake Macquarie, and other large coastal river systems
- **Bushfire water contamination**: Ash and debris can contaminate water sources after bushfires
