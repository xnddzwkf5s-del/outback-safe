---

- **Nearest water**: Derwent River (estuary) — immediate western/northern boundary; Sandy Bay / Sullivans Cove on waterfront
- **Backup**: Mount Wellington/kunanyi creeks (Hobart Rivulet, New Town Rivulet, Sandy Bay Rivulet) — seasonal flow
- **Coordinates**: -42.8821, 147.3272

## Critical Warnings

### DO NOT DRINK WITHOUT TREATMENT:

1. **Queen River / King River (Queenstown area)** — Acid mine drainage, heavy metals (copper, zinc, lead). pH as low as 2.5. Requires advanced filtration — not just boiling. Use Lake Burbury instead.

2. **Tamar River (Launceston)** — Tidal estuary; salt water. Also affected by upstream agricultural runoff and sediment. Go upstream of Trevallyn Dam to South Esk or North Esk for fresh water.

3. **Derwent River (Hobart near mouth)** — Tidal salt/brackish. Heavy metal contamination in sediments from historical industry (zinc works). Go upstream of New Norfolk for freshwater Derwent.

4. **Macquarie Harbour (Strahan)** — Huge harbour but entirely salt/brackish. King River flows into it — historically polluted. Gordon River freshwater is accessible by boat.

5. **East coast lagoons** (Moulting Lagoon/Swan River, Southport Lagoon) — Can be brackish, especially after dry periods due to coastal sandbar closures. Test for salt before drinking.

### BEST RELIABLE FRESHWATER SOURCES:

- **Great Lake** — Massive volume, high elevation, minimal contamination (just don't drink untreated)
- **Lake Gordon / Lake Pedder** — Enormous remote storages, but difficult to access
- **South Esk River** — Tasmania's longest; good flow year-round; many access points along the Midlands
- **Derwent River above New Norfolk** — Reliable, large volume, freshwater
- **Huon River above Huonville** — Reliable, large catchment, freshwater
- **Pieman River system** — Multiple large hydro lakes, good volume, remote
