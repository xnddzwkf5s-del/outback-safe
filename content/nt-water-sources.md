# Northern Territory — Water Sources by Town

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING — SALTWATER CROCODILES:** Saltwater crocodiles (*Crocodylus porosus*) are present in ALL tidal rivers, estuaries, billabongs, and coastal waters north of approximately Katherine (16°S). This includes every Top End town, every river, every creek mouth, and even waterholes that have tidal connection. Assume any natural water body north of Katherine holds a lethal predator. Do not enter the water. Do not camp at the water's edge. Do not collect water from the waterline at night. Freshwater crocodiles (*Crocodylus johnstoni*) are also present in many inland waterways and are aggressive when threatened.

## Darwin Region (Top End Coastal)

### Darwin (Capital City)

- **Nearest water:** Darwin Harbour (Beagle Gulf / Timor Sea) — salt water. Multiple tidal creeks (Rapid Creek, Ludmilla Creek, Buffalo Creek, Sadgroves Creek, Reichardt Creek, Woods Inlet) within the urban area.
- **Nearest freshwater source:** Adelaide River — ~65 km southeast via the Arnhem Highway. Darwin River Dam (Manton Dam) — ~70 km south, the city's primary water supply reservoir (not a natural lake — concrete dam, restricted access).
- **Coordinates:** -12.4381, 130.8411
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME:**
  - Darwin Harbour has saltwater crocodiles. ALL tidal creeks in Darwin are crocodile habitat. Rapid Creek, Buffalo Creek, and Ludmilla Creek are known crocodile zones. Do not enter any waterway in Darwin.
  - Beaches are NOT safe for swimming — saltwater crocodiles patrol the coast year-round.
  - Mindil Beach (tourist spot) is closed for swimming; crocodile sightings common.
  - Darwin River Dam — freshwater crocodiles possible; saltwater crocs possible in connected watercourses.
- **Water reliability:** Darwin has municipal water supply (Darwin River Dam, Howard East borefield). In SHTF scenario, Darwin River Dam is the only major surface freshwater reservoir within practical distance. Manton Dam (recreation lake) is an alternative. Multiple stormwater drains carry wet-season runoff but are contaminated.
- **Alternative sources:** Howard Springs Nature Park (~30 km south) — spring-fed pools; Marlow Lagoon (Palmerston); berry Springs (~45 km south); many suburban parks with artificial lakes (not reliable).

---

### Palmerston

- **Nearest water:** Same general area as Darwin — Elizabeth River and tributary creeks (Mitchell Creek, Hudson Creek) drain to Darwin Harbour. All are tidal and saline.
- **Nearest freshwater source:** Howard Springs (~20 km east). Darwin River Dam (~60 km south).
- **Coordinates:** -12.4792, 130.9897
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** Elizabeth River, Mitchell Creek, and all tidal creeks in the Palmerston area have saltwater crocodiles. Do not enter.
- **Water reliability:** Palmerston shares Darwin's municipal supply. Moulden Lake, Marlow Lagoon, and other urban lakes exist but cannot be counted on long-term — they are ornamental/stormwater retention.
- **Alternative sources:** Howard Springs; Berry Springs Nature Park (~40 km south); additional bore water may be available in the rural area (Howard Springs / Humpty Doo borefields).

---

### Humpty Doo

- **Nearest water:** Multiple tidal creeks drain the Humpty Doo area to the Adelaide River floodplain and Shoal Bay. Arnhem Highway crosses the Adelaide River ~15 km east at the iconic Adelaide River bridge (Jumping Crocodile tours).
- **Nearest freshwater source:** Adelaide River (above tidal limit) — ~30 km upstream. Manton Dam ~40 km south. Corroboree Billabong (~40 km east, but croc-dense).
- **Coordinates:** -12.5767, 131.1017
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The lower Adelaide River near Humpty Doo is one of the most crocodile-dense waterways in Australia. Jumping Crocodile tours operate here for a reason. Do NOT approach the water.
- **Water reliability:** Bore water available throughout the rural area (Howard East bore field). Manton Dam is the nearest reliable surface freshwater. In rural residential setting — many blocks have bores.
- **Alternative sources:** Fogg Dam (~10 km east) — conservation reserve with permanent wetlands; Harrison Dam.

---

### Batchelor

- **Nearest water:** Multiple small creeks in the Batchelor district drain toward the Finniss River / Adelaide River systems. No named major river in immediate vicinity.
- **Nearest named river:** Finniss River — ~15 km west. Adelaide River — ~20 km east (via township).
- **Nearest freshwater body:** Rum Jungle Lake (former uranium mine pit, now recreational lake) — ~3 km east. Litchfield National Park waterfalls (Wangi Falls, Florence Falls, Buley Rockhole) — ~15–30 km southwest.
- **Coordinates:** -13.0483, 131.0278
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — HIGH.** The Finniss River (lower reaches, north of Litchfield) has saltwater crocs. Litchfield NP swimming holes are monitored for crocs but freshwater crocs are possible. Wangi Falls has been closed for croc sightings in past seasons. Rum Jungle Lake — freshwater, but check signage.
- **Water reliability:** Batchelor has town water supply. Rum Jungle Lake is a permanent water body. Litchfield waterfalls are spring-fed and perennial. However, note that Litchfield's waterfalls can have reduced flow toward the end of the dry season. Florence Falls is the most reliable — spring-fed year-round.
- **Alternative sources:** Litchfield Park creeks; Adelaide River (above crossing at Adelaide River township). Buley Rockhole and Florence Falls are the best-known swimming and water collection spots.

---

### Adelaide River (Township)

- **Nearest water:** Adelaide River — the town sits directly on the Adelaide River, right at the Stuart Highway / old railway bridge crossing.
- **Coordinates:** -13.2381, 131.1064
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — HIGH.** The Adelaide River at the township bridge is generally above the primary tidal influence, BUT saltwater crocodiles have been documented upstream of the township. Freshwater crocodiles are present. Do not assume safety. Verify current conditions before approaching river edge.
- **Water reliability:** The Adelaide River at this point flows year-round in most years (fed by Litchfield escarpment runoff). Water level drops significantly in the late dry season. The river here is primarily freshwater though during wet season flood, it is turbid and fast-flowing. During extreme dry-season lows, it may reduce to pools.
- **Alternative sources:** Robin Falls (~15 km west toward Litchfield), Dorat Road.

### Jabiru (Kakadu)

- **Nearest water:** Jabiru lake (Jabiru Town Lake, constructed lake within the township — recreational, may have crocodiles, check). South Alligator River — ~25 km south (Arnhem Highway crossing). East Alligator River — ~30 km north (Ubirr / Cahills Crossing). Magela Creek — ~15 km east (Ranger uranium mine site area).
- **Coordinates:** -12.6714, 132.8319
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.**
  - South Alligator River — some of the highest crocodile densities in Kakadu. Yellow Water billabong, connected to the South Alligator, is croc-dense.
  - East Alligator River — Cahills Crossing: DOZENS of crocodiles visible at any time. Multiple human fatalities. Do NOT cross. Do NOT stand at water's edge at low tide.
  - Jabiru Town Lake — may hold freshwater crocs; check local signage.
  - All water bodies in Kakadu carry crocodile risk.
- **Water reliability:** Jabiru has town water supply (bore field). The lake is artificial. Yellow Water / South Alligator is perennial but croc-dense and difficult to access safely. Magela Creek has permanent billabongs in part (but Ranger mine proximity raises concerns about heavy metal contamination downstream — do not drink unfiltered water from Magela Creek).
- **Alternative sources:** Nourlangie Creek; Burdulba billabong. Barramundi Gorge (Maguk) has a permanent plunge pool (swimming often allowed in croc-free season — check). Gunlom Falls (seasonal — may be dry in late dry).

## Katherine Region

### Katherine

- **Nearest water:** Katherine River — the town of Katherine is built directly on the Katherine River. The Katherine River flows through the centre of town.
- **Coordinates:** -14.4653, 132.2634
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — MODERATE.** The Katherine River in town is generally above the saltwater crocodile zone, BUT saltwater crocodiles have been very occasionally reported upstream of the low-level bridge during extreme wet-season floods. Freshwater crocodiles are present and common throughout the Katherine River in town. They are generally not aggressive unless provoked, but do not swim outside designated areas. The Katherine Hot Springs (within town) is monitored and generally considered safe.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 **PERENNIAL — SPRING-FED.** The Katherine River is one of the most reliable rivers in the NT, with permanent flow year-round sustained by the Tindall Limestone Aquifer (spring discharge into Nitmiluk Gorge and along the river course). This makes Katherine arguably the most water-secure town in inland NT.
- **Alternative sources:** Katherine Hot Springs (33°C geothermal — but may require treatment if SHTF and source is compromised). Nitmiluk Gorge (boat ramp area). Many bores in the Katherine region tap the Tindall Aquifer.

---

### Pine Creek

- **Nearest water:** Pine Creek itself (ephemeral). Copperfield Creek — ~2 km from town (small waterholes). Mary River (upper reaches) — ~25 km northeast. Ferguson River — ~30 km south (Stuart Highway bridge).
- **Coordinates:** -13.8218, 131.8354
- **⚡ Ephemeral:** Pine Creek and local creeks may dry substantially in the dry season. The Mary River (upper Katherine area) has more reliable flow. Pine Creek relies on bore water for town supply.
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — MODERATE.** Freshwater crocodiles possible in Mary River upper reaches. The Mary below the escarpment has saltwater crocs — avoid.
- **Water reliability:** Pine Creek is mining country — old gold-mining dams and pits (e.g. Copperfield Dam, open-cut pits now acting as artificial lakes). These are permanent. Mary River (above the escarpment) has some perennial flow from springs.
- **Alternative sources:** Umbrawarra Gorge (~15 km east) — permanent pools; Spring Hill; Enterprise Pit Lookout area; old mine dams.

---

### Mataranka

- **Nearest water:** Waterhouse River and the Roper River headwaters — Mataranka is effectively AT the source of the Roper system. The Mataranka Thermal Pool and Bitter Springs (Rainbow Spring) are artesian springs feeding the Waterhouse River, which joins Roper Creek to form the Roper River less than 10 km from town.
- **Coordinates:** -14.9275, 133.0683
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — LOW but not zero.** Freshwater crocodiles are present in the Waterhouse River and Roper River. The thermal pool and Bitter Springs are monitored swimming areas. Saltwater crocodiles have been reported in the Roper below Roper Bar (well downstream), but during extreme wet-season floods, they can move further upstream. Verify.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 **PERENNIAL — ARTESIAN SPRING-FED.** The Mataranka thermal springs are among the most reliable water sources in the NT — 33°C crystal-clear water flowing at 30+ million litres per day from the Great Artesian Basin / Daly Basin aquifers. This is a premier SHTF water asset. Flow is constant year-round, unaffected by drought or seasonal surface conditions. However, the water emerges at 33°C — cooling may be needed for drinking.
- **Alternative sources:** Roper River upstream of the springs (fresh); Elsey Creek; Elsey Homestead bore.

---

### Larrimah

- **Nearest water:** Birdum Creek — small creek near Larrimah. Larrimah relies on bore water. Roper Creek — ~15 km south (Mataranka direction). Elsey Creek — ~20 km south.
- **Coordinates:** -15.5747, 133.2173
- **⚡ Ephemeral:** Larrimah is on the Stuart Highway in semi-arid Gulf Savannah transition zone. Surface water is unreliable for most of the dry season. Bore water is primary.
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — LOW.** Freshwater crocs possible in permanent waterholes in the Roper system to the south but unlikely near Larrimah itself.
- **Water reliability:** Low for surface water. The town's bore is the primary water source. Airstrip. The Larrimah Wayside Inn (Pink Panther Hotel) historically had its own bore.
- **Alternative sources:** Re-supply from Mataranka (30 km south) is practical. The rail corridor may have bore-equipped sidings.

## Gulf Country

### Borroloola

- **Nearest water:** McArthur River — Borroloola is located right on the McArthur River, approximately 50 km upstream from the river mouth. The town is accessed via the Carpentaria Highway crossing of the McArthur River. King Ash Bay (fishing village) is at the river mouth.
- **Coordinates:** -16.0658, 136.3083
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The McArthur River at Borroloola is well within the saltwater crocodile zone. Tidal influence extends past Borroloola. Saltwater crocodiles are abundant and large specimens are common. Do NOT enter the river. Do NOT camp at the river bank.
- **Water reliability:** The McArthur River flows year-round in its main channel, though flow reduces in the late dry season. The river is tidal at Borroloola, so water may be brackish depending on tide and season. Upstream of Borroloola, freshwater can be found above the tidal limit (~30–50 km upstream). Bore water is the town supply.
- **Alternative sources:** King Ash Bay (boat access, also croc-dense). Robinson River (north — separate system). Numerous small creeks. The mine area has bores.

---

### McArthur River Mine Area

- **Nearest water:** McArthur River — the mine is located on the McArthur River, approximately 45 km upstream from Borroloola. The mine site is on the middle reaches of the McArthur.
- **Coordinates:** -16.4344, 136.1584
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — HIGH.** The McArthur River at the mine is above the primary tidal zone but saltwater crocodiles are present through much of the river. Do NOT approach the water.
- **Water reliability:** Bores and mine water infrastructure on site. The McArthur River here is above the tidal limit — potentially freshwater access but with mine-related contamination risk (lead, zinc, cadmium leaching from the ore body). Do NOT drink untreated water from the McArthur near the mine. Bores on site are the only safe source.
- **Alternative sources:** Relocate to Borroloola or to upstream river sections well away from the mine influence.

---

### Numbulwar

- **Nearest water:** Gulf of Carpentaria coast. Rose River — ~15 km north (small creek). The Roper River mouth is ~20 km to the west.
- **Coordinates:** -14.2800, 135.7300
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** Saltwater crocodiles are present along the entire Gulf coast around Numbulwar. The Roper River mouth area has extreme crocodile density. Do NOT enter the water. Be extremely careful on beaches — crocs patrol the shoreline.
- **Water reliability:** Numbulwar (Aboriginal community, access by permit) relies on a bore for town water supply. Surface water in the immediate area is limited — small creeks are ephemeral. The Roper River is the nearest major permanent water but access to the Roper mouth area is difficult. Rainwater tanks common in the settlement.
- **Alternative sources:** Bore water only. Rainwater capture during wet season.

---

### Ngukurr

- **Nearest water:** Roper River — Ngukurr is situated ON the Roper River, approximately 120 km upstream from the mouth. The town sits right on the riverbank at the historic Roper River Mission site.
- **Coordinates:** -14.7331, 134.7333
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** Ngukurr is within the tidally-influenced and crocodile-active zone of the Roper River. Saltwater crocodiles are present. Do NOT enter or approach the river. Multiple community warnings.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 The Roper River at Ngukurr has year-round flow (downstream of the Mataranka spring-fed headwaters). However, water may be brackish depending on seasonal flows and tidal influence. The town has bores. Upstream from Ngukurr, above the tidal limit, the river is freshwater. This is a strategic SHTF water corridor.
- **Alternative sources:** Upstream Roper River (above tidal limit — ~50+ km upstream). Hodgson River confluence. Bore water in community.

---

### Maningrida

- **Nearest water:** Liverpool River — Maningrida is situated right at the mouth of the Liverpool River on the Arafura Sea coast. The Blyth River enters the sea just east of town, in Boucaut Bay. Both rivers are accessible from Maningrida.
- **Coordinates:** -12.0511, 134.2258
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** Both the Liverpool and Blyth River mouths have dense saltwater crocodile populations. The coastal zone around Maningrida is prime crocodile habitat. Do NOT enter water.
- **Water reliability:** The Liverpool River at Maningrida is tidal and saline, but upstream reaches (accessible by boat) have freshwater. The town has bores. The escarpment reaches of both rivers have perennial pools and springs. Rainwater tanks are common. The Liverpool River above the tidal limit is a year-round freshwater source.
- **Alternative sources:** Blyth River upstream (freshwater). Escarpment springs. Community bore water.

---

### Gunbalanya (Oenpelli)

- **Nearest water:** East Alligator River — Gunbalanya sits just across the East Alligator River from Ubirr (Kakadu). The town is on the Arnhem Land side, accessed via Cahills Crossing (which crosses the East Alligator). The crossing itself is the direct water source — the East Alligator River passes by the town.
- **Coordinates:** -12.3229, 133.0528
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The East Alligator at Cahills Crossing is one of Australia's most infamous crocodile hotspots. Multiple deaths. Dozens of crocodiles visible at any low tide. Do NOT cross on foot, do NOT fish from the crossing at high density. Do NOT collect water at the crossing during low tide when crocs aggregate.
- **Water reliability:** The East Alligator River above the tidal limit (freshwater section) is perennial due to escarpment springs. Gunbalanya has bores and rainwater tanks. The freshwater billabongs in Arnhem Land behind the escarpment are reliable.
- **Alternative sources:** Inland Arnhem Land billabongs and springs (permit required); Magela Creek (upstream of Ranger mine).

## Victoria River District & Western NT

### Timber Creek

- **Nearest water:** Victoria River — Timber Creek is situated directly ON the Victoria River. The town has a boat ramp and is the primary access point for boating on the Victoria River. The river is tidal at Timber Creek.
- **Coordinates:** -15.6400, 130.4731
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The Victoria River at Timber Creek is well within the saltwater crocodile zone. Tidal influence, large crocodiles frequently observed. BOAT RAMP area has warning signs. Do NOT enter the water. Do NOT camp within 5m of the riverbank.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 The Victoria River at Timber Creek has perennial water, but the water is tidal (can be brackish to saline depending on season and tide). Upstream freshwater is accessible by boat — approximately 20+ km upstream the river transitions to freshwater (above the tidal limit). Timber Creek itself has a bore. During the dry season, the flow reduces but the channel remains filled. During the wet, flooding is catastrophic and the town can be isolated.
- **Alternative sources:** Baines River (confluence ~30 km upstream) — freshwater in upper reaches. Gregory National Park waterholes. Keep River National Park gorge waterholes (~70 km west toward WA border).

---

### Victoria River Downs (VRD)

- **Nearest water:** Victoria River — VRD station is on the Victoria River (mid-upper reaches, well above the tidal limit). The station is one of the largest cattle stations in NT history. Access is via the Buchanan Highway (unsealed).
- **Coordinates:** -16.4039, 131.0163
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — MODERATE.** VRD is far above the tidal limit. Freshwater crocodiles are present. Saltwater crocodiles RARELY move this far upstream except during extreme wet-season flooding. Generally the river here is considered freshwater with freshwater croc risk.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 The Victoria River at VRD is perennial (major waterhole system) but flow during the dry season is low — the river becomes a series of long, deep waterholes rather than a flowing stream. These are permanent and a critical dry-season refuge. Bores throughout the station.
- **Alternative sources:** Wickham River (tributary) — perennial waterholes in its lower reaches. Camfield River waterholes. Multiple station bores.

---

### Kalkarindji / Daguragu

- **Nearest water:** Victoria River (upper headwaters) — Kalkarindji and Daguragu are twin communities situated near the headwaters of the Victoria River, approximately 100 km upstream from VRD. The Victoria River here is a smaller stream that can dry to disconnected pools in extreme conditions. Hooker Creek is also nearby (separate drainage to the south).
- **Coordinates:** Kombongkarrinji (Kalkarindji): -17.4444, 130.8283; Daguragu: -17.4111, 130.8083
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — LOW.** This far inland and at the headwaters, crocodile risk is minimal. Freshwater crocodiles theoretically possible in permanent waterholes but unlikely at this elevation/distance from the coast.
- **Water reliability:** ⚡ **NOT GUARANTEED — EPHEMERAL UPPER REACHES.** The Victoria River headwaters near Kalkarindji are not perennial. In dry years, the upper Victoria River can completely stop flowing. Waterholes may persist but cannot be counted on long-term without verification. The communities have bores. Hooker Creek waterholes may persist in some seasons.
- **Alternative sources:** Deep waterholes on the upper Victoria (verify); community bores; Daguragu artesian bore. Re-supply from VRD (100 km north) is practical for a motorised group.

---

### Wadeye (Port Keats)

- **Nearest water:** The coast of the Timor Sea. Fitzmaurice River — ~20 km south. The Fitzmaurice is the nearest major river but does not pass through Wadeye. Wadeye is situated on the coast at Port Keats, with several small tidal creeks in the immediate area. No named river flows directly through the community.
- **Coordinates:** -14.2336, 129.5258
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The entire coast around Wadeye is active saltwater crocodile habitat. The Fitzmaurice River mouth and all coastal creeks have crocodiles. Wadeye beaches are dangerous.
- **Water reliability:** Wadeye relies on a bore water supply. Surface freshwater is limited in the immediate area — no major perennial river flows through the community. The Fitzmaurice River is the best nearby freshwater option but requires overland travel (~20 km south). Small freshwater springs and billabongs may exist in the hinterland. Rainwater capture is essential in wet season.
- **Alternative sources:** Fitzmaurice River (freshwater above tidal limit); community bores; hinterland springs. Consider relocating toward the Daly River community (~150 km northeast along the coast) for better surface water.

---

### Daly River Community (Nauiyu)

- **Nearest water:** Daly River — the community is situated directly ON the Daly River, at the site of the historic Daly River Police Station and crossing. The Daly River here is tidal-affected but primarily fresh depending on season and tides.
- **Coordinates:** -13.7614, 130.7000
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** The Daly River at the community/ crossing is one of the most crocodile-dense rivers in the NT. The tidal influence brings very large saltwater crocodiles upstream past the crossing. Multiple human fatalities over decades. Do NOT enter the river. The Daly River Barra Classic fishing competition operates here — extreme caution protocols in place.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 The Daly River at the community flows year-round due to the spring-fed Katherine/Flora contributions. However, water quality varies — tidal influence can bring saline water upstream in late dry season (low-flow periods). During the wet, the river is heavily turbid and floods dramatically (major flood events 2018, 2023). In a SHTF scenario, collect water from upstream of the community or from the Katherine River system. The community has bores.
- **Alternative sources:** Tributary creeks above tidal influence; upstream Daly River; relocate toward Katherine (150 km southeast by road, but roads flood-prone).

---

### Peppimenarti

- **Nearest water:** Daly River — Peppimenarti is situated on the Daly River, approximately 80 km upstream from the Daly River community (Nauiyu). This is well above the tidal limit. The river here is freshwater year-round.
- **Coordinates:** -14.1476, 130.0824
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — HIGH.** Freshwater crocodiles abundant. Saltwater crocs have been recorded this far upstream during extreme dry-season low-flow and wet-season floods. Exercise caution. The river here is generally considered freshwater but saltwater crocs are not impossible.
- **Water reliability:** 🔵💧 The Daly River at Peppimenarti is perennial and fresh — spring-fed base from the Katherine/Flora system ensures flow year-round. This is one of the most water-secure locations in the Victoria River / Daly region. The community also has bores.
- **Alternative sources:** The main Daly River channel is the primary source. Small tributary creeks. Groundwater from bores.

## Central Australia (Arid Zone — Alice Springs & Surrounds)

> **NOTE:** Surface water in Central Australia is almost entirely ephemeral. Rivers are dry sand beds for 95% of the time. Desert water knowledge is a survival prerequisite. Bores, springs, and tanks are the practical water infrastructure. The Todd River flowing is a major event (maybe 1–3 times per year). Waterholes in the MacDonnell Ranges offer the only semi-reliable surface water — verify each one by name.

---

### Alice Springs

- **Nearest water:** Todd River (ephemeral) — usually a dry sandy bed through the centre of town. Flows only after significant rainfall — typically 1–3 events per year lasting hours to a few days. Charles River (ephemeral).
- **Nearest reliable surface water:** Waterholes in the MacDonnell Ranges:
  - **Simpsons Gap** (~18 km west) — permanent waterhole
  - **Standley Chasm** (~45 km west) — semi-permanent waterhole
  - **Ellery Creek Big Hole** (~95 km west) — permanent deep waterhole
  - **Ormiston Gorge** (~135 km west) — permanent waterhole
  - **Glen Helen Gorge** (~132 km west) — permanent waterhole (Finke River)
  - **Trephina Gorge** (~85 km east) — permanent waterhole
- **Coordinates:** -23.7000, 133.8800
- **🐊 Crocodile warning:** NO crocodiles in Central Australia. No warning applies. Waterholes are considered safe from crocodile threat.
- **Water reliability:** Alice Springs is on the Mereenie Aquifer (Amadeus Basin) — the town supply is bore water. This is the ONLY reliable water source in town. The Todd River is dry >95% of the time. Even when it flows, it is a flash-flood event (runs for hours to ~2 days, then dries again). You cannot count on the Todd River for any purpose.
- **Alternative sources:** Telegraph Station waterhole (semi-permanent at Historical Reserve). Alice Springs bore fields. MacDonnell Ranges waterholes (see above, reliable in dry season). Rainwater tanks (wet season, Dec–Mar, unreliable).

---

### Tennant Creek

- **Nearest water:** Tennant Creek (ephemeral) — flows only in rare heavy rainfall events. The creek is usually dry. Mary Ann Dam (~5 km north of town) — constructed recreational lake, the town's main surface water feature. Permanent.
- **Coordinates:** -19.6478, 134.1897
- **🐊 Crocodile warning:** No crocodiles at Tennant Creek. The region is 500+ km south of crocodile habitat.
- **Water reliability:** The town water supply comes from bores (Tennant Creek bore field). Mary Ann Dam is a permanent artificial lake used for recreation and as emergency water storage, but quality may degrade without treatment. No natural perennial water exists in the area. Creeks are dry. Reliable water means bore only.
- **Alternative sources:** Attack Creek (ephemeral — same pattern). Heading north to Elliott, waterholes are equally unreliable but bores exist at roadhouses. Barkly Tableland bores are the infrastructure backbone.

---

### Nhulunbuy (Gove Peninsula)

- **Nearest water:** The Gove Peninsula is bounded by the Arafura Sea on one side and the Gulf of Carpentaria on the other (salt water). No major river flows through Nhulunbuy. The nearest significant creek is **Melville Creek** (~5 km from town), which drains to Melville Bay. Several small creeks (Wirrawuy Creek, Yirrkala Creek) drain the Gove Peninsula coastal strip.
- **Nearest major river:** Goyder River — ~80 km southwest (Arnhem Land interior, permit required). Walker River — ~100 km south.
- **Coordinates:** -12.1814, 136.7828
- **🐊 Crocodile warning — EXTREME.** So, saltwater crocodiles are present around the entire Gove Peninsula coastline. Nhulunbuy beaches and coastal creeks are crocodile habitat. Melville Bay, the town harbour area, has croc sightings. Do not swim at beaches. Do not enter coastal creeks.
- **Water reliability:** Nhulunbuy has a town bore water supply. No perennial surface freshwater was available in immediate proximity. The nearest reliable surface freshwater is 80+ km into Arnhem Land (Goyder River escarpment reaches). Rainwater capture is common in the region. Nhulunbuy Water Treatment Plant draws from a bore field (Yirrkala bore field area).
- **Alternative sources:** Yirrkala bore field. Rainwater tanks at most homes (tropical rainfall, reliable wet season). Goyder River (requires overland travel into Arnhem Land — permits required, extreme croc risk in lower sections).

---

### Yuendumu

- **Nearest water:** Yuendumu is situated in the Tanami Desert. No surface rivers. Relies on bores — the community draws from the Tanami Aquifer / bores.
- **Nearest surface water feature:** Bore-fed community water point. There is no named perennial surface water feature within 100 km.
- **Coordinates:** -22.2588, 131.7941
- **🐊 Crocodile warning:** No crocodiles. 800+ km south of crocodile habitat.
- **Water reliability:** Bore only. Yuendumu is entirely dependent on the community bore for water. The bore is reliable, drawing from groundwater in the Tanami. Surface water does not exist here for practical purposes.
- **Alternative sources:** No alternative surface water. The community bore is it. Plan accordingly.

### Yulara (Uluru / Ayers Rock Resort)

- **Nearest water:** Yulara is in the Central Desert — no natural surface water. The nearest is Yulara's own bore water supply — the resort is entirely dependent on bores (drawn from the Amadeus Basin / Ayers Rock groundwater).
- **Nearest named surface feature:** Mutitjulu Waterhole (at the base of Uluru) — ~18 km west. A semi-permanent waterhole in a shaded gorge at the base of Uluru. Fills during rain events and persists for weeks to months depending on rainfall. NOT reliable year-round.
- **Coordinates:** -25.2417, 130.9889
- **🐊 Crocodile warning:** None. 1,400+ km south of crocodile habitat.
- **Water reliability:** Bore only. Yulara operates a desalination plant (treating brackish groundwater) — this is the town's sole water supply. Mutitjulu Waterhole is culturally significant but cannot sustain a population. Do not approach without Anangu traditional owner permission — it is a sacred site.
- **Alternative sources:** Curtain Springs Roadhouse (~100 km east) — bore. Erldunda Roadhouse (~260 km southeast) — bore.

- **🐊 Crocodile warning:** None.
- **Water reliability:** The Mutitjulu community has a bore water supply. The waterhole itself is not reliable year-round — it requires recent rain. In extended dry periods, the waterhole can dry completely.

### Hermannsburg (Ntaria)

  - Finke Gorge National Park waterholes

Palm Valley (25 km, 4WD track) has perennial water from sandstone springs — this is the only natural perennial surface water within accessible distance. Elsewhere in the area, bores are the only water.

### Kings Canyon (Watarrka National Park)

- **Nearest water:** Kings Creek — flows through Watarrka (Kings Canyon). ## SHTF Water Collection Guidelines — NT Context

### Universal Rules

1. **All surface water must be purified** — boiling, filtration, or chemical treatment (chlorine/iodine). This applies universally across the NT. Livestock, wildlife, and upstream human activity contaminate ALL water.
2. **Assume crocodiles at all water bodies north of Katherine.** This is the single most lethal water-related threat in NT.
3. **Bores are the most reliable infrastructure.** In SHTF, know bore locations. Many NT towns and roadhouses have accessible bores.
4. **Spring-fed rivers (Katherine, Daly above tidal, Mataranka, Victoria gorge pools) are the best natural surface water in NT.** They persist through all but the most extreme droughts.
5. **Central Australia: no surface water.** You are entirely dependent on bores, tanks, and cached water. Plan desert travel with a MINIMUM of 10 litres per person per day.

### Croc-Safe Water Collection Protocol (Top End)

1. Approach water during daylight hours ONLY (never dawn, dusk, or night).
2. Have a spotter — one person watches the water at all times while another collects.
3. Use a pole, bucket on rope, or pump — NEVER wade in.
4. Stay at LEAST 5 metres from the water's edge.
5. Avoid areas with floating vegetation, muddy banks, slide marks — these indicate crocodile presence.
6. Never return to the same collection point at the same time of day repeatedly — crocodiles learn patterns.
7. After heavy rain (wet season), crocodiles move — assume any new water body may have a crocodile.

### Worst-Case Scenarios

- **Stranded in Central Australia without a bore:** Head to the MacDonnell Ranges waterholes (Ellery Creek, Ormiston, Glen Helen). They persist longer than any other surface water. Palm Valley (Hermannsburg area) has the most reliable spring water. Kings Canyon (Garden of Eden) is perennial. Uluru area: Mutitjulu Waterhole is the only natural option but NOT reliable — must have rain.
- **Stranded Top End wet season with flooded roads:** Very dangerous. You will NOT lack water (water everywhere) but crocodiles will be on the move as floodwaters spread. Stay on HIGH ground. Roads will be impassable for weeks. Do not attempt crossings.
- **Stranded Top End late dry season:** Rivers drying. Target spring-fed systems (Katherine River, Mataranka springs, Flora River). Avoid tidal sections — they are saline and croc-dense.
- **Kununurra (WA, but near NT border):** Lake Argyle (massive reservoir, WA's largest). Ord River dam — perennial water. Keep River National Park (NT, permanent gorge waterholes). Victoria River (Timber Creek area) is the nearest NT perennial water.
