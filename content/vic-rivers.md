# Victoria — Major River Systems

> **SHTF Reference**: Comprehensive guide to Victoria's river systems, dams, and waterways. Organized by region. Know your water.

---

## 1. Murray River (Border Section)

- **Length**: ~2,508 km total; ~1,800 km forms Victoria–NSW border
- **Source**: Australian Alps (NSW, near Mount Kosciuszko)
- **Mouth**: Murray Mouth, South Australia (Southern Ocean)
- **Key Towns (VIC side)**: Wodonga, Yarrawonga, Echuca, Swan Hill, Mildura, Robinvale, Kerang (nearby)
- **Major Dams/Weirs**: Hume Dam (Albury-Wodonga), Yarrawonga Weir (Lake Mulwala), Torrumbarry Weir, Lock 11 (Mildura Weir), Lake Victoria (SA)
- **Features**: Australia's longest river system (Murray-Darling). Vital for irrigation (Murray-Darling Basin). Heavily regulated with locks, weirs, and irrigation channels. Paddle steamers historically operated between Echuca and the mouth. Suffers from overallocation of water rights. Major flood events: 1956, 1974, 2011, 2022.

---

## 2. Goulburn River

- **Length**: ~654 km
- **Source**: Corn Hill area, near Woods Point (Victorian Alps)
- **Mouth**: Murray River, near Echuca
- **Key Towns**: Alexandra, Yea, Seymour, Nagambie, Shepparton, Murchison
- **Major Dams**: Lake Eildon (Eildon Dam), Goulburn Weir (Nagambie), Waranga Basin (off-stream storage)
- **Features**: Victoria's longest river entirely within the state. The Goulburn-Broken catchment provides a major portion of the Murray-Darling Basin's water. Lake Eildon is one of Victoria's largest reservoirs (3,334 GL capacity). Major irrigation source for the Goulburn Valley (orchards, dairy). Tributaries include the Rubicon, Delatite, Howqua, and Acheron rivers.

---

## 3. Broken River

- **Length**: ~225 km
- **Source**: Wellington Plateau (near Mount Wellington, Victorian Alps)
- **Mouth**: Goulburn River, near Shepparton
- **Key Towns**: Benalla, Nalinga
- **Major Dams**: Lake Nillahcootie
- **Features**: Major Goulburn tributary. Flows through Benalla where it created Lake Benalla. Lake Nillahcootie (40 GL) supplies irrigation and urban water. Subject to flooding — Benalla has extensive levee systems.

---

## 4. Campaspe River

- **Length**: ~232 km
- **Source**: Great Dividing Range, near Woodend
- **Mouth**: Murray River, near Echuca
- **Key Towns**: Kyneton, Elmore, Rochester
- **Major Dams**: Lake Eppalock
- **Features**: Rises on the northern slopes of the Great Dividing Range. Lake Eppalock (304 GL) provides irrigation supply and flood mitigation. Flows through goldfields country (Kyneton, Castlemaine area). The Campaspe irrigates pastures and crops in its lower reaches.

---

## 5. Loddon River

- **Length**: ~361 km
- **Source**: Great Dividing Range, near Glenlyon
- **Mouth**: Murray River, near Kerang
- **Key Towns**: Daylesford (near source), Newstead, Bridgewater, Serpentine, Boort, Kerang
- **Major Dams**: Cairn Curran Reservoir, Tullaroop Reservoir, Laanecoorie Reservoir
- **Features**: Key water supply for central and northern Victoria. Cairn Curran (147 GL) and Tullaroop (72 GL) reservoirs. The Loddon system is supplemented by the Goulburn-Murray irrigation network. Supports grazing, cropping, and dairy. Tributaries include the Bet Bet and Tullaroop Creek.

---

## 6. Avoca River

- **Length**: ~270 km
- **Source**: Pyrenees Ranges, near Amphitheatre
- **Mouth**: Lake Bael Bael / Kerang Lakes system (terminal — doesn't reach the Murray directly)
- **Key Towns**: Avoca, St Arnaud, Charlton
- **Major Dams**: No major on-stream dam — natural catchment
- **Features**: One of the few major rivers in Victoria without a large dam. Terminal river — ends in the Kerang Lakes wetlands system (ephemerally connects to the Murray during floods). Highly seasonal (can dry up in drought). The Avoca River basin is part of the Murray-Darling Basin. Important for environmental flows and wetlands.

---

## 7. Wimmera River

- **Length**: ~278 km
- **Source**: Pyrenees Ranges, near Elmhurst
- **Mouth**: Lake Hindmarsh / Lake Albacutya (terminal — endorheic basin)
- **Key Towns**: Stawell (nearby), Horsham, Dimboola, Jeparit
- **Major Dams**: Lake Lonsdale, Lake Wartook, Lake Bellfield, Rocklands Reservoir, Toolondo Reservoir (Wimmera-Mallee headworks system)
- **Features**: Terminal river — flows inland to the Wimmera lakes, not to the sea. The Wimmera-Mallee pipeline replaced open irrigation channels (completed 2010, massive water savings). Rocklands Reservoir (260 GL) is the main storage. Flows through Horsham, the Wimmera's agricultural hub. Lake Hindmarsh is Victoria's largest natural freshwater lake (when full). Threatened by dryland salinity and reduced flows.

---

## 8. Yarra River

- **Length**: ~242 km
- **Source**: Yarra Ranges, near Mount Baw Baw (specifically, the flanks of Mount Gregory in the Yarra Ranges National Park)
- **Mouth**: Port Phillip Bay, at Newport (Melbourne)
- **Key Towns**: Warburton, Yarra Junction, Launching Place, Wonga Park, Warrandyte, Templestowe, Heidelberg, Abbotsford, Richmond, Melbourne CBD, Southbank, Docklands
- **Major Dams**: Upper Yarra Dam, O'Shannassy Reservoir, Maroondah Reservoir, Silvan Reservoir (off-river, via aqueduct), Sugarloaf Reservoir (pumped from Yarra tributaries)
- **Features**: Melbourne's iconic river. Provides ~70% of Melbourne's drinking water via the closed catchments in the upper Yarra. The lower Yarra through Melbourne CBD is tidal up to Dights Falls, Abbotsford. Major tributaries: Plenty River, Merri Creek, Darebin Creek, Moonee Ponds Creek, Mullum Mullum Creek, Diamond Creek, Olinda Creek, Watts River, Little Yarra River. Wine region (Yarra Valley). Popular for rowing, kayaking, cycling (Main Yarra Trail). Lower reaches historically industrial; extensive rejuvenation (Southbank, Docklands, Birrarung Marr).

---

## 9. Maribyrnong River

- **Length**: ~60 km (from confluence of Deep Creek and Jacksons Creek); ~170 km including tributary headwaters
- **Source**: Macedon Ranges (tributaries: Deep Creek rises near Lancefield, Jacksons Creek near Gisborne)
- **Mouth**: Yarra River, at Footscray (tidal — forms Port of Melbourne at its lower end)
- **Key Towns**: Keilor, Avondale Heights, Maribyrnong, Essendon, Footscray
- **Major Dams**: No major dam — Rosslynne Reservoir nearby (Jacksons Creek catchment)
- **Features**: Flows through Melbourne's western suburbs. Major tributaries: Deep Creek, Jacksons Creek, Steele Creek, Taylors Creek. Lower reaches are tidal and navigable — historically used for shipping wool, tallow. The Maribyrnong River valley was an important Indigenous meeting place. Brimbank Park is a major recreational corridor. Suffers from industrial pollution legacy.

---

## 10. Werribee River

- **Length**: ~110 km
- **Source**: Wombat State Forest, near Ballan
- **Mouth**: Port Phillip Bay, at Werribee South
- **Key Towns**: Bacchus Marsh, Werribee
- **Major Dams**: Melton Reservoir, Pykes Creek Reservoir
- **Features**: Flows east through Bacchus Marsh (market gardens, fruit orchards). Lower reaches pass the Western Treatment Plant (Melbourne Water sewage treatment — largest in Australia, and a world-renowned birdwatching site — RAMSAR listed). The Werribee Gorge (near Bacchus Marsh) is a popular hiking spot. Irrigation and market garden supply for Melbourne's west. Mouth is near the Werribee South beach and marina.

---

## 11. Patterson River

- **Length**: ~5 km (canalised); historically the Dandenong Creek/Eumemmerring Creek system
- **Source**: Dandenong Creek (rises in Dandenong Ranges)
- **Mouth**: Port Phillip Bay, at Carrum
- **Key Towns**: Carrum Downs, Patterson Lakes, Carrum
- **Major Dams**: No major dam
- **Features**: Artificial cut completed 1876 — originally the Carrum Swamp was drained and a channel cut direct to the bay. Now a popular boating, fishing, and waterski waterway. Major floodway for the Dandenong Creek catchment. Patterson Lakes is a residential canal estate. Connected to the Port Phillip Bay via the Patterson River boat ramp and lock gates.

---

## 12. Bunyip River

- **Length**: ~27 km
- **Source**: Dandenong Ranges / Bunyip State Park
- **Mouth**: Western Port Bay (via Bunyip Main Drain, near Koo Wee Rup)
- **Key Towns**: Bunyip, Koo Wee Rup
- **Major Dams**: No major dam
- **Features**: Rises in the Bunyip State Park. Flows south across the Koo Wee Rup swamp (historically a massive tea-tree swamp, drained for agriculture). Now heavily canalised as part of the Koo Wee Rup drainage scheme — Australia's largest man-made drainage system. Prime agricultural land (asparagus, potatoes, dairy). The mouth is at Western Port, near the Lang Lang coast.

---

## 13. Latrobe River

- **Length**: ~239 km
- **Source**: Baw Baw plateau, near Noojee (South Gippsland highlands)
- **Mouth**: Lake Wellington → Gippsland Lakes → Bass Strait
- **Key Towns**: Willow Grove, Moe, Yallourn North, Traralgon, Rosedale
- **Major Dams**: Blue Rock Dam, Moondarra Reservoir, Lake Narracan
- **Features**: Key water source for Gippsland power generation (coal-fired plants at Yallourn, Loy Yang). Blue Rock Dam (208 GL) supplies cooling water and urban drinking. Flows into Lake Wellington (westernmost of the Gippsland Lakes). Major tributaries: Tanjil River, Tyers River, Morwell River, Thomson River (joins the Latrobe just above Sale). Heavily impacted by power generation, irrigation, and drought. Environmental flow restoration ongoing.

---

## 14. Thomson River

- **Length**: ~170 km
- **Source**: Baw Baw Plateau, near Mount St Phillack
- **Mouth**: Latrobe River, near Sale
- **Key Towns**: Walhalla (historical — near headwaters), Cowwarr, Heyfield
- **Major Dams**: Thomson Dam (Thomson Reservoir — 1,123 GL, Victoria's largest water storage)
- **Features**: The Thomson Dam is Melbourne's largest water storage — it was built in the 1980s and diverts water via tunnel to the Upper Yarra system for Melbourne. At 1,123 GL, it accounts for ~60% of Melbourne's total storage capacity. The river itself flows south from the Baw Baw Plateau through rugged country to join the Latrobe. The Thomson-Aberfeldy area is famous for gold mining history (Walhalla). A major environmental contention: the Thomson diversion reduced flows to the Gippsland Lakes.

---

## 15. Mitchell River

- **Length**: ~121 km
- **Source**: Great Dividing Range, near Omeo (Cobungra/Wonnangatta River junction forms the Mitchell)
- **Mouth**: Lake King → Gippsland Lakes → Bass Strait (at Mitchell River Silt Jetties, near Eagle Point)
- **Key Towns**: Bairnsdale, Mitchell, Lindenow, Bairnsdale environs
- **Major Dams**: No major dam — the Mitchell is the largest unregulated river in Victoria (no dams or weirs on main stem)
- **Features**: One of Victoria's last remaining free-flowing major rivers. Famous for the Mitchell River Silt Jetties — a rare geological formation over 8 km long where silt deposits extend into Lake King (second longest in the world). The river cuts the spectacular Mitchell River Gorge (national park). Important for fishing (bream, estuary perch) and agriculture (river flats — vegetables, grapes). Runs through the fertile river flats around Lindenow and Bairnsdale. Part of the Gippsland Lakes catchment.

---

## 16. Tambo River

- **Length**: ~186 km
- **Source:** Bowen Mountains, near Ensay (East Gippsland highlands)
- **Mouth**: Lake King → Gippsland Lakes → Bass Strait (near Metung)
- **Key Towns**: Ensay, Tambo Crossing, Bruthen, Swan Reach, Johnsonville
- **Major Dams**: No major dam
- **Features**: East Gippsland river. Flows through pastoral and forested country. The Tambo joins the Gippsland Lakes at Lake King near Metung — the famous Tambo Bluff marks the confluence. Important for agriculture (grazing, some cropping) and tourism (fishing, boating). The Great Alpine Road follows sections of the Tambo Valley.

---

## 17. Snowy River

- **Length**: ~352 km total (VIC section ~100 km)
- **Source**: Australian Alps (NSW, Mount Kosciuszko — the Snowy Mountains)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait, at Marlo (VIC) — flows from NSW through the Snowy River National Park into Victoria
- **Key Towns (VIC)**: Orbost, Marlo
- **Major Dams**: Lake Jindabyne (NSW) — Snowy Mountains Hydro-Electric Scheme; in VIC largely free-flowing
- **Features**: Iconic Australian river — made famous by "The Man from Snowy River". Heavily impacted by the Snowy Scheme — 99% of headwaters diverted to the Murray and Murrumbidgee via tunnels. Since 2006, environmental flows have been restored (target: 28% of original flow). The Snowy River National Park (VIC) contains deep gorges and old-growth forest. The lower Snowy (Orbost to Marlo) is estuary country — fishing, agriculture, tourism. Princess Bridge (Orbost) is a major crossing. The river mouth at Marlo is a popular holiday spot.

---

## 18. Ovens River

- **Length**: ~191 km
- **Source**: Victorian Alps, near Mount Feathertop
- **Mouth**: Murray River, near Bundalong (Lake Mulwala)
- **Key Towns**: Harrietville, Bright, Myrtleford, Wangaratta, Peechelba
- **Major Dams**: Lake Buffalo, Rocky Valley Dam (tributary — East Kiewa River catchment, adjacent via Kiewa Hydro Scheme)
- **Features**: Renowned as one of Victoria's healthiest rivers — relatively unregulated, clean, clear water flowing from the Alpine National Park. The Ovens Valley is prime agricultural land: tobacco (historically), hops, grapes (wine — King Valley, Alpine Valleys wine region), chestnuts, berries, cattle. Three major tributaries: Buckland River, King River, Buffalo River (flows into Lake Buffalo). Flows through Wangaratta where it forms the King River confluence. Floods are common but water quality is high. Popular for fishing, kayaking, and the Murray to Mountains Rail Trail.

---

## 19. King River

- **Length**: ~126 km
- **Source**: Alpine National Park, near Mount Cobbler
- **Mouth**: Ovens River, at Wangaratta
- **Key Towns**: Whitfield, Moyhu, Wangaratta
- **Major Dams**: No major dam (the King River is subject to a small hydro power station at Whitfield)
- **Features**: Major Ovens tributary. Flows through the King Valley — a renowned cool-climate wine region (Italian varietals: prosecco, sangiovese, nebbiolo). The King Valley has a strong Italian migrant heritage. Clear waters, popular for fishing (trout in upper reaches). Joins the Ovens at Wangaratta. The valley road from Wangaratta to Whitfield follows the river past vineyards, hop yards, and grazing land.

---

## 20. Kiewa River

- **Length**: ~109 km
- **Source**: Bogong High Plains, near Mount Bogong (Victoria's highest mountain, 1,986 m)
- **Mouth**: Murray River, near Wodonga (at Killara)
- **Key Towns**: Mount Beauty, Tawonga, Dederang, Kiewa, Yackandandah (nearby), Wodonga
- **Major Dams**: Rocky Valley Dam (Bogong High Plains), Junction Dam, Clover Dam — Kiewa Hydro-Electric Scheme
- **Features**: Heavily harnessed by the Kiewa Hydro Scheme (1938-1961) — a chain of dams and power stations cascading from the high plains. Mount Beauty is the scheme's service town. In the lower reaches (Kiewa to Wodonga), the river is a quiet agricultural waterway — dairy, grazing, some cropping. The Kiewa Valley flats south of Wodonga are highly productive. Tributaries include the East and West Kiewa rivers (East Kiewa is the main power-producing branch).

---

## 21. Glenelg River

- **Length**: ~476 km
- **Source**: Grampians (Gariwerd) National Park, near Mount William
- **Mouth**: Discovery Bay, Southern Ocean, at Nelson
- **Key Towns**: Balmoral, Harrow, Casterton, Dartmoor, Nelson
- **Major Dams**: Rocklands Reservoir (off-river storage via channel from the Glenelg), Moora Moora Reservoir
- **Features**: Longest river in western Victoria. The Glenelg River Gorge through the Lower Glenelg National Park is spectacular — limestone cliffs, caves, and the famous Princess Margaret Rose Cave. Popular canoeing and camping from Dartmoor to Nelson. Flows through grazing country (wool, beef). The Glenelg–Wannon river junction near Harrow is an important environmental site. The mouth at Nelson is a fishing and holiday destination. Relatively healthy river with good water quality in the lower reaches.

---

## 22. Hopkins River

- **Length**: ~271 km
- **Source**: Great Dividing Range, near Ararat (Mount Cole Creek / Fiery Creek headwaters)
- **Mouth**: Southern Ocean, at Warrnambool (Lady Bay, through the Hopkins River mouth — a protected estuary)
- **Key Towns**: Ararat (near headwaters — via Mount Cole Creek), Wickliffe, Mortlake, Terang (nearby), Warrnambool
- **Major Dams**: None directly on the Hopkins — headworks include Lake Bolac (natural lake, via Fiery Creek), Deep Lake
- **Features**: Major river of the Western District. The Hopkins estuary at Warrnambool is a significant recreational fishing area — the Hopkins River mouth forms a protected harbor. Flows through volcanic plains (basalt country), grazing land, and dairy country. The Hopkins Falls at Wangoom (near Warrnambool) are a popular attraction — a broad, 90-metre wide cascade. The river is vital for western district agriculture. The lower estuary is popular for boating, skiing, and bream fishing.

---

## 23. Wannon River

- **Length**: ~160 km
- **Source**: Grampians (Gariwerd) National Park, near Mount William
- **Mouth**: Glenelg River, near Casterton (Glenelg-Wannon junction, near Harrow)
- **Key Towns**: Dunkeld (near), Cavendish, Coleraine
- **Major Dams**: No major dam
- **Features**: Key Glenelg River tributary. Flows through the Grampians foothills and the Wannon Valley. The Wannon Falls (near Cavendish) are a significant tourist attraction, as are the nearby Nigretta Falls. Prime wool-growing country — the Western District's Merino heritage. Sir Robert Menzies' family farm was in the Wannon (Jeparit/Casterton area). The Wannon electorate is one of Victoria's most conservative seats.

## Water Notes (SHTF Context)

- **Melbourne's water**: Primarily from closed forested catchments in the Yarra Ranges (Upper Yarra, O'Shannassy, Maroondah) plus Thomson Dam. The closed catchments mean water quality is exceptional — no treatment beyond chlorination for most supply.
- **Desalination**: The Victorian Desalination Plant at Wonthaggi has capacity of 150 GL/year — built as drought insurance. Can supply up to one-third of Melbourne's annual need.
- **Gippsland Lakes**: Australia's largest inland waterway system (400 km²). Essential for fishing, tourism, and environmental flows. Salinity issues from drought and reduced river flows.
- **The Wimmera-Mallee Pipeline**: Replaced 17,500 km of open channels with 9,000 km of pipeline — saved 103 GL/year. Critical for western Victoria's drought resilience.
- **Murray-Darling Basin Plan**: Ongoing political issue — water buybacks and environmental flow targets. The Goulburn and Campaspe systems are heavily affected.
