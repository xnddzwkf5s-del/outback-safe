# Northern Territory — Major River Systems

> ⚠️ **CROCODILE WARNING:** ALL rivers, creeks, billabongs, and estuaries in the Top End (north of approximately Katherine) contain saltwater crocodiles (*Crocodylus porosus*). They are ambush predators, territorial, and can grow to 6+ metres. Assume every body of water holds a croc. Do not swim, wade, or camp within 5 metres of the water's edge. Freshwater crocodiles (*Crocodylus johnstoni*) are also present in many inland waterways — less aggressive but still dangerous. This warning applies to EVERY entry below north of the 16°S parallel.

---

## Seasonal Overview: The Wet-Dry Cycle

Northern Territory rivers operate on an extreme tropical wet-dry cycle:

| Season | Months | River State |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **Wet** | November – April | Monsoonal rains. Rivers flood dramatically — many rise 10–20 metres. Floodplains become inland seas. Roads cut. Communities isolated. |
| **Dry** | May – October | Rapid drying. Many rivers reduce to a series of disconnected waterholes (billabongs). Some cease flowing entirely. Dry-season waterholes become critical wildlife refuges. |

**Key implication:** A "river" shown on a map may be a dry sandy bed for 7 months of the year. Always verify current conditions. Dry-season waterholes are the practical water sources.

## Major River Systems

### 1. Mary River

- **Length:** ~225 km
- **Source:** Arnhem Land escarpment, near Moline (southwest of Kakadu)
- **Mouth:** Van Diemen Gulf, via Sampan Creek / Tommycut Creek (multiple distributary mouths)
- **Key Features:**
  - No major tributaries — fed primarily by monsoon runoff and escarpment springs
  - Famous for **Mary River National Park** — one of the world's highest saltwater crocodile densities
  - The floodplain is heavily used for barramundi fishing (Corroboree Billabong, Hardies Lagoon, Shady Camp)
  - Shady Camp barrage — tidal interface, extreme croc zone
  - During the wet, the Mary breaks its banks and the entire floodplain becomes navigable by boat
  - Dry season: contracting billabongs concentrate fish, birds, and crocodiles
- **Key Towns:** No major towns on river. Mary River Roadhouse on Arnhem Highway. Corroboree Park Tavern nearby.
- **Access:** Arnhem Highway crosses near Corroboree; Shady Camp accessible via Point Stuart Road (4WD)

---

### 2. Adelaide River

- **Length:** ~180 km
- **Source:** Litchfield National Park area, near Tabletop Range
- **Mouth:** Adam Bay, Van Diemen Gulf (at the channel known as the Adelaide River Mouth, northwest of Humpty Doo)
- **Key Towns:** Adelaide River township (on Stuart Highway), Humpty Doo (downstream area)
- **Key Features:**
  - Two sections: **upper Adelaide** (above the Arnhem Highway bridge) — freshwater reaches through the Marrakai floodplain; **lower Adelaide** — tidal, estuarine, extreme crocodile density
  - Famous Jumping Crocodile tours operate on the tidal lower Adelaide
  - Adelaide River township: historic WWII site, war cemetery
  - Railway bridge and old highway bridge at the township
  - Wet season: can rise 15+ metres, flooding the township approach
  - Dry season: upper reaches reduce to pools; lower tidal section remains navigable
- **Access:** Stuart Highway (township), Arnhem Highway (lower reaches)

### 3. Finniss River

- **Length:** ~80 km
- **Source:** Litchfield National Park / Tabletop Range (near Wangi Falls)
- **Mouth:** Fog Bay, Timor Sea (southwest of Darwin, near Dundee Beach)
- **Key Towns:** None directly. Dundee Beach nearby.
- **Key Features:**
  - Flows through Litchfield's western margins
  - Popular barramundi fishing in lower reaches
  - Tidal estuarine lower section — saltwater crocodiles
  - Finniss River Station (private pastoral lease)
  - Wet season: floodplain inundation connects to Adelaide River floodplain
  - Dry season: upper reaches dry substantially; lower tidal section permanent
- **Access:** Fog Bay Road (to Dundee Beach); 4WD tracks to lower reaches

### 4. Reynolds River

- **Length:** ~38 km (feeds into Finniss floodplain)
- **Source:** Litchfield National Park area (south of Tabletop Range)
- **Mouth:** Joins Finniss River system / Fog Bay floodplain
- **Key Features:**
  - Small river, highly seasonal
  - Reynolds River floodplain important for wetland ecology
  - Litchfield Park boundary
  - Saltwater crocs in tidal lower reaches
  - Often dry in upper reaches by mid-dry season
- **Access:** Litchfield Park Road (4WD)

### 5. Daly River (and Tributaries)

The Daly is one of the NT's most important river systems — high dry-season flow due to spring-fed tributaries.

- **Length:** ~350 km (Daly main stem from Katherine-Flora confluence to mouth)
- **Source:** Confluence of Katherine River and Flora River (near Dorisvale)
- **Mouth:** Anson Bay, Timor Sea (southwest of Darwin)
- **Key Towns:** Daly River (Nauiyu community), Peppimenarti (upstream), Katherine (on Katherine River tributary)
- **Key Features:**
  - One of the few NT rivers that flows year-round, sustained by limestone aquifer springs (Tindall Limestone Aquifer) in the Katherine and Flora catchments
  - Famous **barramundi fishing competition** (Million Dollar Fish) — the Daly Barra Classic
  - Major flood risk in wet season — Daly River community has evacuated multiple times (1998, 2008, 2018, 2023)
  - Highest recorded flood: ~16 metres at Daly River Police Station gauge
  - Navigable by larger boats in the dry season for significant distances
  - Extreme croc density throughout tidal reaches (all areas below the Daly River crossing)
- **Access:** Daly River Road (sealed from Stuart Highway turnoff; unsealed last section); can be cut for months in wet

#### 5a. Katherine River

- **Length:** ~330 km
- **Source:** Nitmiluk (Katherine Gorge) National Park — spring-fed from Arnhem Land escarpment
- **Confluence:** Joins Flora River to form the Daly River at Dorisvale
- **Key Towns:** Katherine (largest inland town in NT Top End)
- **Key Features:**
  - Flows through Nitmiluk Gorge (13 gorges, spectacular sandstone cliffs) — major tourist attraction
  - Spring-fed — flows year-round from the Tindall Aquifer
  - Katherine River flooded catastrophically in 1998 (Australia Day Flood) and 2006 — most of Katherine town centre inundated
  - Katherine Hot Springs — geothermal pools adjacent to river in town
  - Freshwater reaches around Katherine town (freshwater crocs present; saltwater crocs rare above the low-level bridge but not impossible)
  - Becomes tidal near the Daly confluence
- **Access:** Katherine township; Nitmiluk Gorge boat tours

#### 5b. Flora River

- **Length:** ~140 km
- **Source:** Flora River Nature Park springs (southwest of Katherine)
- **Confluence:** Joins Katherine River near Dorisvale
- **Key Features:**
  - Spring-fed, permanent flow throughout dry season
  - Flora River Nature Park — tufa dams, clear water, pandanus-lined pools
  - Excellent barramundi and sooty grunter fishing
  - Freshwater reaches — freshwater crocs present; saltwater crocs in lower tidal reaches
  - Remote — limited access
- **Access:** 4WD track from Victoria Highway near Katherine

#### 5c. Fergusson River

- **Length:** ~100 km
- **Source:** North of Katherine, near Edith Farms Road area
- **Confluence:** Joins the Daly River northeast of Katherine
- **Key Features:**
  - Crossed by the Stuart Highway at Fergusson River bridge (between Katherine and Pine Creek)
  - Dry-season waterholes in upper reaches
  - Wet-season flooding cuts roads
  - Freshwater through most of its length in dry season
- **Access:** Stuart Highway crossing; minor access tracks

### 7. South Alligator River

- **Length:** ~160 km
- **Source:** Kakadu National Park south (Mount Callanan area / South Alligator Valley)
- **Mouth:** Van Diemen Gulf (northeast of Darwin, within Kakadu)
- **Key Features:**
  - Entirely within Kakadu National Park
  - Despite the name, NO alligators — named by early Dutch explorers who mistook crocs for alligators; all are saltwater and freshwater crocodiles
  - South Alligator floodplain is a RAMSAR wetland of international significance
  - Mamukala Wetlands — spectacular birdlife (magpie geese in thousands)
  - Yellow Water (Ngurrungurrudjba) — connected wetland, boat cruises, world-class birdwatching
  - Aurorar uranium mine historic site (rehabilitated)
  - Boat access to upper freshwater reaches via the South Alligator River boat ramp
  - Saltwater crocs throughout — do not approach water
- **Access:** Arnhem Highway crosses near the South Alligator Inn / Aurora (south of Jabiru)

### 8. East Alligator River

- **Length:** ~160 km
- **Source:** Arnhem Land escarpment, northwest Arnhem Land
- **Mouth:** Van Diemen Gulf (forms north boundary of Kakadu / border with Arnhem Land)
- **Key Towns:** Jabiru (~30 km south), Gunbalanya (Oenpelli) — Arnhem Land side
- **Key Features:**
  - Forms part of the western boundary of Arnhem Land
  - Cahills Crossing — famous (and extremely dangerous) tidal crossing, crocodile feeding spectacle
  - Ubirr rock art site overlooks the East Alligator floodplain
  - Magela Creek (tributary) — subject of Ranger uranium mine environmental monitoring
  - Tidal saltwater reaches extend well upstream into gorges
  - Crossing possible at Cahills Crossing ONLY at low tide (extreme care — multiple fatalities)
  - Do NOT attempt to cross — saltwater crocodiles in the dozens visible from the crossing
- **Access:** Kakadu Highway to Ubirr / Cahills Crossing; Arnhem Land access (permit required) via Gunbalanya

### 9. West Alligator River

- **Length:** ~80 km

- **Key Features:**

  - Lies between the South Alligator and Wildman systems

### 15. Roper River (and Tributaries)

One of the NT's largest navigable rivers, critical to the Gulf region.

- **Length:** ~400 km
- **Source:** East of Mataranka (confluence of Waterhouse River and Roper Creek, near Mataranka)
- **Mouth:** Limmen Bight, Gulf of Carpentaria (at the Roper River mouth, near Numbulwar)
- **Key Towns:** Mataranka (near headwaters), Ngukurr (mid-reaches), Numbulwar (near mouth)
- **Key Features:**
  - Spring-fed — Mataranka Thermal Pool and Bitter Springs contribute significant base flow (Rainbow Spring, part of the Roper headwaters system through the Waterhouse River)
  - Roper Bar — historic ford crossing; navigable above and below
  - Tidal influence extends ~100 km upstream from the mouth (to Roper Bar area)
  - Saltwater crocodiles throughout the tidal reaches and well upstream
  - Excellent barramundi fishing — Roper is one of the NT's best barra rivers
  - Wet season flooding extensive — Roper Highway cut at multiple crossings
  - The Roper River Mission (now Ngukurr) is historic
  - Dry season: permanent water throughout due to spring-fed base; a critical dry-season refuge
- **Access:** Roper Highway to Mataranka; Roper Bar accessible by 4WD (dry season only); Ngukurr by road (unsealed, via the Central Arnhem Road)

#### 15a. Waterhouse River

- **Length:** ~60 km
- **Source:** Mataranka Springs / Rainbow Spring (artesian spring system)
- **Confluence:** Joins Roper Creek to form the Roper River near Mataranka
- **Key Features:**
  - Famously clear and warm — spring-fed artesian water at ~33°C year-round
  - Mataranka Thermal Pool and Bitter Springs are actually on or adjacent to the Waterhouse River
  - Popular swimming spots (these swimming areas are monitored for crocs — but stay alert)
  - Permanent flow
- **Access:** Mataranka township; Mataranka Homestead

#### 15b. Elsey Creek

- **Length:** ~50 km
- **Source:** Elsey Station area
- **Confluence:** Joins Roper River near Mataranka
- **Key Features:**
  - Elsey National Park (inspired by "We of the Never Never" — Jeannie Gunn's classic Australian novel set at Elsey Station)
  - Elsey Cemetery (historic)
  - Thermal springs feed sections of the creek
  - Permanent waterholes in dry season
- **Access:** Mataranka; Elsey National Park tracks

#### 15c. Hodgson River

- **Length:** ~130 km
- **Source:** Hodgson Downs (Alawa Aboriginal Land Trust)
- **Confluence:** Joins the Roper River near Ngukurr
- **Key Features:**
  - Flows through remote Gulf country
  - Part of the Roper River wetlands system
  - Few permanent settlements
  - Crocodiles throughout
- **Access:** Very limited — unsealed remote tracks, permits required for Aboriginal Land

#### 15d. Wilton River

- **Length:** ~150 km
- **Source:** Wilton Plateau / central Arnhem Land
- **Confluence:** Joins the Roper River near Roper Bar
- **Key Features:**
  - Gorge country — Wilton Gorge is spectacular
  - Often accessible only by helicopter
  - Deep permanent waterholes in dry season
  - Sandstone gorge system
  - Extremely remote — crocodiles, no roads
- **Access:** Helicopter or boat (limited)

### 16. McArthur River

- **Length:** ~520 km
- **Source:** Barkly Tableland, near the Northern Territory–Queensland border (south of the Gulf)
- **Mouth:** Gulf of Carpentaria (near Borroloola, at the McArthur River mouth)
- **Key Towns:** Borroloola (near mouth); McArthur River Mine area (mid-reaches)
- **Key Features:**
  - One of the longest rivers in the Gulf region
  - McArthur River Mine (zinc-lead-silver) is on the river — environmental controversies, including concerns about river contamination
  - Only river in the world that flows directly through a major zinc-lead deposit
  - Carpentaria Highway crosses the McArthur River near Borroloola
  - Borroloola is the largest town in the Gulf region
  - Tidal reaches: saltwater crocodiles present
  - Wet season: extensive flooding — Borroloola can be isolated by road for weeks
  - King Ash Bay (fishing village at the mouth) is popular during dry season
- **Access:** Carpentaria Highway (sealed to Borroloola turnoff, then unsealed to Borroloola); Borroloola has airport. King Ash Bay accessible by boat.

### 26. Victoria River (and Tributaries)

The NT's longest river system — a massive outback waterway.

- **Length:** ~780 km (Victoria River main stem)
- **Source:** Central-western NT, east of the Tanami Desert (near Daguragu / Kalkarindji)
- **Mouth:** Joseph Bonaparte Gulf, Timor Sea (near the Western Australian border, south of Wadeye / Port Keats)
- **Key Towns:** Timber Creek (mid-reaches), Victoria River Downs (VRD), Kalkarindji / Daguragu (upper reaches), Victoria River Roadhouse (crossing)
- **Key Features:**
  - Longest river in the Northern Territory
  - Victoria River Roadhouse — historic crossing on the Victoria Highway
  - Timber Creek — only significant town on the river; fuel, supplies, boat ramp
  - Massive tidal range in lower reaches — up to ~7 metres
  - Saltwater crocodiles throughout tidal section (up to Timber Creek area and beyond in wet season)
  - Gregory National Park flanks sections of the river — rugged sandstone escarpments
  - Victoria River Gorge — spectacular, accessible by boat from Timber Creek
  - Wet season: catastrophic flooding. The Victoria can rise 20+ metres. Victoria Highway impassable for weeks. Timber Creek frequently isolated.
  - Dry season: significant flow reduction but permanent water in main channel and deep waterholes
  - "Victoria River Downs" (VRD) station was once the world's largest cattle station
- **Access:** Victoria Highway; Timber Creek; Victoria River Roadhouse; boat access from Timber Creek

### 29. Ord River (NT Section)

- **Length:** ~650 km total; NT section ~150 km (upper reaches)
- **Source:** Kimberley region, Western Australia; headwaters extend into the eastern Kimberley near the NT border
- **Mouth:** Cambridge Gulf (near Wyndham/Kununurra, WA)
- **Key Towns (NT):** None. Kununurra (WA) is the key town.

  - Relevant to understanding the western border water systems; the Ord does not significantly sustain any NT community
- **Access (NT section):** Remote — no formal access

## Water Safety — SHTF Context

### Immediate Priorities

1. **Filtration and boiling are mandatory** for all surface water — livestock, wildlife, and upstream contamination are universal in NT waterways
2. **Crocodile risk overrides water urgency.** Do NOT approach a waterbody at dawn/dusk/night. Do NOT enter water for any reason. Do NOT set camp within 5m of the waterline. Crocodiles can sprint on land at 12+ km/h over short distances.
3. **Dry-season waterholes are strategic.** Know which waterholes have perennial water — many mapped "rivers" are dry sand for 7 months
4. **Spring-fed systems are the most reliable year-round sources:**
   - Katherine River & Flora River (Tindall Aquifer)
   - Mataranka / Roper headwaters (artesian springs)
   - Specific gorge waterholes in Victoria River / Keep River systems
5. **Tidal rivers:** Freshwater is available above the tidal limit. Below the tidal limit, water is saline, and crocodile density is extreme.

### Seasonal Water Availability by Region

| Region | Dry Season (May–Oct) | Wet Season (Nov–Apr) |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Top End Coast (Darwin–Kakadu) | Spring-fed rivers have flow; others contract to billabongs | Flooded — water everywhere, but turbid and difficult to access |
| Katherine Region | Katherine/Flora: permanent; smaller creeks: dry pools | Flooded — roads cut, isolation risk high |
| Gulf Country (Roper, McArthur) | Springs at Mataranka; downstream pools; estuary water brackish | Extensive flooding; communities isolated |
| Victoria River District | Permanent pools in main channel and gorges; tributaries dry | Catastrophic flooding — Victoria Highway impassable |
| Barkly Tableland | Very few surface water sources — rely on bores and small waterholes | Shallow flooding on black-soil plains; unreliable |
| Central Australia (Alice Springs) | Surface water extremely rare — see nt-water-sources.md | Flash flooding in Todd River catchment; ephemeral at best |
