# Western Australia — Nearest Water Sources by Town

> **SHTF Note:** "Nearest water" indicates the closest significant natural or man-made water body. For ephemeral rivers (noted as "dry most of year"), **do not rely on surface water** — you must verify flow before depending on it. Groundwater availability is noted where relevant. Salinity is a major issue in inland WA — assume water needs treatment unless marked "fresh."

---

## Perth Metropolitan Area

### Perth
- **Nearest water:** Swan River — runs through city centre, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -31.9505, 115.8605
- **Water notes:** Tidal estuary at city reach — brackish/fresh boundary moves seasonally. Fresh above Guildford. Canning River joins at Melville Water. Hills dams (Mundaring, Canning, Wungong, Serpentine) are the potable supply. Groundwater (Gnangara Mound) accessible via shallow bores in northern suburbs.
- **Water Source for Perth Metro:** Integrated Water Supply Scheme — dams + desalination (2 plants) + groundwater. In SHTF: prioritise hills reservoirs and borefields.

### Fremantle
- **Nearest water:** Swan River mouth / Indian Ocean — 0 km (coastal port city)
- **Coordinates:** -32.0550, 115.7460
- **Water notes:** Completely tidal/saline at the river mouth. No freshwater surface source in immediate vicinity. Nearest fresh: Canning River upstream of Kent St Weir (~15 km NE) or Swan River above Guildford (~25 km NE).

### Joondalup
- **Nearest water:** Lake Joondalup — within suburb, 0–2 km
- **Coordinates:** -31.7420, 115.7700
- **Water notes:** Lake Joondalup is a shallow, seasonal freshwater/saline wetland — unreliable for drinking. Nearest reliable: Gnangara Mound groundwater (extensive aquifer below northern suburbs) or Wanneroo wetlands. Ocean ~5 km west (saline).

### Mandurah
- **Nearest water:** Peel Inlet / Indian Ocean — 0 km; Murray River mouth ~3 km south
- **Coordinates:** -32.5250, 115.7240
- **Water notes:** Coastal city on Peel-Harvey Estuary (brackish/tidal). Murray River fresh water upstream of Pinjarra (~20 km inland). Serpentine River drains into the estuary from the north. Canals throughout the city are tidal and saline. Harvey Dam ~30 km south.

### Rockingham
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km (coastal); Lake Richmond ~2 km inland
- **Coordinates:** -32.2810, 115.7300
- **Water notes:** Coastal suburb. Lake Richmond is a thrombolite-bearing freshwater/saline lake — ecologically significant. No reliable surface fresh water. Serpentine Dam ~35 km east.

## South-West

### Bunbury
- **Nearest water:** Leschenault Inlet / Indian Ocean — 0 km; Collie River ~3 km E at Eaton
- **Coordinates:** -33.3270, 115.6370
- **Water notes:** Major regional city. Leschenault Inlet is tidal. Collie River provides fresh water upstream. Wellington Dam (~30 km SE) is the main water supply. Preston River flows through southern suburbs.

### Busselton
- **Nearest water:** Geographe Bay / Indian Ocean — 0 km; Vasse River ~1 km N
- **Coordinates:** -33.6480, 115.3440
- **Water notes:** Coastal city. Vasse River is small, seasonally flowing. Vasse-Wonnerup Estuary (Ramsar wetland) ~5 km NE. Nearest major freshwater: Margaret River ~40 km S or Blackwood River ~50 km E. Busselton Jetty (1.8 km) and Geographe Bay are marine only.

### Margaret River
- **Nearest water:** Margaret River — within town, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -33.9520, 115.0750
- **Water notes:** Perennial river flowing through town. Water quality is good (forested catchment). Flows to Indian Ocean at Prevelly (~8 km W). Ten Mile Brook Dam and Margaret River Weir supply town water.

### Augusta
- **Nearest water:** Blackwood River mouth at Hardy Inlet — runs through town, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -34.3140, 115.1600
- **Water notes:** Hardy Inlet is tidal/brackish at Augusta. Blackwood River fresh water upstream (good flow year-round). Flinders Bay / Southern Ocean on southern side of town. Cape Leeuwin lighthouse at the tip — Indian and Southern Oceans meet.

### Albany
- **Nearest water:** Princess Royal Harbour / King George Sound — 0 km; Kalgan River ~15 km NE
- **Coordinates:** -35.0280, 117.8840
- **Water notes:** Major southern port city. Deep sheltered harbour (saline). Yakkan Toort / Kalgan River flows into Oyster Harbour ~10 km NE — fresh upstream. Two Peoples Bay and Wilson Inlet nearby. Town water from Two Peoples Bay / Millbrook catchments and bores.

### Denmark
- **Nearest water:** Denmark River — runs through town, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -34.9600, 117.3530
- **Water notes:** Denmark River flows through town to Wilson Inlet. Perennial — high rainfall catchment. Good water quality. Rapids and river pools are accessible. Wilson Inlet is a large estuary (seasonally open to ocean). Quickup Dam supplies town water.

### Walpole
- **Nearest water:** Walpole Inlet / Nornalup Inlet — 0 km at town edge; Frankland River ~2 km S
- **Coordinates:** -34.9780, 116.7330
- **Water notes:** Town at junction of Walpole and Nornalup Inlets. Deep River and Frankland River feed the inlet system. Coastal freshwater is abundant. High rainfall (~1200 mm/year). Perennial water availability.

### Manjimup
- **Nearest water:** Warren River (upper) — ~8 km W; small creeks through town
- **Coordinates:** -34.2420, 116.1470
- **Water notes:** High rainfall region. Multiple creeks feed into the Warren River system. Nearest major waterbody: Glen Mervyn Dam ~15 km SE. Perennial water available from surrounding forest rivers. Agricultural irrigation dams common on farms.

### Pemberton
- **Nearest water:** Warren River (upper tributaries) — ~5 km S; Big Brook Dam ~3 km N
- **Coordinates:** -34.4420, 116.0370
- **Water notes:** Surrounded by karri forest with abundant perennial creeks and rivers. Big Brook Dam is a popular freshwater lake (swimming, fishing). Donnelly River ~20 km SW. Water is abundant year-round — one of Australia's wettest regions outside tropics.

### Bridgetown
- **Nearest water:** Blackwood River — runs through town, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -33.9610, 116.1370
- **Water notes:** Blackwood River is perennial here (headwaters region). Flows year-round. Good water quality. Key for the local farming region. Joins with tributaries to become one of WA's most reliable southern rivers.

### Narrogin
- **Nearest water:** Small creeks feeding into Arthur River catchment — ephemeral; numerous farm dams within ~5 km
- **Coordinates:** -32.9310, 117.1800
- **Water notes:** Inland Wheatbelt town. No major natural waterway. Relies on dam catchments and groundwater bores for town supply. Dryland farming country. Nearest flowing river: Blackwood River (via Arthur River) ~50 km S.

### Northam
- **Nearest water:** Avon River — runs through town centre, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -31.6530, 116.6740
- **Water notes:** Avon River flows through Northam. Seasonal — strong winter flow, pools/slow in summer. Avon River whitewater season in winter is a tourist draw. Major Wheatbelt town. Morrell River Spring nearby.

### York
- **Nearest water:** Avon River — runs through town centre, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -31.8880, 116.7680
- **Water notes:** Historic town on Avon River. Seasonal flow as above. River pools maintain some water year-round. Mundaring-to-Goldfields pipeline corridor passes through.

### Toodyay
- **Nearest water:** Avon River — runs through town centre, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -31.5490, 116.4660
- **Water notes:** Avon River flows through Toodyay. Seasonal. River pools maintained in summer. Avon Valley National Park nearby. Toodyay is upstream of Northam — river is narrower/faster in winter.

### Merredin
- **Nearest water:** Merredin Peak Reservoir / farm dams — ~1 km; no natural permanent waterway
- **Coordinates:** -31.4840, 118.2780
- **Water notes:** Heart of the Wheatbelt. Totally dependent on the Goldfields Water Supply Scheme (pipeline from Mundaring) + rainwater tanks. Merredin Peak is a granite outcrop with a small catchment into a reservoir. Nearest natural water: Avon River ~130 km W. Salt lakes common to east (undrinkable).

### Southern Cross
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Goldfields pipeline + small catchments.
- **Coordinates:** -31.2300, 119.3280
- **Water notes:** Named for the Southern Cross constellation. On the Goldfields pipeline route. Surrounded by salt lakes and gold mining. Karalee Dam (rock catchment on a granite outcrop) historically supplied water. Very arid — no flowing rivers. Nearest significant natural surface water: none within 200+ km.

## Goldfields

### Kalgoorlie-Boulder
- **Nearest water:** Lake Douglas (man-made, mine void now recreational) — ~5 km S; Goldfields pipeline terminus
- **Coordinates:** -30.7480, 121.4740
- **Water notes:** WA's largest inland city. **Entirely reliant on the Goldfields Water Supply Scheme** (pipeline from Mundaring Weir near Perth, ~560 km). No natural surface water. Surrounded by hypersaline lakes (Lake Lefroy, Lake Cowan — undrinkable). Groundwater is highly saline. Super Pit mine void is filling with saline groundwater. In SHTF: pipeline is the ONLY practical water source. Rainwater tanks on most houses. Annual rainfall ~260 mm.

### Coolgardie
- **Nearest water:** No natural surface water. Goldfields pipeline + rainwater.
- **Coordinates:** -30.9550, 121.1650
- **Water notes:** Historic gold rush town. Also pipeline-dependent. Ben Prior's Park (old mine machinery) and the town's history is defined by water scarcity. Original gold rush population survived on condensers and rock catchments. Nearest fresh water: pipeline or Perth (~550 km). Annual rainfall ~280 mm.

### Kambalda
- **Nearest water:** Lake Lefroy — ~2 km E (hypersaline, undrinkable); Goldfields pipeline ~10 km at Kalgoorlie branch
- **Coordinates:** -31.1990, 121.6700
- **Water notes:** Nickel mining town. Lake Lefroy is a massive salt pan — land speed record attempts held here. Zero natural surface freshwater. Pipeline supply from Kalgoorlie system. Treated mine water may supplement supply.

## South Coast / Goldfields-Esperance

### Esperance
- **Nearest water:** Esperance Bay / Southern Ocean — 0 km; Lake Warden ~5 km NW; Pink Lake ~5 km W
- **Coordinates:** -33.8590, 121.8920
- **Water notes:** Coastal town. Lake Warden is a Ramsar-listed wetland system (saline/brackish). Pink Lake was historically pink (dunaliella salina algae) but has largely lost colour due to reduced salinity. Town water from groundwater and catchments. Bandy Creek, Woody Lake. Nearest perennial river: none close — Lort River ~60 km E (ephemeral). Annual rainfall ~620 mm — wetter than inland.

### Ravensthorpe
- **Nearest water:** Small soaks and farm dams — no permanent natural waterway; Fitzgerald River tributaries ephemeral
- **Coordinates:** -33.5800, 120.0480
- **Water notes:** Mining and farming town near Fitzgerald River National Park. Phillips River (ephemeral) flows through to Culham Inlet. No reliable permanent surface water. Rainfall ~400 mm. Town water from dams and bores.

### Norseman
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Norseman Dam reservoir.
- **Coordinates:** -32.1960, 121.7760
- **Water notes:** Gateway to the Nullarbor. Goldfields pipeline branch supplies town. Surrounded by salt lakes and gold mining. Cowan Lake, Lake Dundas (salt). Zero permanent natural water. A critical stopping point on the Eyre Highway — water here is entirely infrastructure-dependent.

## Northern Goldfields

### Leonora
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Malcolm Dam (~10 km). Pipeline branch from Kalgoorlie.
- **Coordinates:** -28.8850, 121.3320
- **Water notes:** Remote gold mining town north of Kalgoorlie. Malcolm Dam is a water supply infrastructure. Groundwater typically saline. Pipeline from Kalgoorlie system. Rainfall ~230 mm. Extreme water scarcity without infrastructure. Historic Gwalia ghost town adjacent.

### Laverton
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Pipeline + bores.
- **Coordinates:** -28.6250, 122.4050
- **Water notes:** End of the Goldfields pipeline branch. Remote. Surrounded by Great Victoria Desert to the east. Zero natural surface water of any kind. One of the most water-scarce towns in Australia. Great Central Road starts here (to NT).

## Mid West / Murchison

### Meekatharra
- **Nearest water:** Murchison River headwaters — ~20 km N (ephemeral, dry most years); Peace Gorge pool
- **Coordinates:** -26.5930, 118.4930
- **Water notes:** Remote inland town. Murchison River starts near here but is dry except after rare heavy rain. Town water from the Goldfields pipeline (branch) and groundwater bores. Rainfall ~230 mm. Wiluna-Meekatharra Road is a key outback route. Meekatharra Creek ephemeral. Peace Gorge has granite pool catchments.

### Mount Magnet
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Granite rock catchments and pipeline.
- **Coordinates:** -28.0600, 117.8470
- **Water notes:** Gold mining town. Surrounded by salt lakes (Lake Austin — undrinkable). Goldfields pipeline supplies town. Historic rock catchment systems on local granite outcrops. Rainfall ~250 mm. Nearest river: Murchison River ~100 km.

### Cue
- **Nearest water:** No natural permanent surface water. Goldfields pipeline + granite catchments.
- **Coordinates:** -27.4220, 117.8990
- **Water notes:** Historic gold rush town near Mount Magnet. Pipeline-dependent. Walga Rock nearby (aboriginal art site, also a granite water catchment site). Rainfall ~220 mm. Cue Dam (historic rock catchment reservoir).

### Wiluna
- **Nearest water:** Wiluna Soak / nearby claypans (seasonal); Goldfields pipeline terminus (northern branch)
- **Coordinates:** -26.5930, 120.2250
- **Water notes:** Edge of the desert. The Goldfields pipeline reaches its northern limit here. Canning Stock Route starts. Water is extremely scarce. Surrounding claypans hold water after rain (seasonal). Nearest river: Gascoyne headwaters ~100 km SW (ephemeral). Martu Aboriginal lands. Rainfall ~220 mm.

### Morawa
- **Nearest water:** Small creeks (ephemeral) — no major waterway nearby; farm dams within 5 km
- **Coordinates:** -29.2110, 116.0080
- **Water notes:** Wheatbelt town on the edge of Midwest. No natural rivers of significance. Farm dams (rock catchments) are common. Nearest waterway: Greenough River headwaters ~30 km SW (ephemeral). Town supply from groundwater bores. Rainfall ~330 mm.

## Mid West Coast

### Geraldton
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km; Chapman River mouth ~5 km N; Greenough River ~20 km S; Ellendale Pool ~45 km SE
- **Coordinates:** -28.7750, 114.6120
- **Water notes:** WA's largest coastal city north of Perth. Chapman River flows through northern suburbs (seasonal). Ellendale Pool is the most reliable freshwater site in the region — a permanent spring-fed waterhole on the Greenough River, accessible by road. Town water from groundwater and desalination. Rainfall ~440 mm.

### Dongara
- **Nearest water:** Irwin River — runs through town, ~0.5 km; Indian Ocean ~1 km W
- **Coordinates:** -29.2530, 114.9350
- **Water notes:** Coastal town. Irwin River is seasonal — flows in winter, pools in summer. Denison Beach and ocean access. Good fishing. Port Denison marina. Irwin River mouth is a popular spot.

### Jurien Bay
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km; Hill River ~15 km S (ephemeral, seasonal pools)
- **Coordinates:** -30.3050, 115.0440
- **Water notes:** Coastal fishing/tourism town. No major freshwater rivers nearby. Jurien Bay Marine Park. Nearest reliable fresh water: small reservoirs and groundwater bores inland. Lake Thetis (thrombolites) ~2 km N — saline.

### Cervantes
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km; no natural freshwater river nearby
- **Coordinates:** -30.5030, 115.0580
- **Water notes:** Small coastal town near the Pinnacles (Nambung National Park). Zero natural surface fresh water. Lake Thetis nearby (saline). Town water from groundwater. Nearest freshwater river: Hill River ~40 km N (ephemeral). Rainfall ~560 mm.

### Lancelin
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km; Lancelin Island ~1 km offshore (no water)
- **Coordinates:** -31.0160, 115.3320
- **Water notes:** Famous sandboarding/surfing town. No natural freshwater. Groundwater bores supply town. Coastal wetlands nearby are seasonal. Nearest perennial freshwater: Moore River ~50 km S.

## Gascoyne

### Kalbarri
- **Nearest water:** Murchison River — runs through town to mouth, 0 km; Indian Ocean ~1 km W
- **Coordinates:** -27.7020, 114.1800
- **Water notes:** Town at Murchison River mouth. The river maintains permanent pools in the gorge upstream of town — one of the most reliable water sources on the central WA coast. Murchison Gorge in Kalbarri National Park is ~10 km upstream. Ocean is saline. Chinaman's Beach, Red Bluff nearby. Good water security for a small coastal town.

### Shark Bay (Denham)
- **Nearest water:** Shark Bay / Indian Ocean — 0 km (hypersaline in southern reaches); no natural freshwater
- **Coordinates:** Starvation Bay references: Denham — -25.9280, 113.5350
- **Water notes:** Westernmost town in WA (not counting Dirk Hartog Island). Surrounded by hypersaline waters (Shark Bay is a World Heritage Area with extreme evaporation). Zero natural freshwater in the region. Town water from seawater desalination plant. In SHTF: desal plant is critical. No backup surface freshwater. Francois Peron National Park to the north — arid, no surface water.

### Carnarvon
- **Nearest water:** Gascoyne River — runs through town, 0 km (surface dry most of year, but substantial underground aquifer)
- **Coordinates:** -24.8680, 113.6610
- **Water notes:** Key horticultural town. The Gascoyne is famous for being a "river of sand" — dry on the surface most of the year, but water flows through the riverbed aquifer. Carnarvon's banana, mango, and vegetable plantations depend on bores into this aquifer. In SHTF: Dig into riverbed sand to access water (filter). Town water from Gascoyne borefields. Fascine inlet. One Tree Point. Indian Ocean on western side. Bibbawarra bore ~10 km N as backup.

## Pilbara & Ningaloo

### Exmouth
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean / Exmouth Gulf — 0 km; Ningaloo Reef ~5 km W; Cape Range spring systems ~15 km S
- **Coordinates:** -21.9310, 114.1260
- **Water notes:** Northern gateway to Ningaloo Reef. Zero natural surface freshwater. Town water from desalination plant + borefields. Cape Range has natural spring/mound spring systems in Yardie Creek area (Yardie Creek Gorge) — but limited and remote. US Navy base (Harold E. Holt) communications site. Rainwater harvesting critical for any self-sufficient scenario. Average rainfall ~260 mm.

### Coral Bay
- **Nearest water:** Ningaloo Reef / Indian Ocean — 0 km; no natural freshwater
- **Coordinates:** -23.1430, 113.7730
- **Water notes:** Tourist town on Ningaloo Reef. Completely dependent on bores/desal. Zero surface water. Nearest freshwater: Gascoyne borefields ~150 km S at Carnarvon. Unreliable rainfall ~300 mm.

### Onslow
- **Nearest water:** Ashburton River mouth — ~5 km E (ephemeral — dry most of year, flows after cyclones); Indian Ocean — 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -21.6360, 115.1130
- **Water notes:** Historic pearling/gas hub town. Ashburton River is strongly ephemeral. Town water from borefields and the Cane River borefield to the south. Beadon Bay is marine. Wheatstone LNG plant nearby. Rainfall ~320 mm but highly variable. Saltworks (Onslow Solar Salt) near town.

### Pannawonica
- **Nearest water:** Robe River — ~10 km SE (ephemeral); permanent mining-supplied water only
- **Coordinates:** -21.6360, 116.3250
- **Water notes:** Rio Tinto iron ore mining town (closed/gated — not a public town). Robe River is dry most of the year. Town water supplied by Rio Tinto infrastructure. Extremely isolated. Millstream-Chichester National Park ~80 km SE has permanent pools (Fortescue River system).

### Tom Price
- **Nearest water:** Small creeks in Hamersley Range (ephemeral); Kings Lake (mine-associated) ~5 km; Fortescue River tributaries seasonal
- **Coordinates:** -22.6930, 117.7910
- **Water notes:** Highest town in WA (~747 m elevation) in Hamersley Range. Iron ore mining town. Mining companies control water infrastructure. Karijini National Park ~50 km E — Fortescue Falls, Dales Gorge, and Circular Pool are rare permanent surface water in the Pilbara (spring-fed, excellent water quality, but remote). Town water from borefields and pipeline. Mount Bruce lookout. Rainfall ~380 mm.

### Paraburdoo
- **Nearest water:** Ashburton River headwaters — ~15 km (ephemeral); mining-supplied water
- **Coordinates:** -23.2040, 117.6720
- **Water notes:** Iron ore mining town near Tom Price. Ashburton River headwaters are strongly ephemeral. Mining company water infrastructure. Karijini NP ~80 km NE. Remote.

### Newman
- **Nearest water:** Fortescue River — ~10 km S (ephemeral, dry most of year); Ophthalmia Dam ~15 km S
- **Coordinates:** -23.3560, 119.7320
- **Water notes:** Major iron ore mining town (BHP). Fortescue River is ephemeral here. Ophthalmia Dam supplies town water. In SHTF: the dam and mining water infrastructure are the only reliable sources. Whaleback Mountain mine overlooks town. Karlamilyi (Rudall River) NP ~250 km NE. Extreme isolation — nearest major town is Port Hedland ~450 km N.

## Port Hedland & Surrounds

### Port Hedland
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km (port/harbour); no natural freshwater river — Yule River ~30 km S (ephemeral), De Grey River ~80 km NE (ephemeral)
- **Coordinates:** -20.3100, 118.5880
- **Water notes:** Australia's largest iron ore export port. Town water from borefields (Yule River aquifer and other sources) and desalination. De Grey River can carry massive floodwaters after cyclones — but is dry most years. Total reliance on infrastructure. Tidal creeks reach into town (Pretty Pool). Rainfall ~314 mm but highly variable. Industrial port — water quality concerns in harbour. Goode Street borefield is primary supply.

### South Hedland
- **Nearest water:** Same as Port Hedland — ~13 km inland from coast; Yule River catchment ~20 km S
- **Coordinates:** -20.4060, 118.6140
- **Water notes:** Residential twin to Port Hedland. Same water supply system. Zero natural surface water. Borefields and pipeline from Port Hedland system.

### Karratha
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean / Nickol Bay — ~15 km NW; Harding River / Harding Dam ~25 km SE
- **Coordinates:** -20.7360, 116.8460
- **Water notes:** Major Pilbara town (resources hub). Harding Dam (Lake Poongkaliyarra) is the main water supply for the West Pilbara — Karratha, Dampier, Wickham, Roebourne. The dam holds water year-round. Millstream-Chichester National Park (~100 km S) has permanent spring-fed pools. Nickol Bay is marine. Groundwater bores in Burrup Peninsula area.

### Dampier
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km (port/islands); Harding Dam ~30 km SE via Karratha pipeline
- **Coordinates:** -20.6610, 116.7130
- **Water notes:** Port town on the Burrup Peninsula (Murujuga). Harding Dam supplies water. Dampier Archipelago (42 islands). Zero natural freshwater. Murujuga Aboriginal Corporation country. World's largest petroglyph (rock art) collection nearby.

### Roebourne
- **Nearest water:** Harding River — ~2 km E (ephemeral, but Harding Dam is on this system, ~10 km upstream)
- **Coordinates:** -20.7740, 117.1470
- **Water notes:** Historic town — oldest gazetted town on the Pilbara coast (1866). Harding River flows near town. Harding Dam water supply. Important Yinjibarndi cultural centre. Ngurin Bush Tucker Trail along the river. The river typically pools in dry season — but main water supply is the dam.

### Wickham
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean / Port Walcott — 0 km; Harding River mouth ~5 km E; Harding Dam ~20 km SE
- **Coordinates:** -20.6830, 117.1340
- **Water notes:** Rio Tinto port town. Harding River enters ocean nearby at Point Samson. Water from Harding Dam system. Boat Beach, fishing spots. Industrial port nearby (Cape Lambert).

## Kimberley

### Broome
- **Nearest water:** Indian Ocean — 0 km (Roebuck Bay / Cable Beach); no natural freshwater river — nearest significant is Fitzroy River ~250 km E
- **Coordinates:** -17.9620, 122.2360
- **Water notes:** Major Kimberley tourist/pearling town. Town water from Broome aquifer (Broome Sandstone aquifer — a significant groundwater resource). Cable Beach and Roebuck Bay are beautiful but marine. Gantheaume Point, Willie Creek. In SHTF: Broome sandstone aquifer borefields provide reliable fresh groundwater. No surface rivers. Willies Creek and other tidal creeks through town. Dampier Peninsula bores. Rainfall ~615 mm (highly seasonal — nearly all in Dec-Mar).

### Derby
- **Nearest water:** Fitzroy River — ~15 km S (ephemeral, pools in dry season); King Sound / Indian Ocean — 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -17.3040, 123.6310
- **Water notes:** Tidal port town on King Sound. Fitzroy River flows into King Sound nearby. The Fitzroy reduces to disconnected pools in dry season but is accessible 4WD tracks. King Sound has the world's largest tidal range (~11.8 m). Boab Prison Tree, Derby Jetty. Town water from groundwater (Erskine aquifer/bores). Fitzroy River alluvial aquifers are a significant resource. Rainfall ~620 mm (wet season Dec-Mar).

### Fitzroy Crossing
- **Nearest water:** Fitzroy River — runs through town, 0 km
- **Coordinates:** -18.1970, 125.5830
- **Water notes:** Remote town on the Great Northern Highway. Fitzroy River flows right through. Dry season: large disconnected waterholes and pools — water IS present but must be treated. Wet season: massive flows (the town was devastated by 2023 floods that destroyed the bridge). Geikie Gorge (Danggu) National Park ~18 km NE — spectacular gorge with permanent water. Town water from bores and river intake. Bunuba and Gooniyandi country.

### Halls Creek
- **Nearest water:** Small creeks and waterholes (ephemeral); Lake Argyle pipe/aquifer ~300 km N via Kununurra
- **Coordinates:** -18.2230, 127.6680
- **Water notes:** Gateway to Purnululu (Bungle Bungle), Wolfe Creek Crater, and the Tanami Desert. Caroline Pool / Palm Springs are local waterholes — but seasonal. Old Halls Creek (historic town) has spring-fed waterholes. China Wall (quartzite formation) has rock catchments. Town water from bores and dam. Nearest reliable water: Ord River/Lake Argyle system at Kununurra ~360 km N. Very isolated. Rainfall ~550 mm, all in wet season.

### Kununurra
- **Nearest water:** Lake Kununurra (Ord River diversion dam) — runs through town, 0 km; Lake Argyle ~70 km S
- **Coordinates:** -15.7730, 128.7380
- **Water notes:** Kimberley's largest inland town. Lake Kununurra (Diversion Dam) is permanent, fresh, massive. Ord River flows through town. Irrigation channels throughout. Lake Argyle (main Ord River Dam) is Australia's largest freshwater reservoir by volume — 10,760 GL. In SHTF: this is arguably the best water security of any town in northern Australia. Lily Creek Lagoon, Hidden Valley/Mirima National Park, Celebrity Tree Park on the lake. Ord River Irrigation Area. Rainfall ~790 mm. Ivanhoe Crossing. Town water from Lake Kununurra.

### Wyndham
- **Nearest water:** Cambridge Gulf / Timor Sea — 0 km; Ord River mouth ~10 km S; Pentecost River ~30 km S
- **Coordinates:** -15.4870, 128.1240
- **Water notes:** WA's northernmost town. Deep-water port at Cambridge Gulf. Ord River flows into the Gulf. Saltwater crocodiles — do not enter water. Five Rivers Lookout (King, Pentecost, Durack, Forrest, Ord). Parry's Lagoon (freshwater wetland) is a nature reserve ~20 km W. Wyndham's water supply connects to the Kununurra/Lake Argyle system. Afghan cameleer history. Old Wyndham port area. Wet season rainfall is heavy (Nov-Mar). Town water infrastructure critical as surface water is saline/tidal at the coast.

## Summary: Water Security by Town Class

| Water Security | Towns | Water Source |
|----------------|-------|-------------|
| **Excellent** (permanent freshwater river/lake in town) | Kununurra, Bridgetown, Margaret River, Denmark, Walpole, Augusta, Pemberton, Northam, York, Toodyay | Ord River, Blackwood, Margaret, Denmark, Frankland/Nornalup, Blackwood, Warren, Avon |
| **Good** (permanent water within 30 km or reliable dam) | Perth (Swan), Mandurah (Murray), Bunbury (Collie), Albany, Kalbarri, Karratha, Roebourne, Wickham, Dampier, Geraldton, Manjimup | Swan/Murray/Collie rivers, dams, Murchison pools, Harding Dam |

## Regional Water Notes

### South-West (Augusta to Walpole)
Highest natural water security in WA. Rivers are perennial, rainfall is high. Water treatment still required for drinking.

### Perth Metropolitan
Coastal plain has groundwater (Gnangara Mound). Hills have dams. Swan and Canning are tidal in lower reaches. Eastern suburbs bushland has fewer water options.

### Wheatbelt
Dryland farming country. Seasonal Avon River (Northam/York/Toodyay) is the only significant watercourse. Salt lake country to the east. Farm dams and rainwater tanks.

### Goldfields
Almost no natural surface water. The Goldfields Water Supply Scheme (Mundaring-Kalgoorlie pipeline, built 1903) is the ONLY reason these towns exist. Without it, the Goldfields are uninhabitable.

### Pilbara
Ephemeral rivers only. Harding Dam serves West Pilbara. Ophthalmia Dam serves Newman. Mining companies control much of the water. Fortescue Marsh area. Permanent pools only at Millstream (Fortescue Falls) and Karijini.

### Gascoyne
Gascoyne River is an underground river — dig in the riverbed. Carnarvon has good aquifer. Kalbarri has Murchison pools. Shark Bay and Exmouth are completely infrastructure-dependent.

### Kimberley
Abundant wet season water, variable dry season. Ord River/Lake Argyle at Kununurra is the crown jewel. Fitzroy River has pools but goes dry most years. Saltwater crocodiles in coastal rivers. Broome aquifer is reliable.
