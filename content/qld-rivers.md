# Queensland Major River Systems

> **SHTF Reference** — Understanding Queensland's river systems is critical for water procurement, navigation, food sources (fish), and understanding flood/seasonal patterns. Rivers are grouped by drainage basin and geography.

---

## South East Queensland

### Brisbane River
- **Length**: ~344 km
- **Source**: Mount Stanley, Brisbane Range (north-west of Kilcoy)
- **Mouth**: Moreton Bay at Pinkenba / Luggage Point (Brisbane)
- **Key Towns**: Kilcoy, Fernvale, Ipswich, Brisbane
- **Major Tributaries**: Stanley River, Lockyer Creek, Bremer River, Oxley Creek, Enoggera Creek, Moggill Creek
- **Dams**: Wivenhoe Dam (on Stanley), Somerset Dam (on Stanley), Mount Crosby Weir (main extraction point for Brisbane water supply)
- **Features**: Tidal up to Mount Crosby Weir (~85 km inland). Catchment ~13,600 km². Historically flood-prone — major floods 1893, 1974, 2011, 2022. Wivenhoe Dam built 1984 for flood mitigation and water supply. Navigable by small craft below the weir. Brisbane River cod (extinct), Brisbane River turtle, bull sharks present in tidal reaches.

### Mary River
- **Length**: ~291 km
- **Source**: Conondale Range (north of Maleny)
- **Mouth**: Great Sandy Strait at River Heads (near Maryborough)
- **Key Towns**: Kenilworth, Gympie, Tiaro, Maryborough
- **Major Tributaries**: Obi Obi Creek, Yabba Creek, Six Mile Creek, Munna Creek, Wide Bay Creek
- **Dams**: Borumba Dam, proposed Traveston Crossing Dam (cancelled 2009)
- **Features**: Catchment ~9,595 km². Home to the endangered Mary River cod, Mary River turtle, and Australian lungfish (Neoceratodus forsteri — a living fossil). Significant agricultural water source. Gympie weir provides town water.

## Wide Bay – Burnett

### Burnett River
- **Length**: ~435 km
- **Source**: Western slopes of the Burnett Range (near Monto)
- **Mouth**: Coral Sea at Burnett Heads (near Bundaberg)
- **Key Towns**: Monto, Eidsvold, Mundubbera, Gayndah, Bundaberg
- **Major Tributaries**: Auburn River, Boyne River (Burnett), Barambah Creek, Three Moon Creek
- **Dams**: Paradise Dam, Wuruma Dam, Cania Dam, Fred Haigh Dam
- **Features**: Catchment ~33,000 km². Major irrigation source — Bundaberg's sugar cane and horticulture (citrus, macadamias, avocados). Paradise Dam built 2005, controversially lowered due to structural concerns. Burnett River cod (endangered, different species from Murray cod). Tidal reach extends to about 40 km upstream.

## Central Queensland

### Fitzroy River
- **Length**: ~335 km (main stem); ~960 km from headwaters of the Nogoa River (longest tributary system)
- **Source**: Confluence of the Mackenzie and Dawson Rivers near Duaringa
- **Mouth**: Keppel Bay at Port Alma (south of Rockhampton)
- **Key Towns**: Emerald (on Nogoa), Comet, Blackwater, Duaringa, Rockhampton, Gracemere
- **Major Tributaries**: Mackenzie River (includes Nogoa and Comet Rivers), Dawson River (includes Don and Dee Rivers), Isaac River, Connors River
- **Dams**: Fairbairn Dam (on Nogoa, Lake Maraboon), Theresa Creek Dam, Bedford Weir, Fitzroy Barrage (Rockhampton)
- **Features**: Queensland's largest sea-draining catchment at ~142,665 km² — larger than Tasmania. Second-largest externally-draining system in Australia after Murray-Darling. Highly episodic flows — can be dry for years then flood massively (Rockhampton floods 1918, 1954, 1991, 2011, 2013). Fitzroy Barrage at Rockhampton provides town water and tidal barrier. Major coal mining and agriculture in the catchment. Fitzroy River turtle (Rheodytes leukops — a bum-breathing turtle, unique cloacal respiration).

## North Queensland

### Burdekin River
- **Length**: ~886 km
- **Source**: Seaview Range (west of Ingham)
- **Mouth**: Upstart Bay near Home Hill / Ayr
- **Key Towns**: Charters Towers, Ravenswood, Clare, Ayr, Home Hill
- **Major Tributaries**: Suttor River, Belyando River, Cape River, Bowen River (via Suttor)
- **Dams**: Burdekin Falls Dam (Lake Dalrymple) — Queensland's largest dam by capacity (1,860,000 ML), Eungella Dam
- **Features**: Australia's largest river by peak discharge volume. Catchment ~130,000 km². Burdekin Falls Dam supplies water to Townsville via pipeline, and to Bowen and the Bowen Basin coal mines. Seasonal — dry season trickle, wet season raging torrent. Major irrigation: sugar cane and horticulture in lower Burdekin delta. Home to barramundi, sooty grunter, sleepy cod. The Burdekin Delta aquifer is one of Australia's most important groundwater systems.

### Herbert River
- **Length**: ~288 km
- **Source**: Atherton Tableland (south-west of Ravenshoe, near Millstream)
- **Mouth**: Hinchinbrook Channel at Halifax Bay (south-east of Ingham)
- **Key Towns**: Ingham, Halifax, Abergowrie
- **Major Tributaries**: Blencoe Creek, Stony Creek
- **Dams**: None on main stem
- **Features**: Catchment ~10,000 km². Flows year-round due to high rainfall in headwaters. Feeds the wet tropical lowlands — huge sugar cane farming area. Hinchinbrook Channel is a world-renowned fishing destination. Subject to cyclonic flooding.

### Tully River
- **Length**: ~133 km
- **Source**: Cardwell Range (near Ravenshoe)
- **Mouth**: Coral Sea at Tully Heads (south of Mission Beach)
- **Key Towns**: Tully
- **Major Tributaries**: Cochable Creek, Jarra Creek, Davidson Creek
- **Dams**: Kareeya Hydro (Koombooloomba Dam, on tributary)
- **Features**: One of the wettest catchments in Australia. Tully township averages ~4,100 mm rain annually — has recorded annual totals exceeding 7,900 mm. Short but extremely high-volume river. World-class white-water rafting (grade 4 rapids). Vital irrigation for banana, sugar cane, and tropical fruit plantations. Named "Tully" after the Irish river, reflecting the lush green landscape.

### Johnstone River
- **Length**: ~200 km (combined North and South branches)
- **Source**: Atherton Tableland — North Johnstone rises near Malanda; South Johnstone rises near Palmerston
- **Mouth**: Coral Sea at Innisfail
- **Key Towns**: Millaa Millaa, Malanda, Innisfail
- **Major Tributaries**: North Johnstone River, South Johnstone River, Beatrice River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Two main branches converge at Innisfail. Extremely high-rainfall catchment. Prone to devastating floods — Innisfail was inundated in 1913, 1918, 1967, 2017. Surrounded by Wet Tropics World Heritage rainforest. Major sugar and banana region. Excellent barramundi fishing in lower reaches.

### Daintree River
- **Length**: ~140 km
- **Source**: Daintree National Park (Great Dividing Range, west of Mossman Gorge)
- **Mouth**: Coral Sea at Cape Tribulation area (north of Port Douglas)
- **Key Towns**: Daintree Village, downstream of the ferry crossing
- **Major Tributaries**: Mossman River (separate — Mossman River enters at Port Douglas, not the Daintree), Bloomfield River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Catchment ~2,100 km². Flows through Daintree Rainforest, the oldest continuously-surviving tropical rainforest on Earth (~180 million years). Daintree River ferry is the only crossing (cable ferry). Famous for estuarine crocodile populations — some of the largest in the world. Wildlife cruises popular for croc and bird spotting. Salinity wedge extends well upstream during dry season. Pristine catchment with no dams or major diversions.

## Gulf of Carpentaria

### Mitchell River
- **Length**: ~750 km
- **Source**: Atherton Tableland (west of Mareeba)
- **Mouth**: Gulf of Carpentaria at Kowanyama (north of Normanton, south of Aurukun)
- **Key Towns**: Chillagoe, Dunbar, Kowanyama
- **Major Tributaries**: Walsh River, Lynd River, Palmer River, Alice River, Coleman River
- **Dams**: None (proposed dams have been controversial)
- **Features**: Largest river system in the Gulf drainage. Catchment ~73,000 km². Vast seasonal floodplains that fill during the monsoon — ideal barramundi breeding grounds. Kowanyama Aboriginal community at the mouth. Annual fishing season draws anglers for world-class barramundi. Flows only a few months per year; upper reaches nearly dry in winter. No major dams — one of the last relatively untouched large tropical rivers.

### Gilbert River
- **Length**: ~610 km
- **Source**: Gilbert Range (north-west of Einasleigh, southern Gulf)
- **Mouth**: Gulf of Carpentaria (east of Karumba)
- **Key Towns**: Gilbert River (tiny settlement), and passes near Croydon
- **Major Tributaries**: Einasleigh River, Etheridge River, Copperfield River, Percy River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Catchment ~46,000 km². Highly seasonal — massive flows in summer monsoon, reduced to isolated waterholes in winter. Einasleigh River is the main tributary; its gorge is a significant landscape feature. Important barramundi fishery. Pastoral country in mid-reaches; Gulf savannah.

### Flinders River
- **Length**: ~1,004 km
- **Source**: Burra Range (Great Dividing Range, west of Pentland / north-east of Hughenden)
- **Mouth**: Gulf of Carpentaria (west of Karumba, near Burketown)
- **Key Towns**: Hughenden, Richmond, Julia Creek, Cloncurry (via tributary)
- **Major Tributaries**: Cloncurry River, Saxby River, Dutton River
- **Dams**: Corella Dam (near Cloncurry), Julius Dam (Cloncurry water supply)
- **Features**: Longest river in Queensland. Longest river in Australia that doesn't flow through another state. Catchment ~109,000 km². Massive seasonal variation — floods can cover thousands of square kilometres in the lower reaches during the monsoon. The "Sea of Flinders" describes the vast shallow flooding of the Gulf plains. Important cattle country (Flinders grass). Not navigable for most of the year.

## North West / Mount Isa Region

### Leichhardt River
- **Length**: ~480 km
- **Source**: Selwyn Range (south-east of Mount Isa)
- **Mouth**: Gulf of Carpentaria (south-east of Burketown)
- **Key Towns**: Mount Isa
- **Major Tributaries**: Gunpowder Creek, Mistake Creek
- **Dams**: Lake Moondarra (Mount Isa's water supply on Leichhardt River via Rifle Creek Dam), Julius Dam (on tributary)
- **Features**: Catchment ~33,000 km². Critical water source for Mount Isa — Lake Moondarra stores 107,000 ML and is the city's primary water supply. Famous for its recreational fishery (barramundi, sooty grunter). Mining activity in the upper catchment (Mount Isa Mines — copper, zinc, lead, silver). Highly seasonal flow. Lower reaches flood into Gulf plains.

## Channel Country / Lake Eyre Basin

### Georgina River
- **Length**: ~1,130 km (including Eyre Creek continuation)
- **Source**: Barkly Tableland (Northern Territory, north of Camooweal)
- **Mouth**: Terminates in the Simpson Desert (Eyre Creek continues toward Lake Eyre, rarely reaching it)
- **Key Towns**: Camooweal, Boulia, Bedourie
- **Major Tributaries**: Burke River, Hamilton River, Eyre Creek (technically a continuation), Buckley River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Part of the Lake Eyre Basin (endorheic — never reaches the sea). Catchment ~232,000 km² (including Diamantina). During exceptional floods, waters may reach Lake Eyre in South Australia — but this happens only a few times per century. Supports the Channel Country — one of Australia's most productive cattle-fattening regions when flooded. "Channel country" refers to the braided, anastomosing river channels that spread water across vast floodplains.

### Cooper Creek
- **Length**: ~1,300 km (from Barcoo headwaters to Lake Eyre)
- **Source**: Confluence of the Barcoo and Thomson Rivers (south of Windorah)
- **Mouth**: Lake Eyre (only in major floods; otherwise dissipates into the Strzelecki Desert)
- **Key Towns**: Windorah (near confluence), Innamincka (SA), Jundah
- **Major Tributaries**: Barcoo River, Thomson River
- **Dams**: None on main stem; minor weirs
- **Features**: One of the world's most iconic ephemeral river systems. Named by Charles Sturt. Catchment ~244,000 km². The Cooper Creek floodplain is famous for the "Channel Country" — braided river channels that spread up to 100 km wide during floods. Water may take months or even years to travel from headwaters to Lake Eyre. Burke and Wills died at Cooper Creek (Innamincka) in 1861. The Coongie Lakes Ramsar site is a vast wetland complex on Cooper Creek — internationally significant for waterbird breeding.

### Diamantina River
- **Length**: ~900 km
- **Source**: Kirby Range (north-west of Longreach, near Muttaburra)
- **Mouth**: Joins the Georgina/Eyre Creek system in South Australia to form the Warburton River (eventually Lake Eyre in exceptional floods)
- **Key Towns**: Winton (on tributary), Diamantina Lakes, Birdsville
- **Major Tributaries**: Western River, Mayne River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Part of the Diamantina-Georgina-Cooper Creek system flowing toward Lake Eyre. Catchment ~157,000 km². Famous for the Birdsville Track crossing. The Diamantina's braided channels and floodplains support enormous cattle stations (some the size of small countries). Birdsville is at the downstream end — the river rarely flows past here. The Diamantina National Park protects significant sections.

### Barcoo River
- **Length**: ~570 km
- **Source**: Warrego Range (north-east of Tambo)
- **Mouth**: Joins the Thomson River to form Cooper Creek (south of Windorah)
- **Key Towns**: Blackall (near), Tambo, Isisford
- **Major Tributaries**: Alice River (Barcoo)
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Catchment ~60,000 km². The "Barcoo" name is synonymous with the Queensland outback — "Barcoo rot" (desert sores), "Barcoo salute" (swatting flies). Forms part of the Cooper Creek headwaters. The Barcoo River has the most braided channel patterns of any Australian river during flood events. Important for cattle stations.

### Thomson River
- **Length**: ~350 km
- **Source**: Great Dividing Range north of Tambo (near Alpha)
- **Mouth**: Joins the Barcoo River to form Cooper Creek (south of Windorah)
- **Key Towns**: Barcaldine, Longreach, Jundah
- **Major Tributaries**: Landsborough Creek, Darr River
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Catchment ~99,000 km². Forms the other half of the Cooper Creek headwaters. Flows through Longreach — the Longreach Waterhole is a permanent water feature (fed by artesian basin). The Stockman's Hall of Fame overlooks the Thomson River. Ephemeral flow but permanent waterholes (waterholes are critical SHTF water sources in this region).

## South West Queensland

### Paroo River
- **Length**: ~600 km
- **Source**: In the Warrego Range (near Cunnamulla)
- **Mouth**: Joins the Darling River in New South Wales (near Wilcannia, only during floods)
- **Key Towns**: Cunnamulla, Eulo
- **Major Tributaries**: Beechal Creek
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: The last free-flowing (unregulated) river in the northern Murray-Darling Basin — no major dams or weirs. Catchment ~55,000 km². Part of the Murray-Darling Basin (rarely reaches the Darling). The Paroo River wetlands are a declared Ramsar site — critical waterbird habitat. Often reduced to a chain of waterholes during dry periods. Yabby-rich when flowing.

### Bulloo River
- **Length**: ~480 km
- **Source**: Grey Range (north of Quilpie)
- **Mouth**: Bulloo Overflow (terminal — terminates in the Bulloo Lakes system, never reaches the sea)
- **Key Towns**: Quilpie, Thargomindah
- **Major Tributaries**: Boorara Creek
- **Dams**: None
- **Features**: Endorheic basin (internal drainage). Catchment ~62,000 km². The Bulloo Lakes and Bulloo Overflow are critically important wetland habitats — one of the few remaining large terminal lake systems in Australia. Huge waterbird breeding events after floods. Part of the remote Channel Country but not connected to the Cooper/Diamantina system. Thargomindah was the third town in the world (after Paris and London) to have hydroelectric street lighting — powered by artesian bore water pressure (1893).

## Key SHTF Water Notes

1. **Coastal rivers (SEQ to FNQ)**: Generally perennial, but may be turbid after rain. Treat all water. Tidal reaches (especially Brisbane, Mary, Burnett, Fitzroy, lower Burdekin) — check salinity before drinking.
2. **Gulf rivers (Mitchell, Gilbert, Flinders, Leichhardt)**: Seasonal. In the dry season (April–November), many reduce to chains of waterholes. Waterholes may be permanent — check for depth. Crocodiles in all Gulf rivers (estuarine crocs can be hundreds of km upstream).
3. **Channel Country rivers (Cooper, Diamantina, Georgina, Barcoo, Thomson, Bulloo, Paroo)**: Ephemeral. Permanent waterholes exist but are widely spaced. Know where they are before travel. Artesian bores are the primary water source for most Channel Country stations — the Great Artesian Basin underlies most of this region.
4. **Great Artesian Basin**: Underlies ~1.7 million km² of Queensland. Many towns and stations have artesian bores delivering potable water without pumping. Bore water is often slightly mineralised but potable. Know your town's bore locations.
5. **Treatment**: All surface water in Queensland should be treated (boiled, filtered, or chemically treated) due to agricultural runoff, mining contamination, and biological hazards. Assume giardia and cryptosporidium in all surface water.
6. **Crocs**: Estuarine crocodiles range as far south as the Mary River (Maryborough area) and are present in ALL coastal rivers from Gladstone northward. Do not enter water in croc territory without extreme caution.
