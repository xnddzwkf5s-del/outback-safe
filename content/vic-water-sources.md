# Victoria — Nearest Water Sources by Town

> **SHTF Reference**: Find the closest river, lake, reservoir, or other water source for every major town in Victoria. Coordinates are approximate, in decimal degrees (WGS84). Distances are straight-line from town centre.

---

## Melbourne

- **Nearest water**: Yarra River — flows through CBD; Maribyrnong River — western edge; Port Phillip Bay — southern edge
- **Drinking water source**: Upper Yarra, Thomson, and closed Yarra Ranges catchments + Victorian Desalination Plant (Wonthaggi)
- **Coordinates**: -37.8136, 144.9631
- **Notes**: Multiple water sources. The Yarra is tidal up to Dights Falls (Abbotsford). Maribyrnong is tidal to Keilor. Merri Creek, Moonee Ponds Creek, and Darebin Creek run through inner suburbs. Numerous suburban creeks, wetlands, and lakes across the metro area.

---

## Geelong

- **Nearest water**: Corio Bay (Port Phillip Bay) — northern waterfront; Barwon River — southern edge
- **Coordinates**: -38.1493, 144.3598
- **Notes**: The Barwon River flows through the southern suburbs (Belmont, Highton) to Bass Strait at Barwon Heads. Alternative sources: Moorabool River (north-west), Lake Connewarre (estuary south of Geelong). Major reservoirs: West Barwon Dam (recreation reserve near Forrest).

---

## Ballarat

- **Nearest water**: Lake Wendouree — within city (artificial, 2.38 km², originally a swamp); Yarrowee River — runs through southern suburbs
- **Coordinates**: -37.5623, 143.8503
- **Notes**: Lake Wendouree is a major recreational lake but dried up completely during the Millennium Drought (2006-2011) — refilled in 2011. Water supply from Lal Lal Reservoir, Moorabool Reservoirs, and White Swan Reservoir. Ballarat sits atop the Great Dividing Range — many watercourses radiate outward.

---

## Bendigo

- **Nearest water**: Bendigo Creek — flows through city centre (ephemeral); Lake Weeroona — ornamental lake (city); Crusoe Reservoir — southern Bendigo
- **Coordinates**: -36.7587, 144.2833
- **Notes**: Historically gold-mining dependent — Bendigo Creek was dammed and diverted. Water supply: Sandhurst Reservoir, Coliban water system (via Coliban Main Channel from Malmsbury Reservoir and Lauriston Reservoir). Campaspe River is ~35 km east. The Bendigo region is dry — water has always been an issue.

---

## Shepparton

- **Nearest water**: Goulburn River — flows through the southern edge of town; Broken River — confluence with Goulburn just south of Shepparton
- **Coordinates**: -36.3815, 145.3990
- **Notes**: Capital of the Goulburn Valley irrigation region. At the junction of the Goulburn and Broken rivers. Lake Victoria (artificial, central Shepparton) is a recreational lake. Victoria Park Lake. Water supply: Goulburn system. Major flood risk at the river junction.

---

## Mildura

- **Nearest water**: Murray River — immediately adjacent, forming NSW border; Lock 11 (Mildura Weir) pools river for irrigation diversion
- **Coordinates**: -34.2068, 142.1358
- **Notes**: On the Murray — the river IS the town's reason for existence. A major hub of the Sunraysia irrigation district. Water from the Murray via pumped irrigation. Mildura is essentially a desert — without the river, nothing there. The Mildura Weir (Lock 11) creates a permanent pool. Kings Billabong is a wetland nearby.

---

## Wodonga

- **Nearest water**: Murray River — northern edge of town (VIC-NSW border); Hume Dam — 13 km upstream at Lake Hume
- **Coordinates**: -36.1235, 146.8883
- **Notes**: Twin city with Albury (NSW). The Murray forms the entire northern boundary. Lake Hume (3,005 GL) is a major water source and recreation area — 13 km east. Kiewa River joins the Murray ~8 km north-west. House Creek and Sumsion Gardens wetlands run through Wodonga.

---

## Warrnambool

- **Nearest water**: Hopkins River — mouth and estuary at eastern edge; Lady Bay / Southern Ocean — southern boundary (beach); Merri River — western side
- **Coordinates**: -38.3826, 142.4814
- **Notes**: Between two river mouths — Hopkins (east) and Merri (west). Lady Bay is a sheltered beach (protected by Middle Island). The Hopkins River mouth forms a natural harbour — popular fishing and boating. Merri Marine Sanctuary. Water supply from aquifers and the Otway system.

---

## Traralgon

- **Nearest water**: Latrobe River — ~3 km south; Traralgon Creek — runs through the town centre
- **Coordinates**: -38.1950, 146.5370
- **Notes**: Traralgon Creek is a Latrobe tributary. The Latrobe River provides industrial water for Loy Yang power station and is the town's primary water source. Moondarra Reservoir (Blue Rock Dam) is ~20 km north.

---

## Moe

- **Nearest water**: Latrobe River — immediately south-west of town; Lake Narracan — ~5 km south (weir pool on the Latrobe)
- **Coordinates**: -38.1789, 146.2703
- **Notes**: Lake Narracan is a popular recreation lake (waterskiing, fishing, camping). The Latrobe River supplies cooling water for Yallourn power station. Moe is at the northern edge of the Latrobe Valley.

---

## Morwell

- **Nearest water**: Latrobe River — ~2 km south; Morwell River — flows into the Latrobe near Morwell (historically redirected for mining)
- **Coordinates**: -38.2342, 146.3942
- **Notes**: Morwell River was diverted for Yallourn open-cut mine. The Latrobe River supplies industrial water. Close to the Morwell River wetlands and Lake Narracan.

---

## Sale

- **Nearest water**: Thomson River — western edge of town (at Longford); Latrobe River — south of town — confluence of Thomson and Latrobe just south-west; Lake Guthridge (ornamental, in town); Lake Guyatt (ornamental)
- **Coordinates**: -38.1057, 147.0703
- **Notes**: At the junction of the Thomson and Latrobe rivers. The Sale Canal connects the town to the Latrobe. Lake Wellington (Gippsland Lakes) is ~10 km south. Extensive wetlands and floodplains. Water supply: Moondarra system.

---

## Bairnsdale

- **Nearest water**: Mitchell River — flows through the town centre (eastern edge); Lake King / Gippsland Lakes — ~10 km south
- **Coordinates**: -37.8256, 147.6324
- **Notes**: The Mitchell River is central to Bairnsdale — the iconic Mitchell River Silt Jetties extend 8 km into Lake King. The river flats around Bairnsdale are prime agricultural land. Nicholson River (west) and Tambo River (east) are within 15-20 km.

---

## Horsham

- **Nearest water**: Wimmera River — flows through the town centre (north-west to south-east); Horsham Weir creates a pool within the town
- **Coordinates**: -36.7160, 142.1987
- **Notes**: The Wimmera River is the lifeblood of Horsham — running directly through the town. The weir maintains a permanent water feature and swimming area. Green Lake is ~10 km south-east (recreational). Water supply from the Wimmera-Mallee pipeline network (Rocklands Reservoir via Taylors Lake and Lake Lonsdale).

---

## Stawell

- **Nearest water**: Pleasant Creek — runs through town (ephemeral); Lake Fyans — ~15 km south-east; Lake Lonsdale — ~10 km west
- **Coordinates**: -37.0567, 142.7794
- **Notes**: Pleasant Creek was the centre of the Stawell gold rush (1850s). Lake Lonsdale is a major Wimmera-Mallee system supply reservoir. Lake Fyans is a popular recreation lake (fishing, boating). The Grampians ranges are the main catchment to the south.

---

## Ararat

- **Nearest water**: Hopkins River headwaters — Mount Cole Creek and Fiery Creek start in the ranges west of Ararat; Green Hill Lake — ~5 km east; Lake Bolac — ~40 km south
- **Coordinates**: -37.2832, 142.9297
- **Notes**: Ararat sits on the Great Dividing Range — water flows north (Wimmera catchment) and south (Hopkins catchment). Green Hill Lake is a recreational lake. Chalambar Reservoir is ~15 km east. The Hopkins River's headwaters are the nearest perennial streams.

---

## Hamilton

- **Nearest water**: Grange Burn — creek running through the town; Lake Hamilton — ornamental lake in town (formed from Grange Burn); Wannon River — ~15 km west
- **Coordinates**: -37.7432, 142.0241
- **Notes**: Grange Burn was dammed to form Lake Hamilton. The Wannon River is the nearest major watercourse. Rocklands Reservoir (Glenelg system) is ~40 km north-west. Surrounded by the volcanic plains of the Western District — many small lakes and wetlands (part of the Kanawinka Geopark).

---

## Portland

- **Nearest water**: Portland Bay / Southern Ocean — the town IS on the coast; Fitzroy River — flows into Portland Bay at the northern edge of town
- **Coordinates**: -38.3458, 141.6034
- **Notes**: Deep-water port. The Fitzroy River estuary is at the town's northern edge. Portland is right on the ocean. The town dam (reservoir) is on the north-west edge. Heywood's Fitzroy River system provides water supply.

---

## Colac

- **Nearest water**: Lake Colac — immediately adjacent to the town (northern edge — 18.2 km², the largest natural freshwater lake in Victoria's Western District); Barwon River headwaters — flow from the ranges south of Colac
- **Coordinates**: -38.3410, 143.5863
- **Notes**: Lake Colac is a large, shallow freshwater lake used for fishing, sailing, and birdwatching. It can shrink dramatically in drought. The Barwon River's east and west branches rise in the Otway Ranges south of Colac. Many crater lakes nearby (Lake Corangamite — Australia's largest permanent inland lake, saline, ~25 km north).

---

## Wangaratta

- **Nearest water**: Ovens River — flows through the town centre; King River — joins the Ovens at the western edge of Wangaratta
- **Coordinates**: -36.3560, 146.3233
- **Notes**: At the junction of the Ovens and King rivers — one of north-east Victoria's major river confluences. The Ovens River runs right through the city centre. The Murray to Mountains Rail Trail follows the river. Water quality is generally excellent from alpine catchments.

---

## Seymour

- **Nearest water**: Goulburn River — flows through the southern edge of town; Whiteheads Creek — runs through Seymour and joins the Goulburn
- **Coordinates**: -37.0207, 145.1326
- **Notes**: On the Goulburn River. The Goulburn is dammed upstream at Lake Eildon and Goulburn Weir. Seymour has a history of flooding (the river breaks its banks during major events). The Puckapunyal military base is nearby (~10 km west) — relies on the same water system.

## Echuca

- **Nearest water**: Murray River — the town is built on the south bank of the Murray; Campaspe River — joins the Murray at Echuca's western edge
- **Coordinates**: -36.1289, 144.7526
- **Notes**: Historic paddle steamer port on the Murray. At the junction of the Murray and Campaspe rivers. The Port of Echuca is a major tourist attraction. Water supply from the Murray. Torrumbarry Weir is ~30 km downstream. Twin town with Moama (NSW).

## Swan Hill

- **Nearest water**: Murray River — immediately adjacent (south bank of the Murray); Little Murray River — anabranch to the north
- **Coordinates**: -35.3378, 143.5604
- **Notes**: On the Murray River. The Little Murray River is an anabranch (a river channel that branches off and then rejoins the main channel). Historic riverboat port. Swan Hill's Pioneer Settlement is a major tourist attraction. Major agricultural hub (stone fruit, grapes, almonds). The Murray is the unambiguous water source.

---

## Kerang

- **Nearest water**: Loddon River — runs through town (north-west to south-east); Kerang Lakes — extensive wetland system surrounding the town (Lake Tutchewop, Lake William, Kangaroo Lake, Reedy Lake, Middle Lake, Third Lake — the Ramsar-listed Kerang wetlands)
- **Coordinates**: -35.7334, 143.9192
- **Notes**: Surrounded by lakes — Kerang is in the heart of the Kerang Lakes district, an internationally significant wetland system (RAMSAR). The Loddon River flows through town on its way to the Murray. The Avoca River terminates in these wetlands. During floods, the lakes interconnect. Major birdwatching and fishing area.

## Castlemaine

- **Coordinates**: -37.0640, 144.2171
- **Notes**: Goldfields town. Barker's Creek and Campbells Creek were heavily mined (alluvial gold). The Loddon River system supplies water via Malmsbury and Cairn Curran reservoirs. Castlemaine Botanical Gardens lake is an ornamental water feature.

## Maryborough

- **Coordinates**: -37.0482, 143.7367
- **Notes**: Gold rush town. Bet Bet Creek is a Loddon tributary — often dry but floods in wet years. Tullaroop Reservoir (72 GL) is the major water supply. Carisbrook (10 km east) is on the Loddon River itself.

## Daylesford

- **Nearest water**: Lake Daylesford — in town (ornamental/recreation — originally a dam built in 1928); Wombat Creek / Jim Crow Creek — flow through the area; Loddon River headwaters — rise near Glenlyon (~5 km north)
- **Coordinates**: -37.3412, 144.1453
- **Notes**: Spa town famous for mineral springs (natural mineral water — Hepburn Springs, 3 km north). Lake Daylesford is a recreational lake (boating, walking trails). The town sits atop the Great Dividing Range — water flows north to the Loddon/Murray, south to the Werribee/Port Phillip. Many natural springs in Hamstead/Hepburn area.

## Mansfield

## Benalla

Yarrawonga Weir is the offtake for the Mulwala Canal (largest irrigation canal in southern hemisphere) and Yarrawonga Main Channel. The Murray forms the entire northern boundary. ## Mallacoota

## Notes for SHTF Water Sourcing

1. **Coastal towns**: Most coastal towns have a river, creek, or estuary providing freshwater nearby. In an emergency, seawater desalination requires equipment (boiling/distillation, reverse osmosis if available).

2. **Inland towns on rivers**: The towns below sit directly on rivers — these are your best bets for reliable water access in a SHTF scenario:
   - Shepparton (Goulburn), Mildura (Murray), Wodonga (Murray), Wangaratta (Ovens), Seymour (Goulburn), Echuca (Murray), Swan Hill (Murray), Yarrawonga (Murray/Lake Mulwala), Bairnsdale (Mitchell), Horsham (Wimmera), Sale (Thomson), Benalla (Broken), Corryong (Nariel/Murray), Omeo (Cobungra).

3. **Towns relying on piped water only** (no obvious immediate water source): Stawell (Pleasant Creek is ephemeral), Ararat, Maryborough (Bet Bet Creek is ephemeral), Castlemaine, Mansfield. These towns need functional infrastructure or stored water. Make local knowledge a priority for these locations.

4. **Melbourne's water**: In a long-term SHTF, the Yarra and Maribyrnong are the most accessible from the metro area. The closed catchments in the Yarra Ranges are protected by law — but the rivers themselves flow through the city.

5. **Gippsland Lakes**: Europe's largest inland waterway is in Victoria — Lake Wellington, Lake Victoria, Lake King, and Lake Reeve. Brackish to saline. In an emergency, treat water extensively before drinking.

6. **The Murray River**: The single most important water source in Victoria (and arguably Australia). Most inland border towns have direct access. Highly saline at times — treat or find alternative if possible.

7. **Lake Eildon**: Contains 3,334 GL when full — one of the largest surface water stores in Victoria. Accessible from Eildon, Mansfield, Alexandra. Multiple boat ramps around the lake.

8. **Desalination plant**: The Wonthaggi desal plant (Victorian Desalination Plant) at Dalyston/Wonthaggi has capacity for 150 GL/year. A SHTF event that disables power would render this useless without backup power.

9. **Dams without power**: Hume Dam, Eildon, Thomson, and Dartmouth all require power for operation (release valves, pumping stations, monitoring). In a full grid-down, these are not reliable water sources at the dam wall — but the rivers downstream still flow.

10. **Best survival rivers** (long-term reliable without infrastructure): Ovens River (clean, alpine-fed, minimally regulated), Mitchell River (largest unregulated river), Snowy River (environmental flows restored), upper Murray (clean near source), Glenelg River (relatively healthy). These are the rivers that will be flowing reliably even in a complete infrastructure collapse.
