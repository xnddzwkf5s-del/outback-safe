# Tasmania — Major River Systems

## Overview

Tasmania's rivers are short by mainland standards but carry high volumes of water due to heavy rainfall and steep topography. The state has over 40 major river systems, many harnessed by the extensive Tasmanian Hydro-Electric Scheme — one of the world's largest integrated hydro-power networks with over 30 power stations and 50+ major dams. The scheme generates approximately 90% of Tasmania's electricity.

## Southern Rivers

### Derwent River
- **Length**: 239 km (third-longest in Tasmania)
- **Source**: Lake St Clair (Australia's deepest lake, 200 m)
- **Mouth**: Storm Bay, Hobart (estuary)
- **Course**: Lake St Clair → Derwent Bridge → Lake King William → Wayatinah → New Norfolk → Hobart
- **Key Towns**: Derwent Bridge, Hamilton (via tributary), New Norfolk, Bridgewater, Hobart
- **Hydro Dams**: Clark Dam (Lake King William), Wayatinah, Catagunya, Repulse, Cluny, Meadowbank — plus the entire Upper Derwent scheme
- **Features**: Tidal estuary to New Norfolk; Hobart's primary drinking water supply; major recreational fishery (trout, black bream at estuary); MONA on banks; the Derwent drains approximately 20% of Tasmania

### Huon River
- **Length**: 170 km
- **Source**: Junction of Maatsuyker River and other headwater streams, Southwest National Park
- **Mouth**: D'Entrecasteaux Channel at Huonville estuary
- **Key Towns**: Huonville, Franklin, Geeveston (nearby)
- **Hydro Dams**: Scotts Peak Dam (Lake Pedder, tributary via Huon–Serpentine scheme), Edgar Dam, Strathgordon
- **Features**: Deep tidal estuary; famous Huon Valley apple-growing region; excellent salmon farming in lower reaches; Huon pine timber along upper tributaries

### Gordon River
- **Length**: 210 km
- **Source**: King William Saddle (Central Highlands)
- **Mouth**: Macquarie Harbour (near Strahan)
- **Key Towns**: None directly on river; nearest: Strathgordon, Strahan
- **Hydro Dams**: Gordon Dam (140 m — tallest dam in Tasmania, Lake Gordon), Serpentine Dam (Lake Pedder) — Gordon Power Station is the largest in Tasmania (432 MW)
- **Features**: World Heritage wilderness; Gordon Dam is an engineering landmark; famous for wild brown trout fishing in tailwater below Gordon Dam; cruise boats operate from Strahan into lower Gordon; Lake Gordon is Tasmania's largest water storage (12,450 GL)

### Franklin River
- **Length**: 129 km
- **Source**: Cheyne Range (Central Highlands)
- **Mouth**: Joins Gordon River near Heritage Landing
- **Key Towns**: None — entirely within World Heritage wilderness
- **Features**: World-famous whitewater rafting destination; saved from damming in 1983 after a landmark High Court ruling (Franklin Dam controversy); deep gorges, pristine temperate rainforest; the Franklin–Gordon Wild Rivers National Park is a UNESCO site; flows through the Great Ravine — one of Tasmania's deepest gorges

### Picton River
- **Length**: ~50 km
- **Source**: Picton Range (near Hartz Mountains)
- **Mouth**: Joins Huon River south of Huonville
- **Key Towns**: None directly; nearest: Geeveston
- **Features**: Major tributary of the Huon; forms part of the Huon River catchment; surrounded by wet eucalypt and rainforest; good trout fishing in upper reaches

### Styx River
- **Length**: ~60 km
- **Source**: Newdegate Pass region
- **Mouth**: Joins Derwent River near Bushy Park
- **Key Towns**: None directly; nearest: Maydena, Bushy Park
- **Features**: Famous for Styx Valley — home to some of the world's tallest hardwood trees (Eucalyptus regnans, over 90 m); major tributary of the Derwent; the "Big Tree Reserve" has trees up to 400 years old; environmental flashpoint for logging protests

### Tyenna River
- **Length**: ~35 km
- **Source**: Mount Field National Park
- **Mouth**: Joins Derwent River near Bushy Park
- **Key Towns**: Westerway, National Park
- **Features**: Flows through Mount Field National Park; popular for Russell Falls and Horseshoe Falls; excellent trout fishing; major Derwent tributary; feeds the Meadowbank hydro canal system

### Ouse River
- **Length**: ~100 km
- **Source**: Central Plateau
- **Mouth**: Joins Derwent River near Ouse
- **Key Towns**: Ouse (derives name from river)
- **Features**: Major Central Highlands tributary; feeds into Lake Echo hydro scheme; good trout water

### Shannon River
- **Length**: ~65 km
- **Source**: Shannon Lagoon (Great Lake area)
- **Mouth**: Joins Ouse River near Miena
- **Key Towns**: None directly; nearest: Miena
- **Features**: Flows from the Great Lake region; passes through Lagoon of Islands; part of the Derwent catchment; significant for hydro regulation

### Clyde River
- **Length**: 97 km
- **Source**: Southern Midlands (near Mount Hobbs)
- **Mouth**: Joins Derwent estuary at Bridgewater
- **Key Towns**: Bothwell, Hamilton
- **Hydro Dams**: Lake Sorell, Lake Crescent (inter-connected, part of Clyde catchment)
- **Features**: Drains the scenic Clyde Valley; important for irrigation in the Midlands; Lake Sorell and Lake Crescent are premier trout fisheries; the historic town of Bothwell sits on its banks

### Jordan River
- **Length**: ~55 km
- **Source**: Western Tiers / Lakes country (near Arthurs Lake)
- **Mouth**: Joins Derwent estuary near Bridgewater
- **Key Towns**: Jericho, Brighton
- **Features**: Major irrigation source for Brighton–Jericho agricultural area; flows through Coal River Valley wine region; linked to hydro-electric system via Arthurs Lake

### Coal River
- **Length**: ~65 km
- **Source**: Tunnack area (Southern Midlands)
- **Mouth**: Pitt Water (near Richmond, estuary at Frederick Henry Bay)
- **Key Towns**: Richmond, Campania, Tea Tree
- **Features**: Flows through Richmond — historic bridge (oldest in Australia, built 1823–1825), convict-built; Coal River Valley is a major cool-climate wine region; the river is actually named for coal discovered at its mouth (now known to be oil shale), though little coal exists; tidal at lower reaches

### Davey River
- **Length**: ~40 km
- **Source**: Junction of De Witt River and Hardwood River, Southwest National Park
- **Mouth**: Port Davey (remote wilderness inlet)
- **Key Towns**: None — extremely remote, access only via walking track, boat, or air
- **Features**: Flows into Port Davey, one of the most pristine and remote waterways in Australia; World Heritage wilderness; dark, tannin-stained water characteristic of southwest rivers; Melaleuca airstrip nearby; excellent sea-run trout fishing in Port Davey

### Esperance River
- **Length**: ~20 km
- **Source**: Hartz Mountains National Park
- **Mouth**: Esperance Bay (near Dover)
- **Key Towns**: Dover
- **Features**: Short coastal river in far south; flows through dense rainforest; supports small timber-dependent settlements; tannin-darkened waters typical of south Tasmanian rivers

## West Coast Rivers

### Pieman River
- **Length**: 71 km
- **Source**: Junction of Mackintosh River and Murchison River near Tullah
- **Mouth**: Hardwicke Bay (Indian Ocean, near Corinna)
- **Key Towns**: Corinna (historic gold-mining settlement, now eco-tourism), Rosebery, Tullah (nearby)
- **Hydro Dams**: Mackintosh Dam (Lake Mackintosh), Murchison Dam (Lake Murchison), Bastyan Dam (Lake Rosebery), Reece Dam (Lake Pieman)
- **Features**: Deeply tannin-stained water (translucent brown — the "Pieman" name likely from a convict with a pie-shaped face, or from a corruption of an Aboriginal name); Arcadia II heritage cruise boat operates on the river; Corinna is a remote wilderness retreat with a vehicle barge crossing; Lake Pieman is a major hydro storage

### Arthur River
- **Length**: 172 km
- **Source**: North-west of Waratah (near the Magnet Range)
- **Mouth**: Arthur River township (Indian Ocean, west coast)
- **Key Towns**: Arthur River (small coastal settlement at mouth)
- **Features**: Fully wild — one of the few remaining totally undammed major rivers in Australia; flows entirely through the Tarkine wilderness (takayna) — huge temperate rainforest, one of the largest in the Southern Hemisphere; world-class whitewater kayaking and rafting; Arthur River township is the departure point for "Tarkine" wilderness cruises; the river is known for its tannin-stained, dark tea-coloured water; rich Aboriginal heritage, middens and hut depressions line the coast near the mouth

### King River
- **Length**: 72 km
- **Source**: Lake Burbury (man-made hydro storage)
- **Mouth**: Macquarie Harbour (near Strahan)
- **Key Towns**: Queenstown, Strahan (at mouth)
- **Hydro Dams**: Crotty Dam (Lake Burbury) — King River Power Station (144 MW)
- **Features**: Infamous for severe pollution from century of Mount Lyell copper mining at Queenstown — the river ran bright orange-red from acid mine drainage and sediment for decades; massive remediation since 1994 closure of the mine; now recovering, sports good trout fishing in lower reaches; flows through deep King River Gorge; the West Coast Wilderness Railway follows much of the river's course from Queenstown to Strahan; Lake Burbury is a large, scenic hydro impoundment

### Henty River
- **Length**: ~90 km
- **Source**: West Coast Range (near Mount Read)
- **Mouth**: Indian Ocean near Strahan (north of Macquarie Harbour)
- **Key Towns**: None directly; nearest: Zeehan, Strahan
- **Hydro Dams**: Yolande Dam (Lake Margaret), part of the historic Lake Margaret Power Station
- **Features**: Flows through west coast mining country; Lake Margaret is a historic 1914 hydro scheme — one of the oldest operating hydro stations in Australia; Henty Sand Dunes at the coast near the mouth (towering dunes popular with 4WD and sandboarding); the river passes through button-grass plains and rainforest

### Montagu River
- **Length**: ~45 km
- **Source**: Montagu Swamp (northwest Tasmania)
- **Mouth**: Robbins Passage (Bass Strait, far northwest)
- **Key Towns**: Montagu (near Smithton)
- **Features**: One of Tasmania's most northerly rivers; drains rich agricultural dairy country around Smithton (Circular Head region); extensive floodplain and wetlands; significant for irrigation of the northwest dairy and vegetable industries; tidal in lower reaches

### Duck River
- **Length**: ~70 km
- **Source**: West of Smithton in the Tarkine region
- **Mouth**: Duck Bay (near Smithton, emptying into Robbins Passage)
- **Key Towns**: Smithton
- **Features**: Flows through Circular Head dairy country; important water source for Smithton and surrounds; linked to Montagu River wetlands; tidal estuary near mouth

### Flowerdale River
- **Length**: ~40 km
- **Source**: Near West Takone (Dial Range)
- **Mouth**: Joins Inglis River near Wynyard
- **Key Towns**: Flowerdale (small settlement), Wynyard
- **Features**: Major tributary of the Inglis; lush farmland; feeds into the fertile Wynyard coastal plain

### Inglis River
- **Length**: ~45 km
- **Source**: Near West Takone (Dial Range)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait at Wynyard
- **Key Towns**: Wynyard
- **Features**: Flows through Wynyard's town centre (riverfront parkland, "Fossil Bluff" at mouth — 23 million-year-old coastal fossil site); excellent kayaking; forms a scenic estuary; important water supply for Wynyard area

## Northern Rivers

### Tamar River (kanamaluka)
- **Length**: 70 km (estuary section — combined North Esk + South Esk = 420+ km total system)
- **Source**: Confluence of North Esk and South Esk rivers at Launceston
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait at Low Head (George Town)
- **Key Towns**: Launceston, Legana, Exeter, Beaconsfield, Beauty Point, George Town, Low Head
- **Hydro Dams**: Trevallyn Dam (Launceston gorge, 26 m — Trevallyn Power Station, 80 MW)
- **Features**: Actually a tidal estuary, not a true river; Australia's longest navigable estuary (70 km); the Tamar Valley is one of Australia's premier cool-climate wine regions; historic port — Launceston was Tasmania's first major port; the Batman Bridge (1968) — one of the world's earliest cable-stayed bridges; extensive convict-built infrastructure; major industrial zone (Bell Bay aluminium smelter, woodchip export); excellent fishing — bream, flathead, Australian salmon, mullet; tidal range up to 3 metres affecting navigation and water quality

### North Esk River
- **Length**: 86 km
- **Source**: Ben Nevis (North Eastern Highlands, near Ringarooma)
- **Mouth**: Joins South Esk to form Tamar at Launceston
- **Key Towns**: Fingal (via tributary), St Marys (via tributary), Launceston
- **Features**: Forms the northern arm of the Tamar system; flows through Cataract Gorge in Launceston — one of Australia's most spectacular urban natural features (Duck Reach Power Station — first publicly-owned hydro station in the Southern Hemisphere, 1895); heavily regulated for irrigation; popular for whitewater kayaking in gorge section

### South Esk River
- **Length**: 252 km (longest river in Tasmania)
- **Source**: Near Mathinna Plains (North Eastern Highlands, Ben Lomond area)
- **Mouth**: Joins North Esk to form Tamar at Launceston
- **Key Towns**: Mathinna (near), Fingal, Avoca, Campbell Town, Evandale, Longford, Perth
- **Hydro Dams**: None, but part of the Meander–South Esk irrigation scheme
- **Features**: Tasmania's longest river; flows through the fertile Fingal Valley (historic coal mining) and Midlands grain/sheep country; the river runs through some of Tasmania's richest agricultural land; significant for irrigation; the historic township of Evandale is on its banks; many convict-built bridges cross the river

### Macquarie River
- **Length**: 189 km
- **Source**: Tooms Lake (near Lake Leake, Central Highlands)
- **Mouth**: Joins South Esk River near Longford
- **Key Towns**: Ross, Campbell Town, Longford
- **Features**: Major tributary of the South Esk; flows through the heart of the Midlands — Australia's oldest continuously farmed region outside Sydney; the Macquarie is central to the Midlands irrigation scheme; Ross Bridge (1836) — finest convict-built stone bridge in Australia with 186 carved voussoirs; Killymoon and Brickendon estates (UNESCO World Heritage) are on its banks; popular trout fishery, especially around Campbell Town and Ross

### Meander River
- **Length**: ~110 km
- **Source**: Great Western Tiers (near Mother Cummings Peak)
- **Mouth**: Joins South Esk River near Hadspen
- **Key Towns**: Meander, Deloraine, Westbury, Hadspen
- **Hydro Dams**: Huntsman Lake (Meander Dam, completed 2007) — one of Tasmania's newest large dams
- **Features**: Prime trout fishing water — renowned for quality brown trout; flows through the scenic Meander Valley with the Great Western Tiers as backdrop; Deloraine sits on its banks with beautiful riverfront parkland; extensive irrigation district serves potato, poppy, and dairy farming; the Meander Dam created a significant new fishery at Huntsman Lake

### Rubicon River
- **Length**: ~24 km
- **Source**: Junction of Franklin Rivulet and tributaries near Port Sorell
- **Mouth**: Port Sorell (Bass Strait)
- **Key Towns**: Port Sorell
- **Features**: Short but significant coastal river; flows into the Port Sorell estuary, an important Ramsar wetland and shorebird habitat; Rubicon Estuary is a popular recreational area; oyster farming in the estuary

### Mersey River
- **Length**: 147 km
- **Source**: Lake Meston (Walls of Jerusalem region, Central Plateau)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait at Devonport
- **Key Towns**: Mole Creek (via tributary), Railton, Latrobe, Devonport
- **Hydro Dams**: Parangana Dam (Lake Parangana), Rowallan Dam (Lake Rowallan), Mersey Forth Power Station
- **Features**: Major north-coast river draining through Devonport; the Mersey–Forth hydro scheme diverts water from both catchments; flows through scenic Mersey Valley; popular whitewater rafting in upper reaches (Arm River confluence area); Devonport at the mouth is Tasmania's third-largest city and major ferry port (Spirit of Tasmania)

### Forth River
- **Length**: 97 km
- **Source**: Pelion Plains (Overland Track area, Cradle Mountain–Lake St Clair National Park)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait near Turners Beach (west of Devonport)
- **Key Towns**: Forth (small coastal town at mouth)
- **Hydro Dams**: Cethana Dam (Lake Cethana), Devil's Gate Dam (Lake Barrington), Paloona Dam (Lake Paloona), Wilmot Dam, Lemonthyme Power Station
- **Features**: Highly developed hydro catchment; Lake Barrington is world-famous for rowing (hosted the 1990 World Rowing Championships); Lemonthyme Lodge area — old-growth myrtle-beech rainforest; the Forth Power Station is a major generation asset

### Leven River
- **Length**: ~50 km
- **Source**: Black Bluff Range (near Loongana)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait at Ulverstone
- **Key Towns**: Gunns Plains, Ulverstone
- **Features**: Flows through Gunns Plains Caves — an extensive limestone karst system; major water supply for Ulverstone; excellent trout fishing particularly in the Gunns Plains section; the canyon section below Gunns Plains is popular for kayaking

## Other Significant Rivers

### Ringarooma River
- **Length**: ~85 km
- **Source**: Mount Albert / Ben Nevis area (northeast highlands)
- **Mouth**: Bass Strait near Tomahawk (northeast coast)
- **Key Towns**: Derby (via Ringarooma River tributaries), Bridport (nearby)
- **Features**: Drains the rich agricultural region around Ringarooma and Branxholm; important for irrigation; flows through historic tin-mining country (Derby region); world-class mountain bike trails in the catchment (Blue Derby network)

### Ansons River
- **Length**: ~20 km
- **Source**: Ansons Bay region (northeast coast)
- **Mouth**: Ansons Bay (Bass Strait)
- **Key Towns**: St Helens (nearby)
- **Features**: Coastal river in the northeast; part of the Bay of Fires region; Ansons Bay is a scenic coastal lagoon

### George River
- **Length**: ~25 km
- **Source**: Near Goshen (northeast)
- **Mouth**: Georges Bay (St Helens)
- **Key Towns**: St Helens
- **Features**: Flows into Georges Bay at St Helens — Tasmania's largest fishing port and lobster capital; important for the St Helens town water supply; tidal estuary section

### Scamander River
- **Length**: ~15 km
- **Source**: Junction of Trout Creek and other streams near Upper Scamander
- **Mouth**: Indian Ocean at Scamander (east coast)
- **Key Towns**: Scamander
- **Features**: Short east-coast river; popular surf beach at the mouth; good bream and flathead fishing in the estuary

### Apsley River
- **Length**: ~55 km
- **Source**: Ben Lomond National Park area
- **Mouth**: Joins the Swan River system (east coast, near Cranbrook)
- **Key Towns**: Cranbrook, Swansea (nearby)
- **Features**: Flows through Douglas-Apsley National Park with its iconic waterholes and gorges; beautiful dry eucalypt waterholes — a swimming paradise in summer; feeds into the Moulting Lagoon/Swan River system

### Swan River
- **Length**: ~70 km
- **Source**: Near Mount Elephant (east coast)
- **Mouth**: Moulting Lagoon then Great Oyster Bay (near Swansea)
- **Key Towns**: Swansea
- **Features**: Feeds into Moulting Lagoon — a Ramsar wetland of international importance for migratory shorebirds; Swansea sits on Great Oyster Bay and draws water from the Swan River catchment; Moulting Lagoon is also fed by the Apsley River; important black swan habitat (the river's namesake); excellent wading bird observation

### Prosser River
- **Length**: ~30 km
- **Source**: Near Buckland (east of Hobart)
- **Mouth**: Prosser Bay at Orford (east coast)
- **Key Towns**: Orford
- **Features**: Flows through Buckland and Orford; Orford is a popular coastal town at the mouth; Prosser Bay is a sheltered anchorage; the river provides water supply for Orford/Triabunna area

Tasmania's lake system is dominated by the Hydro-Electric scheme — over 50 large impoundments across the state.

| **Lake St Clair** | ~390 GL (natural) | None (natural, slight regulation) | Australia's deepest freshwater lake (200 m max depth); carved by glaciers during the last ice age; at the southern end of the Overland Track; source of the Derwent River; within World Heritage area |

## Untamed (Free-Flowing) Major Rivers

Most Tasmanian rivers have been harnessed, but a few significant ones remain free-flowing:

1. **Arthur River** — The last major undammed river on Tasmania's west coast; flows entirely through the Tarkine wilderness
2. **Franklin River** — Saved from damming in 1983; World Heritage-listed; remains completely free of any impoundments
3. **Pieman River** — While heavily dammed downstream (four dams), the upper Mackintosh and Murchison branches are partially regulated; the coast section near its mouth is free-flowing
4. **South Esk River** — The longest river in Tasmania; no large dams on the main stem (but is regulated by the Meander Dam indirectly)
5. **Macquarie River** — No large hydro dams, though heavily used for irrigation
6. **Davey River** — Pristine, remote, completely free-flowing in World Heritage wilderness

## Key Notes for SHTF

- **Hydro scheme = massive stored water**: Tasmania's 50+ reservoirs hold an enormous emergency water supply, but access to dam infrastructure is controlled by Hydro Tasmania.
- **Tannin-stained water**: West and southwest rivers (Arthur, Pieman, Gordon, Davey) have high tannin content — safe to drink when properly treated but may stain clothes and has a distinct taste. Boiling is the minimum treatment.
- **Agricultural runoff**: Rivers in the Midlands and northwest (Macquarie, South Esk, Mersey, Meander) pass through intensive agricultural areas — filtration and treatment essential.
- **Acid mine drainage**: The King River and Queen River are affected by mining runoff — avoid drinking from these without advanced treatment. The Queen River through Queenstown remains highly acidic.
- **Tidal estuaries**: The Derwent (to New Norfolk), Tamar (to Launceston, 70 km), Huon (to Huonville), and many smaller rivers are tidal far inland — plan water collection above the tidal limit or treat for salt.
- **Coastal lagoons**: Many east coast rivers (Swan, Apsley) feed into coastal lagoons and Ramsar wetlands — rich in birdlife but water may be brackish.
