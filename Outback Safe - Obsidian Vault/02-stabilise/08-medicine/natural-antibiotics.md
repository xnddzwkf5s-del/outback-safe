# Natural Antibiotics

**Antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria. Modern antibiotics derive from natural sources, and natural remedies have been used for centuries — they are a practical fallback for minor infections and wound care when the pharmacy is not available.**

Source: Healthline — [5 Natural Antibiotics to Try at Home](https://www.healthline.com/health/natural-antibiotics). A sampling study of 58 Chinese plants found 23 with antibacterial and 15 with antifungal properties; a 2014 study found an herbal therapy as effective as a chemical antibiotic for small-intestine bacterial overgrowth.

> **Critical rule:** Natural remedies are NOT a substitute for prescribed antibiotics in serious infection. If antibiotics are prescribed, finish the entire course. Taking antibiotics unnecessarily builds resistance.

---

## 1. Honey — the Oldest Known Antibiotic

- **History:** used since ancient times; Egyptians used it as antibiotic and skin protectant
- **Why it works:** contains hydrogen peroxide; high sugar content stops bacterial growth; low pH pulls moisture from bacteria, dehydrating and killing them
- **Use:** apply directly to wound or infected area; ingest a tablespoon for internal infections or stir into warm herbal tea
- **Best form:** raw Manuka honey
- **Safety:** safe on skin or internally, but **never give honey to an infant under 1 year old**

## 2. Garlic

- **Why it works:** organosulfur compounds effective against a wide variety of bacteria (2021 review)
- **Use:** garlic concentrate/extract, or homemade — soak garlic cloves in olive oil; apply concentrate directly to a wound or blemish; safe to ingest
- **Dosage:** up to 2 cloves per day considered acceptable; follow supplement label directions
- **Safety:** large doses may cause internal bleeding; if taking blood-thinning medication, consult a health professional before use

## 3. Myrrh

- **Why it works:** extract kills everyday pathogens including *E. coli*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and *Candida albicans* (2000 study); a 2020 study found myrrh oil preferentially kills nongrowing (dormant, more antibiotic-resistant) bacteria without organisms building resistance
- **Use:** typically prepackaged — follow label dosage
- **Safety:** ingestion may cause diarrhoea; skin application may cause minor rash; large doses may cause heart problems

## 4. Thyme Essential Oil

- **Why it works:** especially effective against antibiotic-resistant bacteria; in a 2011 study of 120+ bacterial strains it killed bacteria more effectively than lavender oil
- **Use:** **external use only** — never take by mouth; dilute with equal parts carrier oil (coconut or olive) before applying
- **Safety:** undiluted essential oil on skin causes inflammation and irritation

## 5. Oregano Essential Oil (Carvacrol)

- **Why it works:** carvacrol helps heal gastric ulcers and reduce inflammation
- **Fungal skin infections:** 1 drop oregano oil per teaspoon of carrier oil (olive/coconut), applied to the affected area
- **Sinus infections:** diffuse the oil in the air
- **Home cleaner:** oregano oil + vinegar + water + lemon eradicates household bacteria
- **Safety:** do NOT ingest oregano essential oil; do not use undiluted on skin

## Also Noted

**Cranberry extract** — antibacterial and antioxidant compounds; a home remedy for urinary tract infections (UTIs).

---

## Key Takeaways for Remote Situations

- **Honey and garlic** are the two most universally available with the strongest evidence — honey for wounds (raw Manuka best), garlic ingested or applied to skin
- **Essential oils (thyme, oregano) are external use only** — always dilute
- Natural options are for minor infections and wound care when antibiotics are unavailable — watch for signs of serious infection (spreading redness, fever, pus, systemic illness) which require evacuation and professional care
- Never give honey to infants under 1 year
